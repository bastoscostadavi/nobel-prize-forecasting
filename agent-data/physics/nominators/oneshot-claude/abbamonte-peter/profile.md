## Biography

Abbamonte received his PhD in physics from UIUC in 1999. His doctoral research was done with Eric Isaacs and Phil Platzman in the Materials Physics Department at Bell Laboratories. On an NSF International Research Fellowship he then worked with George Sawatzky at the University of Groningen. He returned to the US for a biophysics postdoc in Sol Gruner's group at Cornell, studying photosynthesis in *Rhodobacter sphaeroides*. He joined the scientific staff at Brookhaven National Laboratory in 2003. UIUC recruited him in 2005, and he became full professor in 2013 and Fox Family Professor in Engineering in 2019. In 2012 he founded Inprentus, a maker of XUV and soft-X-ray diffraction gratings, in the UI Research Park (UIUC profile). He is also listed as a Chicago Quantum Exchange member.

## Research footprint

The UIUC profile describes him as "one of the originators of the technique of resonant soft x-ray scattering." With it he discovered a Wigner crystal of holes in doped quasi-1D spin ladders and showed that stripes in copper-oxide superconductors carry charge. According to the profile, the technique is "now in use at every major synchrotron facility in the world." He also found a solution to the phase problem for inelastic X-ray scattering. This allows real-time imaging of electron dynamics with attosecond resolution, and he used it to image exciton formation in insulators and to measure graphene's effective fine-structure constant. Since 2011 he has developed momentum-resolved inelastic electron scattering (M-EELS) with about 1 meV resolution to measure the dynamic charge susceptibility χ(q,ω). His profile calls the M-EELS instrument what he believes will be his "crowning achievement." With M-EELS his group reported a Bose condensate of excitons in TiSe2, popularly called "excitonium." His broader interests include:
- broken symmetries and intertwined orders;
- tests of holographic theories of strongly correlated matter;
- plasmons in strange metals;
- instrumentation such as electron optics and detectors.

Recent 2026 preprints cover M-EELS of 1T-TaS2, plasmon quantum geometry in Weyl metals and Kondo suppression of CDW in UTe3. He also co-authored a 2022 paper revisiting dark-matter interpretations of semiconductor excess rates.

## Public statements and values

In a 2015 *News-Gazette* interview about Inprentus, he explained why he started it: precision gratings "were only made in Europe; Zeiss in Germany and Hitachi in Japan. Both got out of the business because they were losing money." He said the old makers "were using gears and carriage returns," whereas Inprentus uses "atomic force microscope-based mechanical lithography." He added: "We knew there was a market." Together these show an instrument-builder's view that new measurement capability drives discovery, and a willingness to build tools himself (inference).
