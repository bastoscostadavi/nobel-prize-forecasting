## Biography

Klaus Sengstock is professor of experimental physics at the University of Hamburg. He leads the Quantum Gases group at the Institute for Quantum Physics and the Center for Optical Quantum Technologies on the Bahrenfeld campus (Luruper Chaussee). He is a co-speaker of research area A of the cluster of excellence "CUI: Advanced Imaging of Matter", together with Henry Chapman and Horst Weller. He is also a leading figure in Hamburg's quantum-computing activities, including Hamburg Quantum Computing (HQC) and the Rymax One Rydberg quantum computer project (Hamburg group page). Before Hamburg he worked in Wolfgang Ertmer's group at Hannover on Bose–Einstein condensate atom optics (co-authored chapter with Burger, Bongs and Ertmer). His exact education and appointment dates were not found in the sources available.

## Research footprint

The group page lists these research topics: quantum magnetism, topological quantum matter, optical atomic clocks, single-atom imaging, ultracold gases in combination with solid-state systems, ultrashort light pulses interacting with quantum gases, ytterbium-based quantum computing, and Bose–Einstein condensates. Representative verified publications:

- Early BEC atom optics and dark solitons in the Hannover group (with Burger, Bongs, Ertmer).
- "Quantum Simulation of Frustrated Classical Magnetism in Triangular Optical Lattices" (Struck, Ölschläger, Le Targat, Soltan-Panahi, Eckardt, Lewenstein, …, Sengstock; Science 2011), which used lattice shaking to engineer frustrated magnetism.
- "Experimental reconstruction of the Berry curvature in a Floquet Bloch band" (Fläschner, Rem, Tarnowski, Vogel, Lühmann, Sengstock, …; Science 2016), a landmark in measuring topological band geometry with cold atoms.

His focus has moved (per the sequence of these publications and his current roles) from BEC atom optics, through Floquet engineering and topology in optical lattices, toward quantum-computing hardware based on ytterbium and Rydberg atoms.
