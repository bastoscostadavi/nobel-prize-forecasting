## Biography

Juan Ignacio Cirac Sasturain was born on 11 October 1965 in Manresa (Barcelona), Spain. He received his PhD from the Complutense University of Madrid in 1991 (advisor Luis Lorenzo Sánchez Soto), then worked with Peter Zoller at JILA, Boulder. He taught at the University of Castilla-La Mancha (1991–1996) and became professor at the Institute for Theoretical Physics in Innsbruck in 1996. Since 2001 he has directed the Theory Division of MPQ, and since 2002 has been a distinguished visiting professor at ICFO, Barcelona. Honours include the Prince of Asturias Award (2006), BBVA Frontiers of Knowledge Award (2008), Benjamin Franklin Medal (2010), Wolf Prize in Physics (2013, with Zoller), Max Planck Medal and Micius Quantum Prize (2018), and the John Stewart Bell Prize (2019). He is a member of the Leopoldina (2017) and an APS Fellow (Wikipedia).

## Research footprint

Cirac's early work was in theoretical quantum optics (two-level atoms interacting with non-classical light). With Zoller he proposed the trapped-ion controlled-NOT gate (the Cirac–Zoller gate, 1995), a founding blueprint for ion-trap quantum computing. He then helped launch quantum simulation with cold atoms in optical lattices, and contributed to quantum communication (quantum repeaters) and entanglement theory (e.g. W states). From the 2000s his focus broadened to many-body theory via quantum information: with Frank Verstraete and others he developed projected entangled pair states (PEPS) and the wider tensor-network program for simulating strongly correlated systems. His arXiv output around 2018–2020 shows lattice gauge theories on quantum simulators, quantum metrology, emitters in structured baths, and iPEPS dynamics. The overall arc runs from quantum optics, through quantum computing proposals, to a general theory of entangled many-body states (Wikipedia; arXiv listing).
