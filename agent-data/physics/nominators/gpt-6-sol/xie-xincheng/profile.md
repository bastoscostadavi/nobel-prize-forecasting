## Biography

Born February 1959 in Nanjing, Jiangsu. He graduated from USTC's Department of Modern Physics (1982) and earned his PhD at the University of Maryland (1988). He was a postdoc at the University of Washington and Maryland. He joined Oklahoma State University in 1991 and became full professor there in 2001. In 2005 he returned to China at the Institute of Physics, CAS, where he directed the Laboratory of Condensed Matter Theory and Computational Materials Physics. He moved to Peking University in 2009 and was Dean of its School of Physics from 2011 to 2018. In 2018 he became Vice President of the National Natural Science Foundation of China (NSFC). He is a CAS academician (2015), a TWAS fellow, and an APS Fellow (2008, for contributions to the theory of 2D electron systems). (Wikipedia; CAS.)

## Research footprint

Xie is a theorist of electronic transport in low-dimensional and disordered systems. His early work covered the quantum Hall effect, metal–insulator transitions in 2D, and mesoscopic transport. With Hua Jiang, Qing-Feng Sun and collaborators at PKU/IOP, he produced influential numerical and analytical work on the topological Anderson insulator. This includes "Numerical study of the topological Anderson insulator in HgTe/CdTe quantum wells" (*PRB* 80, 165316, 2009; ~260 citations) and later work on disorder types and Anderson transitions in quantum spin Hall systems. He also showed that the quantized spin Hall resistance is robust to dephasing (*PRL* 103, 036803, 2009). His recent co-authored work includes collaborations with experimental groups:
- quantum anomalous Hall effect in MBE-grown MnBi2Te4 films
- thermal Hall effects
- quantum oscillations in topological materials

His focus has moved from quantum Hall and 2D-electron physics toward topological matter and transport signatures of topology.
