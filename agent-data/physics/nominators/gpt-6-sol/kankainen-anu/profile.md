## Biography

Anu Kristiina Kankainen first studied chemistry and mathematics, then moved to physics (Finnish Wikipedia). She received her PhD in physics from the University of Jyväskylä in 2006 under Juha Äystö, with a thesis on rare nuclei relevant to astrophysics. She was a teaching assistant (2006/7–2009) and an Academy of Finland postdoctoral researcher (2009–2012) at Jyväskylä. From 2013 to 2014 she was a postdoctoral research associate at the University of Edinburgh. She then returned to Jyväskylä as an Academy of Finland Research Fellow (2014–2019), became tenure-track associate professor in 2019 and professor in 2022, and was named Academy Professor in 2026 (ORCID). She holds an ERC Consolidator Grant (awarded 2017, project MAIDEN on the r-process). She received the Väisälä Prize of the Finnish Academy of Science and Letters in 2023 (Finnish Wikipedia).

## Research footprint

Kankainen leads Finland's nuclear-astrophysics group, which in 2021 was described as the only one in the country (Finnish Wikipedia). It sits within the Exotic Nuclei and Beams (IGISOL) group led by Ari Jokinen and Iain Moore. Her main method is high-precision mass measurement of short-lived, neutron- or proton-rich nuclei with the JYFLTRAP Penning trap at the IGISOL facility, together with beta-decay studies. The data constrain nucleosynthesis models for the rapid neutron-capture (r-) process in supernovae and neutron-star mergers, and the rapid proton-capture (rp-) process in type I X-ray bursts. Recent 2026 papers include direct mass measurements of ⁹¹Rh and ⁸⁹Ru (Physical Review C) and detector-array instrumentation (NIM A). She co-edited the volume *IGISOL: Three decades of research*. By mid-2023 she had published about 200 papers (Finnish Wikipedia).
