# Merge notes: claude-opus-5-5, run 2

All 100 source nominations were assigned exactly once to 45 candidate groups. Decisions use only this run's nomination records. Scientist frequencies count entries in the source `nominees` arrays; incidental names in motivations are not added as nominees. Ties use first occurrence in lexicographic source-file order, then the order of the nominees within that file. The representative subfield is taken from the lexicographically first source in each group.

## Ambiguous merge and split decisions

- **Topological insulators (c03), the quantum anomalous Hall effect (c18), and their compound nomination (c44):** `fang-zhong.json` explicitly bundles Z2 topological-insulator classification, quantum spin Hall transport, and quantum anomalous Hall transport. Under the explicit compound-candidate rule, it is preserved once as c44 because neither narrower group preserves this full bundle. Its source nominees Kane, Molenkamp and Qi-Kun Xue are retained together. The eight narrower topological-insulator nominations form c03; the two dedicated QAH nominations `samuelson-lars.json` and `wang-enge.json` form c18. `damascelli-andrea.json`, which credits Hasan's three-dimensional surface-state spectroscopy, remains in c03 because it presents that work as experimental establishment of the same topological-insulator discovery; Hasan remains in `other_names`. Removing the compound nomination from c03 leaves Kane and Molenkamp with eight occurrences each and Mele with seven.
- **Optical-lattice simulation (c02) versus general theoretical atomic quantum computing (c42):** `bloch-immanuel.json` is kept as a separate broad nomination for Cirac and Zoller's trapped-ion quantum logic and atomic quantum simulation foundations. Its explicitly central 1995 ion-gate blueprint is distinct from the optical-lattice Hubbard realization and gas microscopy in c02. The lattice-specific sources `lewenstein-maciej.json`, `lukin-mikhail.json`, and `ye-jun.json` are merged with the other lattice simulation nominations despite their alternative Cirac/Bloch/Greiner credits. Programmable Rydberg arrays (c05) also remain separate because blockade entanglement and rearrangeable tweezer arrays are a different invention.
- **Broad ultracold molecular control (c24) versus direct molecular laser cooling (c26):** `larsson-mats.json` includes Ye's near-degenerate ground-state KRb preparation and quantum-controlled chemistry alongside buffer-gas and laser cooling. `meijer-gerard.json` specifically targets closed optical cycling, direct laser cooling, molecular MOTs and Tarbutt's cooling/trapping work. They remain separate because reducing the broad nomination to direct laser cooling would obscure the ground-state polar-molecule and chemistry contribution and change the appropriate credited scientists.
- **Establishment of anyons (c09):** `hansson-thors-hans.json`, `stern-ady.json`, and `viefers-susanne.json` are merged around fractional exchange/braiding statistics. They combine different stages and methods: theoretical prediction, collider correlations, interferometric braiding, and fractional-charge measurements. The common anyonic-statistics achievement is explicit in all three. Frequency ordering puts Manfra (three source nominations), Fève (two), and Rosenow (one, earliest tied appearance) in `nominees`; Heiblum, Leinaas and Myrheim remain in `other_names`. Fractional quantum anomalous Hall states (c14) remain separate: their stated achievement is zero-field fractional Chern-band order in different materials, rather than the GaAs statistics measurements.
- **Magic-angle graphene (c01) versus zero-field fractional Chern states (c14):** Later fractional Hall states mentioned in motivations for magic-angle graphene are described as consequences of the twistronics platform. These sources stay with the 2011 flat-band prediction and 2018 correlated-insulator/superconductivity discovery. The two nominations specifically for the 2023–2024 fractional quantum anomalous Hall discovery form c14.
- **Rydberg-array credits (c05):** `aspect-alain.json` emphasizes Grangier's initial single-atom tweezers and blockade entanglement. It is merged with Browaeys/Lukin/Saffman array nominations because all explicitly trace programmable neutral-atom arrays to the same tweezer/blockade platform. Grangier is preserved in `other_names`.
- **Circuit QED (c12):** Girvin and Wallraff are alternate credits for the same proposed and demonstrated strong-coupling architecture. Schoelkopf and Blais each appear twice; Girvin and Wallraff appear once, with Girvin first in `devoret-michel.json`, so Girvin takes the third nominee position and Wallraff is retained in `other_names`.
- **Fluctuation relations (c13):** The Crooks- and Evans-credit variants are merged because both target exact far-from-equilibrium work/entropy relations and Seifert's stochastic-thermodynamic framework. Jarzynski and Seifert each appear twice. Crooks wins the one-appearance tie over Evans because `splettstoesser-janine.json` precedes `ueda-masahito.json`; Evans is retained in `other_names`.

## Name normalizations

The top-level `name_normalizations` object records all differing exact source spellings listed below; exact canonical spellings are omitted from alias lists.

- `J. Ignacio Cirac` in `lewenstein-maciej.json` and `lukin-mikhail.json`, and `Ignacio Cirac` in `ye-jun.json`, are normalized to `Juan Ignacio Cirac`, the spelling in `bloch-immanuel.json`. The shared surname, given name and Max Planck affiliation in the source records support the identity. This combines three Cirac occurrences in c02; it does not merge the distinct achievements c02 and c42.
- `Qikun Xue` in `samuelson-lars.json` is normalized to `Qi-Kun Xue`, the spelling in `fang-zhong.json` and `wang-enge.json`. Matching Tsinghua/SUSTech affiliations and the same 2013 experiment support the identity. This combines two occurrences in c18.
- All other nominee names are preserved as supplied, including accents, initials and the parenthetical form `Mordehai (Moty) Heiblum`. Affiliation variation does not create additional identities.

## Source-record condition

No malformed source record was found. All 100 files parse as JSON, contain the required nomination fields, and have one to three named nominee entries. Compound one-off nominations, including multiband superconductivity in MgB2 and iron-based materials (c34), geometric phases (c20), and femtosecond laser technologies (c43), are preserved as nominated rather than split or discarded.
