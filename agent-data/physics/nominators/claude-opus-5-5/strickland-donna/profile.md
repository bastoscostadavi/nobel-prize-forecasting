## Biography

Born 27 May 1959 in Guelph, Ontario. She received a B.Eng. in engineering physics from McMaster University (1981) and a PhD from the University of Rochester's Institute of Optics (1989), supervised by Gérard Mourou. She was a research associate at the National Research Council of Canada (1988–91), where she worked with Paul Corkum. She then worked at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (1991–92) and at Princeton's Advanced Technology Center (1992–97), and joined Waterloo in 1997. She was promoted to full professor in 2018. Her honours include the 2018 Nobel Prize in Physics (with Mourou and Ashkin), Companion of the Order of Canada (2019), and the Légion d'honneur (2022). She is a Fellow of the Royal Society and a member of the US NAS (Wikipedia). She was president of Optica (then OSA) in 2013.

## Research footprint

As a doctoral student she co-developed chirped pulse amplification (CPA) with Mourou (published 1985). In CPA a short pulse is stretched, amplified, and then recompressed, which avoids damaging the amplifier. It became the standard route to ultrashort, ultra-intense laser pulses, used in laser eye surgery, micromachining and high-field physics. Her later work at Waterloo has covered high-intensity laser–matter interactions, multi-frequency and nonlinear-optical sources that extend lasers into the mid-infrared and ultraviolet, and laser applications such as treating presbyopia (Wikipedia). She is an experimentalist and builds laser systems.

## Public statements and values

- "I will use the platform the Nobel Prize affords me to continue to advocate on behalf of science and the many scientists who devote their lives to probing the fundamental questions" (Nobel biographical essay).
- On CPA: "it was just a fun thing to do... It is the one time in my life that I worked very, very hard!" (NobelPrize.org telephone interview, 2018).
- She said she was not "a woman whose been looking at these prizes thinking 'Why isn't there a woman?'", but she tells the story of Maria Goeppert Mayer, who went years without a paid position (same interview).
- On not applying earlier for full professor: "I never applied... it doesn't carry necessarily a pay raise" (reported on Wikipedia).
