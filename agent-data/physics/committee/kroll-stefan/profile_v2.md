## Biography

Kröll completed doctoral work at Lund University under Sune Svanberg in laser spectroscopy of free atoms; the publication record places the dissertation in the mid-1980s. His doctoral period included a research stay in John Hall's laboratory in Boulder. He subsequently worked with Marcus Aldén at Lund on nonlinear optical modelling and spent time at SRI International studying coherent light–matter interactions in rare-earth-doped crystals. He became professor of atomic physics at Lund in 1999 and led the university's Quantum Information Group. His administrative record includes service as head of the Department of Physics and on the LTH faculty board. He has also participated in WACQT management and in the company Deep Light Vision. The Academy elected him to its Class for Physics in January 2019.

## Research / intellectual footprint

- His early research used high-resolution laser spectroscopy to study atomic hyperfine structure, radiative lifetimes and optogalvanic signals, followed by work on coherent nonlinear optics.
- From the 1990s onward, he worked on spectral-hole burning, photon echoes and optical storage in rare-earth-ion-doped crystals.
- Publications in quantum information include proposals and experiments on crystal-based qubits, controlled reversible inhomogeneous broadening, atomic-frequency-comb storage, spin-wave storage and efficient optical quantum memories.
- Later research applies slow-light filters in spectral-hole-burning materials to ultrasound optical tomography, including modelling and experimental work aimed at biomedical imaging.
- Recent listed topics include laser stabilisation, optical imaging and fibre-based quantum-repeaters.
