## Biography

According to INSPIRE-HEP, Ronald (Ron) J. Lipton received his PhD from Northwestern University. His late-1970s papers (Phys. Rev. Lett. 1978) are from fixed-target experiments on prompt-muon and charm production with the Northwestern group of B. Gobbi. He has been a Fermilab scientist since at least 1990. He was a member of the D0 collaboration (co-author of the 1995 top-quark observation) and wrote the 1998 overview of the D0 silicon tracker. Later he joined CMS and remains on its author list in 2026. No personal honours were found in the sources checked.

## Research footprint

Lipton's career has two strands: authorship in large collider collaborations, and hands-on detector development. As a D0 and CMS member he appears on the top-quark discovery (1995) and the Higgs-boson observation (2012), plus many CMS performance papers. He has 2,336 INSPIRE records, most of them collaboration papers. His own contributions are in silicon detectors and their electronics. He described the D0 silicon tracker (1998). With Grzegorz Deptuch, James Hoff and others he developed vertically integrated (3D) pixel readout for ILC vertex detectors and X-ray imaging (2008–2015). He co-authored muon-collider studies, including the 2013 proposal for a U.S. muon accelerator facility and Higgs self-coupling at a multi-TeV muon collider (2014). Since about 2020 he has worked on low-gain avalanche diodes (LGADs) for 4D (space-time) tracking: buried-layer and double-sided LGADs, single-event-burnout studies, LGAD-in-CMOS design (2021–22), and LGAD sensors on 12-inch wafers for 3D integration with advanced CMOS (2026). He co-authored Snowmass 2021 reports on 4D trackers, fast-timing electronics and integration/packaging. In 2024 he also contributed to quantum-network component work on silicon-carbide-on-insulator.
