## Biography

Johansson completed a licentiate and then a PhD in theoretical physics at Chalmers, receiving the doctorate in 1998. His thesis concerned multiple Andreev reflection and the ac Josephson effect in mesoscopic junctions. He then spent two years as a postdoctoral researcher in Karlsruhe before returning to Chalmers. He is now professor of applied and theoretical quantum physics in the Applied Quantum Physics Laboratory. In January 2021 he was elected to the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences, Class for Physics. In 2025 he succeeded Per Delsing as director of WACQT, a national research programme involving several Swedish universities. He presented the scientific explanation at the 2025 Nobel Prize announcement and later delivered the presentation speech at the award ceremony.

## Research / intellectual footprint

- His doctoral and early work addressed mesoscopic charge transport, Josephson junctions and readout of superconducting charge qubits.
- He proposed a superconducting-circuit implementation of the dynamical Casimir effect and co-authored the later experimental observation reported in *Nature* in 2011.
- Other research includes the scattering of microwave fields from artificial atoms, single-photon routing, cross-Kerr effects, surface-acoustic-wave coupling and “giant atoms” in waveguide QED.
- More recent publications concern gate fidelity, decoherence, multi-qubit gates, microwave-photon generation, processor components and quantum algorithms.
- His publication record includes both theory-only papers and collaborations with experimental groups at Chalmers and elsewhere.
- This record is included solely to document subject-matter experience in superconducting circuits and quantum information.
