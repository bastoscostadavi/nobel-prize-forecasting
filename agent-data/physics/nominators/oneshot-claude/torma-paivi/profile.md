## Biography

Törmä was born in Nivala, Finland, on 19 October 1969. She took her master's studies at Oulu and Cambridge and received a PhD in theoretical physics from the University of Helsinki in 1996, supervised by Stig Stenholm. She was a postdoc with Wolfgang Schleich in Ulm and a Marie Curie Fellow with Peter Zoller in Innsbruck. She became a professor at the University of Jyväskylä in 2001 and moved to Aalto (then Helsinki University of Technology) in 2008.

Her service roles include:
- leading the Nanoscience Centre in Jyväskylä;
- directing a Centre of Excellence in Computational Nanoscience;
- vice chair of the Academy of Finland board;
- member of Finland's Research and Innovation Council;
- chair of the Millennium Technology Prize international selection committee (2017–2024).

Her honours are an ERC Advanced Grant (2013), an Academy Professorship (2017–21), membership of Academia Europaea, the Order of the White Rose (First Class), the Magnus Ehrnrooth Prize (2019) and the Theodor Homén Prize (2025).

## Research footprint

Her PhD was on optical multiports and measuring quantum states of light. In Innsbruck she was among the first theorists to work on ultracold Fermi gases, and she proposed a spectroscopic probe of their superfluidity. In Jyväskylä she started an experimental programme in nanophotonics that led to observations of strong coupling, lasing and Bose–Einstein condensation in plasmonic lattices. On the theory side she showed that quantum geometry contributes to superfluid weight. This result links band topology to superconductivity in flat bands and bears on twisted multilayer graphene. She co-edited *Quantum Gas Experiments: Exploring Many-Body States* (2015). She is unusual in running both theory and experiment.
