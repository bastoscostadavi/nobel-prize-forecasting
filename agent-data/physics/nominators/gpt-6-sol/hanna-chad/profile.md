## Biography

Hanna began working on LIGO in 2004 as an undergraduate at Penn State (Penn State News, 2016). INSPIRE lists past affiliations with Louisiana State University and the Perimeter Institute. He was a postdoctoral scholar in the LIGO Laboratory at Caltech (2008–2010) and a postdoctoral fellow at the Perimeter Institute (2010–2013). He joined the Penn State faculty in 2014 as an assistant professor. In 2016 he became the inaugural Norman and Trygve Freed Early Career Professor in Physics. At that time he was co-chair of the LIGO Scientific Collaboration's Compact Binary Coalescence Astrophysics group. His faculty page now lists him as Distinguished Professor. He is affiliated with LIGO and SCiMMA (Scalable Cyberinfrastructure for Multi-Messenger Astrophysics).

## Research footprint

Hanna works on detecting compact-binary mergers in gravitational-wave data quickly and sensitively. With Kipp Cannon, Drew Keppel and others he developed singular-value-decomposition filtering (*PRD* 82, 2010), early-warning detection of binary coalescences (*ApJ* 748, 2012) and likelihood-ratio ranking statistics. These methods fed into the GstLAL pipeline. Its analysis framework for prompt discovery is described in Messick et al., *PRD* 95 (2017). His group's projects include:

- real-time noise reduction;
- the iDQ data-quality engine;
- searches for sub-solar-mass black holes, used to constrain primordial black holes as dark matter (*PRL* 121, 231103, 2018);
- NSF-funded cyberinfrastructure for multi-messenger astronomy.

Earlier theory-leaning work covered ISCO and ring-down frequencies (with Palenzuela and others) and electromagnetic counterparts of neutron-star mergers (with Luis Lehner). His focus has moved from search methods, to operating detection pipelines through the discovery era, to computing and astrophysical population science.

## Public statements and values

- On the 2016 detection announcement: "This detection from LIGO is one of the biggest physics discoveries of this century" (Penn State News, 11 Feb 2016).
- In the same piece: when the public learns "the space around them everywhere is wiggling about because somewhere off in the universe a black hole has merged, I think that will send a pretty profound message ... about just how cool a place our universe is."
- He wrote for *The Conversation* (2016) about receiving LIGO's detection alert, and again in 2017 on the multi-messenger era.
- He stresses undergraduate research, which is how he entered the field himself.
