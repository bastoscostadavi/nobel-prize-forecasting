## Biography

Giudice was born in Padua on 25 January 1961. He graduated in physics from the University of Padua (1984) and received his PhD from SISSA, Trieste (1988) under Riccardo Barbieri. He was a Research Associate at Fermilab (1988–1990) and a Research Fellow in Steven Weinberg's group at UT Austin (1990–1992). After a period with INFN, he joined CERN in 1993 and later headed its Theoretical Physics Department. He is a member of the Accademia dei Lincei, the Istituto Veneto and the Accademia Galileiana. He has held:
- Jacques Solvay Chair (ULB, 2013)
- Perimeter Distinguished Visiting Research Chair (2019)
- Sackler Lecturer (Tel Aviv, 2020)
- Chandrasekhar Lecturer (TIFR, 2022)
- Erwin Schrödinger Professor (Vienna, 2023)

(Wikipedia.)

## Research footprint

Giudice builds theories beyond the Standard Model and connects them to colliders and cosmology. His main contributions:
- **Supersymmetry.** The Barbieri–Giudice naturalness (fine-tuning) measure; the Giudice–Masiero mechanism for the μ-problem; gauge mediation (review with Rattazzi, ~1,900 citations); early anomaly-mediation (with Luty, Murayama and Rattazzi) and split-supersymmetry papers; analytic continuation into superspace.
- **Extra dimensions.** Graviton phenomenology for LHC studies.
- **Minimal Flavour Violation.** With D'Ambrosio, Isidori and Strumia (2002, ~2,100 citations).
- **Higgs vacuum stability.** At NNLO (2012), finding that the Higgs vacuum is near-critical (2013).

He contributed to the Review of Particle Physics, the LHC Higgs Cross Section handbook and the FCC-ee design report. His 2025–2026 preprints turn to black-hole quasinormal modes, photon rings and quantum chaos, and to a "super-composite Higgs" (with Agashe, Rattazzi and Sundrum). His focus has moved from supersymmetric model-building toward naturalness, near-criticality and gravitational questions.

## Public statements and values

He is an active popular-science communicator. He wrote *A Zeptospace Odyssey*, a book on LHC physics that was a finalist for the 2012 Galileo Prize, gave a TED talk, and took part in the 2012 Isaac Asimov Memorial Debate. He has advocated future accelerators through the LHC Experiments Committee, ECFA and the 2020 European Strategy preparatory group. In 2024 he wrote "Remembering Steven Weinberg".
