## Biography

Holmestad was born in 1967. She earned a Siv.ing. (MSc) in technical physics in 1991 and a Dr.ing. (PhD) in materials physics in 1994, both at NTH (now NTNU). She worked at SINTEF in 1995–1996 and has been a professor at NTNU Physics since 1999 (NTNU page). The CASA profile says she became the university's youngest female professor at 30, in 1998. She led the TEM Gemini Centre (SINTEF/NTNU) in 2006 and 2008–2013. She was project leader of NORTEM, the Norwegian Centre for Transmission Electron Microscopy, in 2010–2013 and again from 2017. NORTEM is a national infrastructure run with SINTEF and the University of Oslo. She is a member of the Norwegian Academy of Technological Sciences (2006) and the Royal Norwegian Society of Sciences and Letters (2008) (Wikipedia).

## Research footprint

Her work links microstructure to macroscopic properties using:
- TEM, HREM, STEM, EDS and EELS;
- quantitative convergent-beam electron diffraction;
- electron crystallography;
- scanning precession electron diffraction.

She also uses solid-state calculations. Her main topic is precipitation in age-hardenable Al–Mg–Si(–Cu) alloys: the role of alloy composition and Cu, and the composition of β″ precipitates studied with atom-probe tomography and first-principles calculations. Other areas include solar-cell materials and functional oxides. She co-developed Atomap (2017), open software for analysing atomic-resolution images. Since 2015 she has worked in the SFI CASA centre for advanced structural analysis, and since 2021 in SFI PhysMet, both with strong industrial partners such as Hydro. She holds a patent on an aluminium alloy (CASA).

## Public statements and values

On long-term work with Hydro and SINTEF: "I feel privileged for taking part. This kind of long-term cooperation has huge, mutual benefits." On alloy design: "By smart alloy design, we can reduce the total solute, the alloys can be easier processed and Hydro saves money." (both from the CASA profile, 2015). The same profile says her role is to go "as far down in detail as necessary, not further".
