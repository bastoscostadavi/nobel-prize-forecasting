## Biography

Kivelson was born in Boston on 13 May 1954. He is the son of UCLA space physicist Margaret Kivelson and chemist Daniel Kivelson (Wikipedia). He earned his PhD at Harvard in 1979 under C. Daniel Gelatt Jr. and was then a postdoc at the ITP in Santa Barbara with J. Robert Schrieffer. He was on the faculty at Stony Brook (1982–1989) and UCLA (1988/89–2004), and then moved to Stanford, where he became Prabhu Goel Family Professor in 2012. His honours include a Guggenheim Fellowship (1995), the American Academy of Arts and Sciences (2001), the National Academy of Sciences (2010), the Bardeen Prize (2012) and the APS Oliver E. Buckley Prize (2025), awarded "for broad and insightful theoretical contributions that have significantly advanced the understanding of correlated quantum systems."

## Research footprint

His current research covers the theory of quantum liquid-crystalline phases of correlated electron fluids, intertwined orders and high-temperature superconductivity, spin liquids and other fractionalized phases, and the glass transition in supercooled liquids (Stanford profile). Landmark work includes:
- Resonating-valence-bond topology and the Rokhsar–Kivelson quantum dimer model (late 1980s).
- The Kivelson–Lee–Zhang global phase diagram of the quantum Hall effect (1992).
- The Emery–Kivelson analysis of phase fluctuations in superconductors with low superfluid density (1995).
- Stripe-fluctuation mechanisms and the Kivelson–Fradkin–Emery "electronic liquid crystal" framework (1998), extended to nematic Fermi fluids with Oganesyan and Fradkin (2001).
- The "intertwined orders" perspective on the cuprates (review with Fradkin and Tranquada, 2015).

He co-wrote the textbook *Statistical Mechanics of Phases and Phase Transitions* (Princeton UP, 2024).
