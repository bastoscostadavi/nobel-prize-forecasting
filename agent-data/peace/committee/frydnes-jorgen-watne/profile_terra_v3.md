## Research and public-policy record

He has no known record of party-political activity or of academic writing on security policy.

In his 2025 speech he made a sustained argument for a **democratic-peace** position. He said the research answer to whether democracy leads to peace is "crystal clear" and that the "mechanisms of democracy make war less likely".

In his 2024 presentation speech he spoke about **nuclear disarmament**. He called on the NPT nuclear-weapon states to honour their treaty obligations and on "courageous and visionary political leaders" to take up disarmament.

He turned Utøya into a "learning arena" for democracy and dialogue (the Hegnhuset learning centre).

Not an academic researcher. His public work covers **memory politics and commemoration after terror**, **dialogue and tolerance**, **freedom of expression** (PEN) and **human rights** (Helsinki Committee).

## Biographical record

He became Secretary General of Norsk PEN in 2023. He was elected to the Nobel Committee in late 2020 as its youngest ever permanent member and in 2024 became its youngest ever chair. He has sat on the Norwegian Helsinki Committee's board and chaired the council of ARKIVET peace and human rights centre, stepping down from both in spring 2025 (2026 register). Author of *Ingen mann er en øy* (2021; English edition *No Man Is an Island*, UMass Press, 2025).

Born 26 November 1984 in Bergen. BA in political science (University of Oslo) and MA in international politics (University of York). He worked for Médecins Sans Frontières from 2004 to 2011 and later sat on the board of its Norwegian section. From 2011 to 2023 he was managing director of Utøya AS, where he led the rebuilding of the island after the 22 July 2011 terrorist attack. Utøya and Frydnes received the Fritt Ord Tribute in 2021.

## Professional connections

- Labour-nominated seat (succeeded Thorbjørn Jagland). Berit Reiss-Andersen, his predecessor as chair, acted as his lawyer in the 2024 PwC/Utøya book dispute.
- Networks: Norsk PEN, Norwegian Helsinki Committee, MSF Norway, ARKIVET, and the Memory Matters consortium.
- Works with secretary Kristian Berg Harpviken, whom he welcomed as "a very important contributor to carry forward Alfred Nobel's legacy" (NRK 2024).

## Documented public statements

- 2024 speech: "the nuclear taboo is maintained by collective agreement… But the taboo is fragile". He described himself as belonging to a generation that lived through "an optimistic bubble in history. That time is over," and said "Memory work can be an act of resistance."
- 2025 press release (committee text): "Democracy is a precondition for lasting peace"; "The tools of democracy are also the tools of peace."
- 2025 speech: "If you only support people who share your political views, you have understood neither freedom nor democracy." He criticised expecting activists under dictatorship to show "a moral purity their opponents never display". He said dialogue in authoritarian systems "can lead to improvement – but it can also be a trap."
- On lobbying (Oct 2025): the committee "sits in a room filled with the portraits of all laureates and that room is filled with both courage and integrity"; decisions rest "only [on] the work and the will of Alfred Nobel." He also said: "we have to stay strong and principled in our choices… that's our job" (Al Jazeera).
- March 2026, on the new ethics and disclosure rules after the Jagland/Epstein revelations: "We should have done this long ago"; "In the final consequence, we can ask a member to step down" (E24).
- 2020 keynote: "being tolerant is and should be hard" (Körber-Stiftung, via Wikipedia).
