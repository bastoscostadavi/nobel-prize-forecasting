## Biography

Born in 1982 in Zhejiang, China. He received his B.Sc. from USTC in 2004. He then earned a Ph.D. in physics at the Cavendish Laboratory, University of Cambridge (2011), advised by Pan Jianwei and Mete Atatüre. His thesis was on multiphoton entanglement and optically active quantum dots. He was a fellow of Churchill College, Cambridge, and has been a professor of physics at USTC since 2011. His honours include the EPS Fresnel Prize (2017, fundamental aspects), Optica (OSA) Fellowship (2017), the APS Rolf Landauer and Charles H. Bennett Award in Quantum Computing (2021), a World Economic Forum Young Global Leader title (2021) and MIT Technology Review Innovators Under 35 (2017). He also shared a First-Class State Natural Science Award.

## Research footprint

Lu works on scalable quantum photonics. During his Ph.D. he did quantum-dot spin measurements. He developed deterministic, high-quality single-photon sources based on pulsed resonance fluorescence of quantum dots, and set records for multiphoton entanglement (eight photons, and later more). With Pan Jianwei he led the quantum teleportation of multiple degrees of freedom of a single photon (Nature 2015), which Physics World named its 2015 Breakthrough of the Year. He also led boson-sampling experiments, culminating in the "Jiuzhang" Gaussian boson-sampling photonic processor, which claimed a quantum computational advantage (Zhong et al., Science 2020). He contributed to Micius satellite experiments, including ground-to-satellite teleportation. His recent interests also include superconducting circuits and atom arrays (OCPA bio).
