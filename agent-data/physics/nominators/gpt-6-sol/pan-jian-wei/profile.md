## Biography

Born 11 March 1970 in Dongyang, Zhejiang. He earned his bachelor's and master's degrees at USTC (entered 1987) and his PhD at the University of Vienna in Anton Zeilinger's group. He returned to lead quantum-information research at USTC and became Vice President of USTC in 2014. He was elected to the CAS in 2011 at age 41, and to TWAS in 2012. He is a Fellow of the Royal Society (2024), APS and Optica, and a vice chairman of the Jiusan Society. His honours include:
- Fresnel Prize (2005)
- State Natural Science Award, First Class (2015, with his team)
- Future Science Prize (2017)
- Willis E. Lamb Award (2018)
- R. W. Wood Prize (2019)
- ZEISS Research Award (2020)
- IEEE Photonics Society Quantum Electronics Award (2026, with Chao-Yang Lu)
- UNESCO-Russia Mendeleev International Prize in the Basic Sciences (2026)

*Nature* named him one of "Nature's 10" in 2017, and *Time* included him in its 100 most influential people in 2018. (Wikipedia.)

## Research footprint

As a doctoral student in Vienna he co-authored "Experimental quantum teleportation" (Bouwmeester, Pan et al., *Nature* 390, 1997; ~4,800 Crossref citations) and early entanglement-swapping experiments. At USTC his group built a series of multiphoton experiments:
- five-photon entanglement (2004), followed by steadily larger photon-entanglement records
- teleportation of multiple degrees of freedom, a *Physics World* 2015 Breakthrough of the Year

He led the scientific side of the Micius satellite (QUESS, launched 2016). Its results include satellite-based entanglement distribution over 1,200 km (Yin et al., *Science* 2017; ~1,250 citations) and satellite quantum key distribution and teleportation. From about 2020 his teams pursued quantum computational advantage with two platforms: photonic boson sampling (Jiuzhang) and superconducting processors (Zuchongzhi). Recent 2026 preprints address surface-code logical operations, atom-array readout and Rydberg-atom control. His work has moved from foundational tests of entanglement toward large-scale engineering of quantum networks and processors.
