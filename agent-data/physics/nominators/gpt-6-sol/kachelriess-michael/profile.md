## Biography

Kachelrieß (also spelled Kachelriess) is a professor of physics at NTNU in Trondheim. He teaches gravitation and cosmology, classical mechanics, advanced quantum field theory, particle physics and astrophysics (personal homepage). His career can be traced through his INSPIRE paper affiliations: Gran Sasso (1997), CERN and IFIC Valencia (around 2000), and the Max Planck Institute for Physics in Munich (2004–2005). NTNU appears from at least 2011. He wrote the graduate textbook *Quantum Fields: From the Hubble to the Planck Scale* (Oxford University Press), reviewed in the CERN Courier in July 2018. His education and exact appointment dates were not found.

## Research footprint

His NTNU page lists these research interests:
- high-energy astrophysics;
- hypothetical particles in astrophysics and cosmology;
- supersymmetry and physics beyond the Standard Model;
- neutrino masses and mixing;
- matter under extreme conditions.

His most-cited paper is "Ultrahigh-energy cosmic rays without GZK cutoff" (Berezinsky, Kachelriess, Vilenkin, *PRL* 1997). Other highly cited work:
- supernova neutrino physics: the neutronization burst, shock propagation, and Majoron bounds, with Tomàs, Raffelt, Janka and Valle;
- lower limits on extragalactic magnetic fields (Dolag et al. 2011);
- constraints on ultra-high-energy cosmic rays and cosmogenic neutrinos from Fermi-LAT;
- cosmic-ray anisotropy and the Galactic-to-extragalactic transition.

He co-develops public codes: ELMAG for electromagnetic cascades and AAfrag for secondary-particle yields. Recent work (2024–2026) covers:
- Cygnus X-3 as a PeVatron in light of LHAASO data;
- the knee of the cosmic-ray spectrum;
- the structure of the Galactic magnetic field from polarized synchrotron data;
- intergalactic magnetic-field models for multi-messenger astronomy.

His focus has moved from neutrino and beyond-Standard-Model phenomenology toward cosmic-ray propagation and Galactic sources.
