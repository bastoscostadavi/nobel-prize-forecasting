# Merge notes

Consolidated the assigned run's 100 nomination JSON files into 54 candidate groups using only those files and `prompts/physics_merge_v1.md`. Every source is assigned once. Counts use the people explicitly listed in each source's `nominees` field; names mentioned only in motivation prose are explanatory context, not additional nominees. Sources within each group are ordered lexicographically, and equal-frequency people are ordered by first appearance in that order, including order within a source's nominees array.

## Ambiguous achievement boundaries

- The optical-lattice nominations were merged despite variation between a title emphasizing the superfluid–Mott transition, quantum gas microscopy, or analog many-body simulation, and despite different nominee subsets. Their motivations identify the same 1998 lattice-model proposal, 2002 transition experiment, and resulting lattice simulator program. The broader nominations from `lhuillier-anne`, `lukin-mikhail`, and `ye-jun` remain here because their discovery titles and central experimental achievement are optical-lattice simulation; their mentions of ion gates supply context.
- `bloch-immanuel.json` explicitly combines two distinct theoretical architectures: the 1995 trapped-ion quantum computer and the 1998 optical-lattice Hubbard simulator. It is retained as a separate combined candidate. Merging it with either the optical-lattice group or `molmer-klaus.json` (trapped-ion quantum computing with Blatt's experiments) would either obscure one of its equally explicit contributions or extend the narrower group's achievement and nominee set. It is not duplicated between groups.
- `fang-zhong.json` explicitly joins Kane and Mele's topological-insulator theory with Xue's experimental quantum anomalous Hall effect. It remains a combined singleton, separate from the quantum spin Hall/topological-insulator group and the magnetic-topological-insulator quantum anomalous Hall group. The former centers on time-reversal-invariant helical transport with Molenkamp; the latter centers on magnetization-driven quantized chiral transport with Fang and Dai. Assigning the combined record to either would broaden the contribution and change the laureates it credits.
- Magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene nominations were merged around the flat-band prediction and correlated insulating/superconducting discovery. Later fractional Chern insulators mentioned as consequences do not make those nominations equivalent to the three explicit zero-field fractional Chern-insulator nominations. Those three were merged across twisted MoTe2 and rhombohedral graphene because all explicitly nominate the same zero-field fractional Hall discovery, and all credit Xu, Mak and Ju.
- `hansson-thors-hans.json` is the theoretical discovery of anyons from two-dimensional particle configuration space and flux-charge composites. `viefers-susanne.json` is the prediction and direct measurement of fractional quantum Hall exchange statistics through collision and interferometry experiments. They remain separate: merging would obscure the experimental contribution and materially change the named people.
- `atature-mete.json` spans defect and semiconductor-dot spin-photon interfaces and remote spin entanglement/quantum networks. `awschalom-david.json` centers on diamond nitrogen-vacancy electron/nuclear spin control and nanoscale magnetometry. They remain separate because the first's quantum-dot interfaces and remote networking substantially extend the second's diamond sensing contribution and nominate different specialists.
- `simmons-michelle.json` explicitly combines two semiconductor architectures: Loss's quantum-dot electron-spin qubits and Kane's phosphorus-donor nuclear-spin qubits. Under the compound-candidate policy it is preserved as a standalone combined candidate with the source's Loss, Bruce E. Kane and Vandersypen nominee order. `flensberg-karsten.json` remains a separate dots-only candidate, preserving Loss, Charles M. Marcus and Vandersypen in source order. Merging these records would obscure the distinction between electrically controlled dot electron spins and phosphorus-donor nuclear spins, while assigning the combined source to the dots-only group would omit its donor architecture. Neither source is duplicated. Optical spin-photon interfaces and diamond NV sensing remain separate because their control/readout architectures and nominated contributions differ.
- Circuit QED nominations were merged around strong coupling between superconducting artificial atoms and microwave photons. Girvin and Wallraff receive different credit subsets; both are preserved, with Girvin taking the tied third place by the earlier `devoret-michel.json` source.
- Optomechanics and quantum acoustics nominations were merged because both explicitly credit the same trio for quantum ground-state control of macroscopic mechanical resonators, radiation-pressure cooling, and single-phonon control; differing emphasis on optical or superconducting coupling does not change the shared contribution.
- Both fluctuation-theorem nominations were merged around exact nonequilibrium work/entropy relations and stochastic thermodynamics. Seifert's trajectory framework and Crooks's work relation are variants within that shared nominated program, with both names preserved. Seifert takes the tied third place because `splettstoesser-janine.json` precedes `ueda-masahito.json`.
- BAO nominations were merged around the acoustic standard ruler despite one including the early theoretical prediction and the other concentrating on survey discovery and reconstruction. Eisenstein and Cole lead by frequency; Sunyaev precedes the tied Percival because `lahav-ofer.json` is earlier.
- Perovskite semiconductor and perovskite solar-cell nominations were merged because they identify the same photovoltaic absorber, solid-state device and charge-transport discoveries and nominate the same trio. Metamaterials/metasurfaces remain separate from photonic crystals because their nominated mechanisms are engineered subwavelength response and wavefront control versus periodic dielectric band gaps.

## Name normalization and counting

The top-level `name_normalizations` object in `candidates.json` records these canonical names and their differing source spellings; exact matches to each canonical spelling are omitted from the mappings.

- `Juan Ignacio Cirac`, `Ignacio Cirac`, and `J. Ignacio Cirac` were normalized to `Juan Ignacio Cirac`. Matching institute affiliations and the explicitly shared Cirac–Zoller proposals establish the identity within these sources. The optical-lattice group counts one appearance in each of `lhuillier-anne.json`, `lukin-mikhail.json`, and `ye-jun.json`, for three nominations, and retains Cirac in `other_names`. The same canonical form is used in the combined-theory singleton and trapped-ion singleton.
- `Qikun Xue` in `samuelson-lars.json` was normalized to `Qi-Kun Xue`, matching `wang-enge.json` through the same institution, 2013 Cr-doped-film discovery and collaborating theorists. The quantum anomalous Hall group therefore counts two appearances of that person. The combined topological-insulator/QAH singleton uses the same canonical form.
- No other name normalizations were needed. In the magic-angle group Allan H. MacDonald and Pablo Jarillo-Herrero each appear in all 13 sources; Rafi Bistritzer appears in 12. MacDonald takes the first tied position from `balents-leon.json`. In the optical-lattice group Bloch and Zoller each appear in all 13 sources, Greiner in nine, Cirac in three and Dalibard in one. Bloch precedes the tied Zoller from `aspect-alain.json`.
- All other tie decisions follow the same mechanical frequency/first-appearance rule. Other named nominees are preserved even when a group has more than three people.

## Source integrity

All 100 source files parsed as JSON and contained the required nomination fields and one to three structured nominees. No malformed source records were found. Sources were left untouched. No scientific ranking or winner analysis was performed.
