## Biography

Kröll did his PhD at Lund under Sune Svanberg on laser spectroscopy of free atoms (OpenAlex lists a 1986 dissertation), including a year in John Hall's lab in Boulder. As a postdoc with Marcus Aldén in Lund he modelled nonlinear optics (CARS). He was then a visiting scientist at SRI International, working on coherent light–matter interaction in rare-earth-doped crystals. He became full professor of atomic physics at Lund in 1999 and led the Quantum Information Group; the group page now calls him "Former Group Leader". His management roles include head of Lund's Department of Physics and membership of the LTH faculty board. He is also on the executive management team of the Wallenberg Centre for Quantum Technology (WACQT). He was elected to the KVA Class for Physics on 16 January 2019.

## Research / intellectual footprint

- **Early work (1980s):** High-resolution laser spectroscopy of atoms: hyperfine structure, radiative lifetimes, optogalvanic methods. After that, nonlinear-optics modelling for CARS.
- **Rare-earth-ion-doped crystals (1990s–2000s):** Spectral hole burning, photon echoes and optical data storage, which then led into quantum information. Key papers include "Quantum computer hardware based on rare-earth-ion-doped inorganic crystals" (Opt. Commun. 2002), the CRIB quantum-memory proposal (PRA 2006), atomic-frequency-comb memory with spin-wave storage (PRL 2010) and efficient quantum memory (PRL 2013). He co-authored the widely cited "Quantum memories" review (EPJ D 2010).
- **Applied turn (2010s–present):** He uses slow light in spectral-hole-burning crystals as narrowband filters for ultrasound optical tomography (deep-tissue imaging for breast cancer, stroke and neonatal asphyxia). This led to the spin-off Deep Light Vision (2022). Recent papers (2024–2025) cover laser stabilization with slow light, UOT imaging models and a fiber-based quantum repeater analysis.
- **Method:** He is primarily an experimentalist who combines lab work with modelling. He has consistently moved from fundamental quantum optics toward devices and applications.

## Public statements and values

- When he was appointed to lead quantum sensing in WACQT, he said: "One vision is that quantum sensors will play a major role in the field of medicine... But completely other uses for quantum sensors can also open up, fields of use which we cannot imagine today." (Chalmers/Cision)
- On measurement (LU news, in Swedish): "Människans kunskap om världen och våra tekniska framsteg begränsas av vad vi kan mäta, och hur noggrant." ("Human knowledge of the world and our technological progress are limited by what we can measure, and how precisely.") In the same piece he describes gravimetry uses such as finding mineral deposits or giving early warning of earthquakes.
