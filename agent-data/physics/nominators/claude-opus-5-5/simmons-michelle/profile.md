## Biography

Simmons was born in London on 14 July 1967. She studied physics and chemistry of materials at Durham University and received a PhD there in 1992 on CdTe solar-cell structures. From 1992 to 1998 she was a research fellow with Michael Pepper at the Cavendish Laboratory, Cambridge. In 1999 she moved to UNSW on an ARC QEII Fellowship and became a founding member of the ARC Centre of Excellence for Quantum Computer Technology, which she later directed. She has been Scientia Professor since 2009 and ARC Laureate Fellow since 2013. She founded Silicon Quantum Computing in 2017. Her honours include:
- Pawsey Medal (2005)
- Thomas Ranken Lyle Medal and Feynman Prize in Nanotechnology (2015)
- L'Oréal-UNESCO Laureate (2017)
- Australian of the Year (2018)
- Bakerian Medal (2022)
- Prime Minister's Prize for Science (2023)
- Companion of the Order of Australia (AC, 2026)

She is FRS (2018), FAA, FTSE, and a Fellow of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences.

## Research footprint

At Cambridge she worked on low-dimensional semiconductor transport, including the "0.7 structure" and hole transistors. At UNSW she founded the field sometimes called atomic electronics: scanning-tunnelling-microscope hydrogen lithography to place individual phosphorus donors in silicon with atomic precision. Landmarks include:
- the narrowest conducting doped wires in silicon (Weber et al., "Ohm's law survives to the atomic scale," *Science* 335, 2012)
- a deterministic single-atom transistor (Fuechsle et al., *Nature Nanotechnology* 7, 2012)
- a fast two-qubit gate between phosphorus-donor electrons (He et al., *Nature* 571, 2019)

Her focus has shifted from materials and transport physics toward engineering scalable donor-based quantum processors, and increasingly toward commercialising them through SQC. She was the inaugural editor-in-chief of *npj Quantum Information*.

## Public statements and values

In her 2017 Australia Day address she stressed high expectations in education. Her UNSW profile quotes her: "We must set the bar high and tell students we expect them to jump over it." She delivered the 2023 Boyer Lectures, "The Atomic Revolution" (ABC). She has spoken publicly (TEDx Sydney 2012) about building quantum computers in silicon.
