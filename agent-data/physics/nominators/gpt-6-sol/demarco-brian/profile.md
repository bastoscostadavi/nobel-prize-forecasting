## Biography

DeMarco received a BA in physics, summa cum laude, from SUNY Geneseo (1996). He earned a PhD at the University of Colorado Boulder (2001), where he and Deborah Jin created the first quantum-degenerate Fermi gas of atoms (DeMarco & Jin, *Science* 1999). *Science* named this one of the top ten discoveries of 1999, and it earned him the first JILA Scientific Achievement Award and the APS DAMOP Thesis Award (2002). From 2001 to 2003 he was an NRC postdoc with David Wineland's Ion Storage Group at NIST Boulder, working on trapped-ion quantum logic. He joined Illinois in 2003. He has held an NSF CAREER award, an ONR Young Investigator award and a Sloan Fellowship. He served on the NRC Committee on AMO Science and in the Defense Sciences Study Group (2016–17), and he chairs the NASA Fundamental Physical Sciences Standing Review Board (UIUC page).

## Research footprint

DeMarco's Illinois group uses ultracold atoms in optical lattices as quantum simulators for condensed-matter problems. The UIUC page lists these firsts from the group: the crossover between quantum tunnelling and thermal activation of phase slips (*Nature*); three-dimensional Anderson localization of matter (Kondov et al., *Science* 2011); an early observation of many-body localization; and realisations of the disordered Bose- and Fermi-Hubbard models. Other work covers thermometry and cooling in strongly correlated lattice gases, quantum quenches of disorder across the Bose-glass–superfluid transition (with David Ceperley), and disorder-controlled relaxation in a 3D Hubbard simulator (with Ana Maria Rey and Rahul Nandkishore, 2021). His recent output includes control hardware for AMO experiments (Entangleware Sequencer, 2023) and national QIS roadmapping. Over time his role has moved from Fermi gas pioneer, to disorder physics, to leadership of Illinois's quantum centre.
