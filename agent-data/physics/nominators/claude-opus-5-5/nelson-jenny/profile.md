## Biography

Nelson studied at the University of Cambridge (BA) and earned her Ph.D. at the University of Bristol in 1988 under Michael Berry, with a thesis on the optics of fractal clusters. She held Greenpeace research fellowships and an EPSRC Advanced Research Fellowship (1997–2003) before becoming Professor of Physics at Imperial. Wikipedia says she has headed the climate-change-mitigation team at the Grantham Institute and held a Sêr Cymru joint chair at Swansea University's SPECIFIC centre. Her honours include the IOP James Joule Medal (2009), the Armourers and Brasiers' Company Prize (2012), a Royal Society Industry Fellowship (2010–2013), Fellowship of the Royal Society (2014) and the IOP Faraday Medal (2016). She wrote the textbook *The Physics of Solar Cells*.

## Research footprint

Her Royal Society citation credits her with "fundamental physical models, simulation tools and experiments" linking photovoltaic device performance to material properties across several material classes: quantum semiconductor heterostructures (quantum-well solar cells, with Keith Barnham), nanocrystalline oxides, conjugated molecular materials and hybrid organic-inorganic materials. Since about 2000 her main focus has been organic photovoltaics. Notable papers include the regioregularity effect in polythiophene:fullerene cells (2006), morphology evolution in polymer:fullerene blends (Nature Materials 2008), degradation of organic cells under air exposure, "Factors limiting device efficiency in organic photovoltaics" (2013) and a single-junction organic cell above 19% efficiency (Nature Materials 2022, as co-author). In perovskites she has worked on hydration, ion migration and methylammonium dynamics (Nature Communications 2015–2016), and her group co-developed the open-source drift-diffusion code Driftfusion. Recent work covers charge-transfer states in low-offset organic cells and ion transport in organic electrochemical transistors. Since 2010 she has also studied how photovoltaics can reduce CO2 emissions, including an expert-elicitation study on electrolysis costs (2017, co-author).
