# Physics nomination merge notes: claude-opus-5-5, run 3

Consolidated the 100 source nomination JSON files into 46 candidate groups using only those records and `prompts/physics_merge_v1.md`. Every source is assigned once. Scientist frequencies use each record's explicit `nominees` list; names mentioned only in narrative motivations are not additional nominees. Ties use first appearance in lexicographic source-file order, and within a file its nominee order. All one-off achievements are retained.

## Ambiguous merge and split decisions

- **Topological insulators (c04), magnetic quantum anomalous Hall effect (c40), fractional quantum anomalous Hall effect (c30), and the combined nomination (c44):** Merged quantum spin Hall and broader two-/three-dimensional topological-insulator nominations because they share the time-reversal-invariant classification and its realizations. Kept `samuelson-lars.json` as its own compound candidate c44, preserving the explicitly bundled topological-insulator/QSH and magnetic quantum anomalous Hall discoveries and all three credited scientists. The compound policy requires retaining the complete bundle instead of assigning it to a narrower group or duplicating the source. Kept `wang-yayu.json` separate as c40 because its subject is the magnetic zero-field Hall realization and theory credited to Xue, Fang and Dai. Kept `bergholtz-emil.json` separate as c30 because fractional zero-field topological order in moiré/graphene platforms is a distinct achievement with different credited experimenters.
- **Anyons (c08):** Merged `viefers-susanne.json` with `hansson-thors-hans.json` and `stern-ady.json`. Viefers emphasizes the original fractional-statistics prediction while the other records emphasize fractional-charge measurements and anyonic exchange/braiding experiments, but all concern establishing anyons, and all explicitly include Manfra's experimental confirmation. Preserved Leinaas and Myrheim in `other_names` after applying frequency counting; no theoretical credit was discarded.
- **Atomic quantum platforms (c02, c05, c18):** Kept optical-lattice Hubbard simulation, Rydberg blockade/tweezer arrays, and trapped-ion computation/simulation separate. Their motivations sometimes mention the other platforms, and Cirac, Zoller, Bloch or Lukin recur, but the physical architectures and central achievements differ. Optical-lattice nominations covering the Mott transition and subsequent quantum gas microscopy were merged as one platform achievement.
- **Quantum key distribution (c09):** Merged `lu-chaoyang.json`, emphasizing Pan's large-scale implementation, with the Bennett/Brassard/Ekert nominations. All explicitly nominate quantum key distribution and its communication applications; later teleportation and network demonstrations in the motivations extend the same program. Pan remains in `other_names`, while Ekert has the greater explicit nomination frequency.
- **Magic-angle graphene (c01):** Merged `ghosh-arindam.json`, which includes Eva Y. Andrei's scanning-probe contributions, with the magic-angle flat-band/correlated-insulator/superconductivity nominations. Its central discovery is the same; Andrei is retained in `other_names`. References to later fractional quantum anomalous Hall states in these motivations do not turn c01 into c30.
- **Single defect spins (c15):** Merged the broad defect-spin sensing/network nomination in `atature-mete.json` with the NV-center nanoscale-sensing nomination in `awschalom-david.json`. Both identify optical readout/coherent single-spin control and its sensing use as the achievement. Broader material/network examples do not establish a separate nominated discovery. Awschalom and Walsworth are both retained through the prescribed frequency/tie ordering.
- **Laser-plasma wakefields (c14):** Merged `marklund-mattias.json` with `strickland-donna.json`. The former also describes Joshi's beam-driven and beat-wave work, but its nomination and common theoretical origin focus on plasma-wakefield acceleration initiated by lasers. Joshi and Leemans are retained; their equal frequency is resolved by source-file order.
- **Tunnel magnetoresistance (c19):** Merged the Parkin- and Yuasa-credit variants because both nominate the sequence from room-temperature tunneling magnetoresistance to symmetry-filtered giant TMR in MgO barriers. The scientist substitution alone is not a reason to split. Parkin precedes Yuasa under the stated tie rule.
- **Compound one-offs:** `babaev-egor.json` deliberately combines MgB2 and iron-based multiband superconductivity and is retained intact as c36 rather than being assigned twice or having half its discovery omitted. Likewise, `polman-albert.json` combines metamaterials/metasurfaces/plasmonics (c35), and `steinberg-aephraim.json` combines the Aharonov-Bohm and Berry phases (c20). These are their own source-defined bundles; they are not merged into other records solely because of broad connections. Photonic crystals (c16) remain separate from the subwavelength optics bundle.
- **Cosmological theory and observations (c07, c17, c23):** Kept inflationary theory, WMAP precision mapping, and galaxy BAO measurements separate even though the source motivations link them. Their discoveries and explicitly credited scientists differ.

## Name normalizations affecting counting

Every differing source spelling used below is explicitly recorded in the top-level `name_normalizations` object.

- `J. Ignacio Cirac` in `lukin-mikhail.json` was normalized to `Juan Ignacio Cirac`, supported by the matching Max Planck affiliation and Cirac/Zoller optical-lattice proposal described in `lewenstein-maciej.json`. This gives Cirac two explicit appearances in c02.
- `Mark Kasevich` in `lykken-joseph.json` was normalized to `Mark A. Kasevich`, as in `bagnato-vanderlei.json`: both identify the Stanford scientist's light-pulse atom interferometry. This gives Kasevich two appearances in c10.
- `Lyman A. Page` in `riess-adam.json` was normalized to `Lyman A. Page Jr.`, as in `goobar-ariel.json`: both identify the Princeton WMAP instrument architect. This gives Page two appearances in c17.
- No other source nominee spellings were combined. In particular, Charles H. Bennett (quantum key distribution) and Charles L. Bennett (WMAP) remain distinct. Alexander Polyakov and Alexander M. Polyakov occur in separate achievements, so no within-group count depends on normalizing that variant.

## Source-record integrity

All 100 records parsed as JSON and supplied their required nomination fields. No malformed source record was encountered. Affiliation wording and narrative scientific assertions were not externally checked or changed.
