## Biography

Eriksson was born in 1960 and received his PhD from Uppsala University in 1989 under Börje Johansson. He held a postdoctoral position at Los Alamos National Laboratory from 1989 to 1991 and later returned there for another research period. At Uppsala he became docent in 1993, professor in 1999 and professor of theoretical magnetism in 2002. He has led the Materials Theory division at the Ångström Laboratory. He was elected to the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences, Class for Physics, in 2010. His reported honours include the Wallmark Prize, the Göran Gustafsson Prize in Physics, the Björkén Prize, an honorary doctorate from KTH, the Celsius Medal and Uppsala University's Rudbeck Medal. He chaired the Nobel Committee for the 2025 physics prize and remains a member in 2026.

## Research / intellectual footprint

- Eriksson's work uses first-principles electronic-structure calculations to study magnetism, chemical bonding, optical properties, mechanical properties and phase stability in solids and surfaces.
- Methods named on his Uppsala profile include density functional theory, dynamical mean-field theory, self-interaction corrections, Monte Carlo simulation, lattice dynamics and atomistic spin-dynamics simulation [S3].
- His publication record includes orbital polarization, elastic constants, dilute magnetic semiconductors, two-dimensional materials, magnetic damping and ultrafast magnetisation dynamics.
- He co-authored *Full-Potential Electronic Structure Method* and *Atomistic Spin Dynamics: Foundations and Applications*, as well as a multi-code study of reproducibility in solid-state DFT calculations.
- Current institutional projects connect materials theory to magnetic materials, correlated electrons, spectroscopy and research facilities including MAX IV and ESS.
