## Biography

Born 27 December 1971 in Sobernheim, Germany, he studied physics at Johannes Gutenberg University Mainz. He took his diploma in 1997 and his doctorate in 2000, with research stays at Pacific Northwest National Laboratory. His career since then (Wikipedia):
- postdoc at GSI Darmstadt with H.-Jürgen Kluge, 2000–2002;
- CERN Fellow and ISOLTRAP project leader at ISOLDE, until 2004;
- Helmholtz Young Investigator group leader in Mainz, 2004, with habilitation in 2006;
- MPIK director since October 2007;
- honorary professor at Heidelberg since 2008;
- Vice President of the Max Planck Society, 2020–2023.

His honours include the DPG Gustav Hertz Prize (2004), two ERC Advanced Grants (2011, 2019), the EPS Lise Meitner Prize (2020) and the Otto Hahn Prize (2021). He also received the DPG Stern–Gerlach Medal (2025) and the Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz Prize (2026). He is a member of the Leopoldina (2024) and the Heidelberg Academy (2022).

## Research footprint

He started in mass spectrometry of exotic nuclides at ISOLTRAP/ISOLDE and SHIPTRAP/GSI. This produced *Science* 337, 1207 (2012) on nuclear shell effects in the heaviest elements, and *Nature* 498, 346 (2013) on exotic calcium masses constraining nuclear forces.

At MPIK he pushed Penning-trap precision to new levels:
- g-factor measurements of hydrogen-like ions (²⁸Si¹³⁺, *PRL* 2011), testing bound-state QED;
- the atomic mass of the electron (Sturm et al., *Nature* 506, 467, 2014);
- the first spin-flip observation (2011) and direct high-precision measurement (Mooser et al., *Nature* 509, 596, 2014) of the magnetic moment of a single trapped proton, in Mainz collaborations.

His current work includes:
- precision tests of fundamental interactions (ERC "FunI");
- nuclear-structure masses near ¹⁰⁰Sn;
- nuclear astrophysics on storage rings;
- molecular-ion physics at the Cryogenic Storage Ring;
- novel trap technology, such as permanent-magnet cryogenic Penning traps (arXiv 2026).
