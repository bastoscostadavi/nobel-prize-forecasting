## Biography

Alejandro Frank Hoeflich was born on 4 August 1951 in Monterrey, Nuevo León. He completed his licenciatura, master's and doctorate (nuclear physics) at UNAM's Facultad de Ciencias. He joined UNAM's Instituto de Ciencias Nucleares in 1979, became investigador titular "C" in 1987, and directed the institute from 2004 to 2012. He had sabbaticals at Brookhaven, Yale and the University of Seville and made repeated visits to GANIL (Caen). His honours include:

- a Guggenheim Fellowship (1991–92);
- the Marcos Moshinsky Medal (1996);
- APS Fellowship (1998);
- the Premio Universidad Nacional (2001);
- Mexico's Premio Nacional de Ciencias y Artes (2004).

He entered El Colegio Nacional in 2016. He founded UNAM's Programa Adopte un Talento (PAUTA) and the Centro de Ciencias de la Complejidad, and has presided over the Fundación Marcos Moshinsky.

## Research footprint

Frank is known for algebraic and group-theoretical methods in nuclear and molecular structure. He co-authored the textbooks *Algebraic Methods in Molecular and Nuclear Structure Physics* (1994, with Piet Van Isacker) and *Symmetries in Atomic Nuclei: From Isospin to Supersymmetry* (2008, with Van Isacker and Jan Jolie). Spanish Wikipedia calls him one of the creators of algebraic scattering theory. El Colegio Nacional credits him and collaborators with novel predictions in nuclear supersymmetry, and his arXiv papers with Roelof Bijker and José Barea (2003–2009) cover new tests and extensions of it. He has also worked on quantum mechanics, optics, stochastic matrices, shape coexistence, phase transitions and chaos in nuclei. Since 2012 he has shifted toward complexity science, working with physicians, biologists and mathematicians on early-warning signals and time series in biological systems. He has published over 200 papers.
