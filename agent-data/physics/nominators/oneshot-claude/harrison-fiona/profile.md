## Biography

Harrison was born in Santa Monica, California, and grew up in Boulder, Colorado. She graduated from Dartmouth College in 1985 with high honours in physics and earned her PhD at UC Berkeley in 1993. She went to Caltech as a Millikan Fellow and joined its faculty in 1995. She became full professor in 2005 and later held a named Rosen professorship. She also leads the Division of Physics, Mathematics and Astronomy as the Kent and Joyce Kresa Leadership Chair. Harrison was principal investigator of NASA's NuSTAR mission, launched in 2012. She co-chaired the National Academies' Astro2020 decadal survey with Robert Kennicutt. Her honours include:
- the Presidential Early Career Award (2000);
- a NASA Outstanding Public Leadership Medal (2013);
- the AAS Bruno Rossi Prize (2015);
- the APS Hans A. Bethe Prize (2020);
- the Mohler Prize (2022).

She is a member of the NAS and a fellow of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences and the APS (Wikipedia; Caltech).

## Research footprint

Harrison combines instrument building with high-energy observational astrophysics. As a young researcher she worked on gamma-ray burst afterglows. She was first author of the 1999 ApJ Letter on GRB 990510, which reported achromatic breaks in afterglow light curves as evidence of collimated (jetted) outflows. She conceived NuSTAR, the first focusing telescope in orbit to operate in the hard X-ray band (3–79 keV). Its focal-plane detectors and electronics were built in her Caltech labs. NuSTAR results she led or enabled include:
- mapping radioactive titanium-44 in Cassiopeia A to constrain core-collapse asymmetries;
- measuring black-hole spins;
- discovering a magnetar near the Galactic Centre;
- identifying ultraluminous X-ray sources powered by pulsars.

The Bethe Prize citation honoured "pioneering work in conceiving and executing the first focusing telescope in the high-energy X-ray regime." Recently she has co-authored papers on the proposed UVEX ultraviolet explorer and on AGN, ULXs and nearby galaxies (2025–2026 arXiv).
