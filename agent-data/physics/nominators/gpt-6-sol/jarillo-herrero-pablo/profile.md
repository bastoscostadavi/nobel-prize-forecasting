## Biography

Jarillo-Herrero was born in Valencia, Spain, on 11 June 1976 (Wikipedia). He earned a Licenciatura in physics from the University of Valencia (1999) and an MSc from UC San Diego (2001). He completed his PhD at Delft University of Technology in 2005 under Leo Kouwenhoven, with a thesis on quantum transport in carbon nanotubes. After a Delft postdoc, he was a NanoResearch Initiative Fellow at Columbia University from 2006. He joined MIT as assistant professor in January 2008, received tenure in 2015 and became full professor in 2018 (MIT). His honours include:

- the APS Oliver E. Buckley Prize (2020) and the Wolf Prize in Physics (2020);
- the NAS Award for Scientific Discovery (2021) and the Max Planck–Humboldt Research Award (2021);
- the BBVA Frontiers of Knowledge Award in Basic Sciences (2025) and the Kavli Prize in Nanoscience (2026);
- Physics World Breakthrough of the Year (2018).

He was elected a foreign member of the US National Academy of Sciences (2022) and of the Spanish Royal Academy of Sciences (2023).

## Research footprint

His doctoral work on carbon nanotubes produced results such as the orbital Kondo effect (Nature 2005) and supercurrent transistors (Nature 2006). At Columbia and MIT he moved to graphene, working on bipolar supercurrents, p–n–p junction quantum Hall transport, hot-carrier photoresponse (Science 2011), Hofstadter's butterfly in graphene/hBN heterostructures (Science 2013) and 2D magnetism in CrI₃ (Science and Nature Nanotechnology 2018). In 2018 his group reported correlated insulating states and superconductivity in "magic-angle" twisted bilayer graphene, whose flat bands had been predicted by Bistritzer and MacDonald (Wikipedia). This launched the field of twistronics. Since then his group has explored moiré systems for superconductivity, magnetism, topology and ferroelectricity. Watanabe and Taniguchi's hBN crystals are a constant enabling ingredient.
