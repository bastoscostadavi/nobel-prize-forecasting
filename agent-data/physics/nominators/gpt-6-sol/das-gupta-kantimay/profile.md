## Biography

Das Gupta was a Research Associate in the Semiconductor Physics group of the Cavendish Laboratory, University of Cambridge, from 2002 to 2011 (ORCID). He joined the Department of Physics at IIT Bombay in 2011 and is now a Professor there (IITB page). At IITB he has taught quantum devices, condensed matter physics, semiconductor physics, electromagnetism and analytical techniques. ## Research footprint

At Cambridge, working with David Ritchie, Michael Pepper, Harvey Beere and Ian Farrer, he helped develop undoped GaAs/AlGaAs heterostructures. These use gates, rather than modulation doping, to induce closely spaced, density-matched electron and hole layers. That work produced:
- "Anomalous Coulomb Drag in Electron-Hole Bilayers" (Croxall, Das Gupta et al., *PRL* 101, 246801, 2008; ~120 Crossref citations), reporting an upturn in drag at low temperature relevant to proposed excitonic condensation
- studies of quantum Hall edge-state breakdown in bilayers
- patterned back-gating techniques
- very shallow undoped heterostructures for impurity diagnostics
- ambipolar high-mobility transistors and quantum point contacts (2013–2016)

At IIT Bombay his group has broadened to:
- magnetotransport in GaN and InN epitaxial films, including evidence of a thin superconducting layer in InN (2019)
- focused-ion-beam tungsten-carbide superconducting patterns
- noise spectroscopy of ionic-liquid-gated channels
- thermoelectric properties of MoS2
- Coulomb-blockade spectroscopy of quantum dots
- epitaxial NbN thin films (2026)

It has kept a Cambridge collaboration on GaAs devices.
