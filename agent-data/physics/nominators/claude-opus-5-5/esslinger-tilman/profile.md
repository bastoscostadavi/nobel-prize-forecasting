## Biography

German experimental physicist. He received his Ph.D. in 1995 from LMU Munich and the Max Planck Institute of Quantum Optics, supervised by Theodor Hänsch, working on subrecoil laser cooling and optical lattices. He then led his own group in Hänsch's laboratory and habilitated. He became full professor at ETH Zurich in October 2001. His honours include the Philip Morris Research Prize (2000, with Hänsch and Immanuel Bloch), ERC Advanced Grants (2010, 2017), APS Fellowship (2014), the Senior BEC Award (2021, with Rudolf Grimm), an honorary doctorate from Heriot-Watt University (2022), an SNSF Advanced Grant (2022), and the Micius Quantum Prize (2025, with Bloch and Markus Greiner) "for the pioneering experimental realization of bosonic and fermionic Hubbard models in optical lattices as analog quantum simulators" (Wikipedia).

## Research footprint

In Munich he worked on atom lasers and phase coherence of Bose–Einstein condensates, and co-authored the superfluid-to-Mott-insulator transition experiment (Greiner, Mandel, Esslinger, Hänsch & Bloch, Nature 2002). At ETH his group was the first to realise the Fermi–Hubbard model with ultracold atoms and observed a fermionic Mott insulator (Jördens et al., Nature 2008), followed by short-range quantum magnetism. It merged quantum gases with cavity QED and observed the Dicke superradiant phase transition (Baumann et al., Nature 2010), and later a supersolid phase with Higgs and Goldstone modes. It realised the topological Haldane model (Jotzu et al., Nature 2014), created cold-atom analogues of mesoscopic conductors (thermoelectric effect, quantised conductance of neutral matter), used Floquet engineering for gauge fields, and ran topological pumps. Recent work uses dynamical lattices for massively parallel two-qubit SWAP gates. He has 140+ papers and more than 45,000 citations (Wikipedia).
