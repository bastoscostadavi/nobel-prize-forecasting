## Biography

Balents was born in Somerville, New Jersey. He earned bachelor's degrees in physics and mathematics at MIT and a PhD in physics at Harvard (1994). After postdoctoral training he was a member of technical staff at Bell Laboratories, and he joined UC Santa Barbara in 1999 (UCSB page). He is a permanent member of KITP, holds the Yzurdiaga Chair, and co-directs the Quantum Materials program of the Canadian Institute for Advanced Research (CIFAR). He is an APS Fellow. He was elected to the American Academy of Arts and Sciences in 2018 (KITP news) and to the National Academy of Sciences in 2019 (UCSB News). On his NAS election he said he owed "much of my scientific success to the great environment here, especially the Kavli Institute for Theoretical Physics."

## Research footprint

The UCSB page says Balents is known for "charting new directions for experiments in quantum materials." He pioneered the theory of Coulomb-interaction effects in quantum wires and carbon nanotubes. With Joel Moore he formulated three-dimensional topological insulators (2007), the first bulk materials with topologically protected surface states. He later helped establish Weyl semimetals, for example through the Burkov–Balents multilayer proposal (2011). He co-authored the deconfined quantum criticality proposal (2004). He is a major figure in quantum spin liquid theory: he wrote an influential *Nature* review on spin liquids in frustrated magnets (2010) and predicted spin-liquid behaviour in materials such as spin-ice pyrochlores. His group also works on topological spintronics, 2D van der Waals and moiré materials, and ultrafast non-equilibrium probes of quantum materials (UCSB page). The common thread is materials-driven theory that anticipates new experimental phases.
