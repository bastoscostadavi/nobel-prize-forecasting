## Biography

Bengt Johan Åkerman was born on 20 March 1970. He studied engineering physics at Lund University and at EPFL in Lausanne, then earned a PhD in materials physics at KTH. In 1999 he became a postdoc at UC San Diego. From 2001 he worked at Motorola and Freescale Semiconductor in Phoenix, where he helped develop the first commercially available MRAM and was responsible for its reliability measurements and statistical models. He returned to KTH in 2005 with an SSF Future Research Leaders grant and was appointed professor at Gothenburg in 2008. He also holds a guest professorship at KTH. His honours include:
- membership of the Royal Swedish Academy of Engineering Sciences (IVA) since 2019;
- the Göran Gustafsson Prize in Physics (2014).

(All from sv.wikipedia.)

## Research footprint

Åkerman's group works on magnetization dynamics driven by spin currents, especially spin-torque and spin Hall nano-oscillators. Key results:

- **Magnetic droplet solitons (2013).** The group reported spin-torque-generated magnetic droplet solitons in nano-contact oscillators, the first experimental creation of such droplets (sv.wikipedia; see also the invited JAP 2014 review with M. Hoefer).
- **Synchronization of spin Hall nano-oscillators.** A 2016 *Nature Physics* paper showed long-range mutual synchronization. A 2019 *Nature Nanotechnology* paper showed two-dimensional synchronized arrays for neuromorphic computing.
- **Recent work.** Oscillators driven by bulk spin-orbit torque (PtBi alloys), spin-orbit torques in van der Waals magnets above room temperature, and direct imaging of propagating spin waves (2026, *Advanced Materials*, with S. Wintz).

His focus has moved from MRAM reliability in industry to fundamental nonlinear magnetization dynamics, and then to oscillator arrays for unconventional computing.
