## Biography

Adiel (Ady) Stern was born in 1960 and raised in Beersheba, and lives in Tel Aviv. He studied at Tel Aviv University for a BSc in mathematics, computer science and physics and a PhD in physics. He then spent three years as a Junior Fellow of the Harvard Society of Fellows before joining the faculty of the Weizmann Institute of Science, where he is a professor of condensed matter physics. He is a Fellow of the American Physical Society. In 2025 he received the Institute of Physics Simon Memorial Prize (Low Temperature Group) for "original and influential theoretical work on the quantum Hall effect, quantum statistics of emerging quasi-particles, topological order and decoherence in condensed matter systems at low temperatures" (Wikipedia; Israel Academy CV).

## Research footprint

Stern's early work addressed decoherence and dephasing of electrons in mesoscopic systems, including a widely cited analysis with Yakir Aharonov and Yoseph Imry of how interference is lost through interaction with an environment. He is best known for theory of the fractional quantum Hall effect: composite fermions and the half-filled Landau level, quasiparticle charge and statistics, and interferometric proposals (with Bertrand Halperin and others) to detect non-Abelian anyons in the ν = 5/2 state. He co-authored the 2008 Reviews of Modern Physics review "Non-Abelian anyons and topological quantum computation", and worked with Moty Heiblum's group on the observation of quarter-charge quasiparticles. Later interests include topological superconductivity and Majorana modes, Floquet and interacting topological phases, and moiré and graphene systems. Recent arXiv papers (2025–2026) cover fractional quantum anomalous Hall states in rhombohedral graphene, braiding in even-denominator states, quantum geometry, fractional excitonic insulators and the quantum twisting microscope.
