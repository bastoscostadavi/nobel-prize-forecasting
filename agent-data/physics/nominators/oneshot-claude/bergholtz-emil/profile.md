## Biography

His full name is Carl Erik Emil Johansson Bergholtz. He is Professor of Theoretical Physics at Stockholm University. He spent eight years abroad, first as a Distinguished PKS Fellow at the Max Planck Institute for the Physics of Complex Systems in Dresden and then as an Emmy Noether group leader at the Free University of Berlin. He returned to Stockholm in 2016 as a Wallenberg Academy Fellow and became full professor in 2019. He was appointed Wallenberg Scholar in 2024. In 2022 the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences awarded him the Göran Gustafsson Prize in Physics "for his innovative research on topological phases and quantum materials" (SU profile). He co-leads QUANTA, a quantum-technology strategic research area with Linköping and Uppsala universities. According to his SU profile, the Swedish Research Council recommended QUANTA for government funding in 2026.

## Research footprint

Bergholtz works on collective quantum phenomena in which topology, entanglement and interactions dominate. His main areas are:
- fractional quantum Hall states, including early work on thin-torus limits;
- topological flat-band models and fractional Chern insulators, now studied in moiré materials (review with Zhao Liu);
- frustrated magnets and Weyl semimetals;
- non-Hermitian topology. The 2021 *Reviews of Modern Physics* article "Exceptional topology of non-Hermitian systems", written with Jan Carl Budich and Flore Kunst, is widely cited in this area. Work with Kunst and others on biorthogonal bulk–boundary correspondence is also part of this line.

His methods mix analytical field theory, strong-coupling expansions and exact diagonalization, with tools borrowed from quantum information such as tensor networks and entanglement measures. His recent work has moved towards quantum technologies: quantum sensing, robust moiré platforms, and dissipative or measurement-based dynamics.

## Public statements and values

From his SU profile: "While such discoveries may eventually inspire new technologies, my primary motivation is to understand the underlying principles." He says he aims to keep "theory ... closely linked to experiment while retaining its creative and exploratory freedom". He also wants to cultivate an environment where "bold ideas are encouraged" and "interdisciplinary connections thrive". The same profile says his work draws on "mathematics, high-energy physics, materials science, photonics, cold atoms, and complex systems".
