## Biography

Born in 1976; German. He studied at RWTH Aachen and earned his Ph.D. at Caltech (2004) in Kerry Vahala's group. He led an independent Max Planck research group at MPQ Garching (2005–2009) and joined EPFL as assistant professor in 2008, later becoming full professor. His honours include the EPS Fresnel Prize (2009), the Helmholtz Prize for Metrology (2009), the EFTF Young Investigator Award (2010), the ZEISS Research Award, the Swiss National Latsis Prize (2014) and the Marcel Benoist Swiss Science Prize (2025). He is a member of the Leopoldina and an international member of the US National Academy of Engineering. He has been a Clarivate Highly Cited Researcher since 2014. He co-founded the photonics company LiGenTec (CV).

## Research footprint

As a Caltech student with Vahala he observed radiation-pressure dynamical backaction amplification in toroidal microresonators (2005), showing that optical microcavities are also mechanical oscillators. At MPQ in 2006 he demonstrated radiation-pressure backaction cooling, at the same time as groups in Paris and Vienna. With Zwerger he developed the quantum theory of sideband cooling, and in 2008 he achieved resolved-sideband cooling. With Aspelmeyer and Marquardt he wrote the review "Cavity optomechanics" (RMP 2014). His group showed optomechanically induced transparency, quantum-coherent coupling, measurement below the standard quantum limit and real-time quantum feedback. In 2007 his group discovered Kerr frequency-comb generation in a monolithic microresonator (Del'Haye et al., Nature 2007). They later found dissipative Kerr solitons (Herr et al., Nature Photonics 2014), the basis of chip-scale combs for spectroscopy, LiDAR, optical clocks and telecom. Current work covers silicon-nitride photonic integrated circuits, electro-optic combs, and superconducting-circuit optomechanics.
