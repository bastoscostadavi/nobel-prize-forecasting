My prediction: Marc Melitz, Elhanan Helpman, and Gene Grossman.

The prize would recognize the modern theory of international trade: how increasing returns, innovation, and differences across firms shape trade patterns, productivity, and the distributional effects of globalization. Melitz’s firm-heterogeneity framework would be the centerpiece, alongside Helpman and Grossman’s foundational work on trade and innovation.

It’s an inherently low-confidence forecast—Nobel choices are unusually difficult to call—but this feels like a mature, prize-sized body of work.
