## Biography

Fang Zhong (方忠; family name Fang) was born in July 1970 in Wuhan, Hubei. He received his bachelor's (1991) and doctoral (1996) degrees from Huazhong University of Science and Technology, followed by visiting positions in Japan and at Oak Ridge National Laboratory. He joined IOP-CAS as a research fellow in 2003, became deputy director in 2012 and Director in 2017–2018, and was also appointed director of the Beijing National Research Center for Condensed Matter Physics (2017). Honours include the ICTP Prize (2008), the C. N. Yang Award of AAPPS (2010), APS Fellowship (2011), the Zhou Peiyuan Physics Prize (2013), election to the Chinese Academy of Sciences (2019), and the 2025 Future Science Prize in physical sciences, shared with Xi Dai and Hong Ding, "for their contributions to the computational prediction and experimental realization of topological electronic materials" (Wikipedia).

## Research footprint

Fang's work uses first-principles calculations to connect topology and band geometry to real materials. An early landmark was a 2003 Science paper with Naoto Nagaosa, Yoshinori Tokura and others linking the anomalous Hall effect to Berry-curvature "monopoles" in momentum space. With Xi Dai, Hongming Weng and collaborators he predicted a series of topological materials: the Bi₂Se₃ family of topological insulators (with Shou-Cheng Zhang's group), the quantum anomalous Hall effect in magnetically doped topological insulators, the Dirac semimetals Na₃Bi and Cd₃As₂, and the TaAs family of Weyl semimetals, whose Fermi arcs were then observed by Hong Ding's ARPES group. His team later produced large catalogues of topological materials via symmetry indicators. Recent arXiv work (2026) covers altermagnets and unconventional magnetism predicted from crystal symmetry, topological moiré bands in twisted semiconductors, axion insulators, topological ferroelectrics and band-geometry descriptors. He also oversees IOP's large facilities and its "AI plus materials science" programme as director.
