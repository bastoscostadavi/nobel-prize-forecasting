## Biography

Yoshiro Takahashi is professor of physics at Kyoto University and heads the Quantum Optics Group (量子光学研究室). The group currently includes associate professors Yosuke Takasu and Koki Ono, program-specific associate professor Kosuke Shibata, Hakubi Center associate professor Tetsushi Takano and assistant professor Shintaro Taie (group members page). The group's website invites prospective students to contact him directly. His education, career history before Kyoto and honours were not found in the accessible sources.

## Research footprint

Takahashi's group made ytterbium, a two-electron atom, a leading platform for quantum gases:

- "Spin-Singlet Bose-Einstein Condensation of Two-Electron Atoms" (Takasu et al., PRL 2003), the first ytterbium BEC. Many isotopes, bosonic and fermionic, followed.
- "An SU(6) Mott insulator of an atomic Fermi gas realized by large-spin Pomeranchuk cooling" (Taie, Yamazaki, Sugawa, Takahashi; Nature Physics 2012), which exploited SU(N) symmetry for quantum simulation of Hubbard physics.
- Current research lines (group page): quantum simulation in optical lattices, an ytterbium quantum gas microscope that images single atoms site by site, mixtures and controlled impurities in lattices, quantum simulation of the Kondo effect using orbital degrees of freedom, and Rydberg-atom quantum computing aimed at universal computation.
- Recent results listed on the group site: "Hybrid Atom Tweezer Array of Nuclear Spin and Optical Clock Qubits" (PRX 2024), "Observation of Nonlinearity of Generalized King Plot in the Search for New Boson" (PRX 2022, Featured in Physics), Hilbert-space-fragmentation dynamics in Bose–Hubbard chains (Science Advances 2025), and orders-of-magnitude improved spectroscopy of an inner-shell orbital clock transition in ytterbium (Nature Photonics 2026).

The arc runs from making new quantum-degenerate gases, through SU(N) lattice quantum simulation, to precision tests of fundamental physics and neutral-atom quantum computing.
