## Biography

Randall Gardner Hulet was born in 1956 in Walnut Creek, California. He earned a B.S. at Stanford (1978) and a Ph.D. at MIT (1984) under Daniel Kleppner. He was then a postdoc at NIST Boulder with David Wineland. He joined Rice as an assistant professor in 1987 and became full professor in 1996 and Fayez Sarofim Professor in 2000. His honours include the APS I.I. Rabi Prize (1995), the APS Davisson-Germer Prize, the Willis E. Lamb Award (2011), the Herbert Walther Award (2017), a NASA Exceptional Scientific Achievement Medal and a Chinese Academy of Sciences Distinguished Scientist Award. He is a member of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences, a Fellow of the APS, Optica and the AAAS, and an honorary doctor of Utrecht University.

## Research footprint

Hulet's early work was in Rydberg-atom cavity QED, including inhibited spontaneous emission (PRL 1985), and in single-ion physics: an observation of quantum jumps (PRL 1986) and a realization of a "weak value" measurement (1991). At Rice he became a pioneer of ultracold lithium. His 1995 PRL reported evidence of Bose-Einstein condensation in an atomic gas with attractive interactions, one of the first three BEC reports; Rice states that his group "was among the first three to achieve Bose-Einstein condensation of an atomic gas in 1995." His group then studied BEC collapse, bright matter-wave soliton trains (Nature 2002) and Fermi pressure in a trapped gas (Science 2001). His later work covered pairing and phase separation in spin-polarized Fermi gases (Science 2006), spin imbalance in 1D Fermi gases (Nature 2010) and antiferromagnetic correlations in the Fermi-Hubbard model (Nature 2015). Recent work (2022–2026) concerns Tomonaga-Luttinger liquids in 1D attractive and repulsive Fermi gases, including a spin-incoherent Luttinger liquid. He co-authored the community paper "Quantum Simulators: Architectures and Opportunities" (PRX Quantum 2021).
