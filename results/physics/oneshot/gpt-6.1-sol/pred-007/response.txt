My pick is **Sir John Pendry**, potentially sharing the prize with **David R. Smith**, for pioneering **metamaterials**—engineered structures that manipulate electromagnetic waves in ways ordinary materials cannot.

The case is strong: Pendry’s theoretical work and Smith’s experimental demonstrations helped establish negative refraction, with consequences for imaging, antennas, and wave control. The discoveries have also had decades to mature, which often matters for Nobel recognition.

My strongest alternative would be **Pablo Jarillo-Herrero, Allan MacDonald, and Rafi Bistritzer** for *magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene*: showing how rotating two graphene sheets can produce striking electronic behavior, including superconductivity.

I’d put metamaterials first, but with **low confidence**. Physics Nobel predictions are unusually uncertain because several deserving discoveries compete each year and the committee’s deliberations remain secret.
