## Biography

Bagnato was born in São Carlos, São Paulo, on 28 September 1958. From 1977 he studied physics at USP and materials engineering at UFSCar in parallel, then did a master's at USP on the optics of inorganic crystals. While already a USP São Carlos faculty member, he earned a PhD at MIT (1983–1987) under David E. Pritchard. His thesis was "Magnetic trap for laser-cooled neutral atoms." He returned to Brazil in 1988 and founded his laboratory. He directed IFSC-USP from 2018 to 2022. His honours include:
- Commander (2007) and Grand Cross (2018) of the National Order of Scientific Merit
- Almirante Álvaro Alberto Prize (2019)
- CBMM Prize (2021)
- Willis E. Lamb Award (2024)

He is a member of the Brazilian Academy of Sciences, the US National Academy of Sciences (international), TWAS, the Pontifical Academy of Sciences, and, from 2025, the US National Academy of Engineering (pt.wikipedia; Texas A&M page).

## Research footprint

Bagnato runs two parallel programs:

1. **Atomic physics.** He was an early contributor to magnetic trapping of laser-cooled atoms at MIT. He then built Brazil's cold-atom program, including the country's first Bose–Einstein condensate, and was part of the team that built Latin America's first atomic clock. His group is known for quantum turbulence in BECs, including "Emergence of turbulence in an oscillating Bose-Einstein condensate" (Henn et al., *PRL* 103, 2009).
2. **Biophotonics.** Since 1998 he has developed laser and light-based medicine and dentistry, especially photodynamic therapy for cancer and antimicrobial control. His group has also worked on optical diagnostics, and the techniques have been deployed in Brazilian clinics.

He reports about 1,000 papers, over 140 supervised theses, more than 50 products, and a São Carlos business park of 40+ companies. He also launched Brazil's first physics MOOC.
