My speculative pick is **John Pendry and David R. Smith, for pioneering metamaterials and demonstrating negative refraction**.

Metamaterials are engineered structures whose geometry gives them unusual physical properties—for example, bending electromagnetic waves in directions ordinary materials cannot. Pendry supplied key theoretical ideas, and Smith helped demonstrate them experimentally. Their work opened new approaches to lenses, antennas, and controlling light.

That looks like a plausible Nobel combination: a fundamental insight, convincing experiments, and decades of influence.

Other strong possibilities include:

- **Michael Berry and Yakir Aharonov**, for discoveries about quantum phase and its observable effects.
- **Eli Yablonovitch and Sajeev John**, for pioneering photonic crystals, which control light propagation.
- **Ignacio Cirac and Peter Zoller**, for foundational proposals in quantum computing and simulation.
