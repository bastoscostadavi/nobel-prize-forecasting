## Biography

Atatüre was born in Kayseri, Turkey, in 1975. He earned a B.Sc. at Bilkent University (1996) and a Ph.D. in the Quantum Imaging Laboratory at Boston University (2002). From 2002 to 2007 he was a postdoc in Ataç İmamoğlu's Quantum Photonics Group at ETH Zurich, where he received his venia legendi. He joined the Cavendish in 2007 as a University Lecturer and was promoted to Reader in 2011 and to Professor in 2015. He became Head of the Cavendish Laboratory in 2023. His honours include the IOP Thomas Young Medal (2020), ERC Consolidator (2013) and Advanced (2020) grants, Fellowship of Optica (2020), membership of the Turkish Academy of Sciences (2012) and membership of Academia Europaea (2021). Wikipedia describes him as co-founder and CSO of the spin-out Nu Quantum.

## Research footprint

At ETH he worked on semiconductor quantum dots. His papers from that period include deterministic coupling of single dots to nanocavity modes (Science 2005), the quantum nature of a strongly coupled dot-cavity system (Nature 2007) and near-unity spin-state preparation (Science 2006). At Cambridge he studied resonance fluorescence, producing work on subnatural-linewidth single photons (PRL 2012) and on-demand indistinguishable single photons (Nature Nanotechnology 2013). He also studied cooling and control of nuclear spin ensembles, which led to "a many-body quantum register for a spin qubit" (2025). He broadened into diamond colour centres, including the electronic structure of the silicon-vacancy centre (PRL 2014) and strain engineering, and into 2D materials: quantum-emitter arrays in atomically thin semiconductors and "atomically thin quantum light-emitting diodes" (both Nature Communications 2016). Other work covers room-temperature optically detected magnetic resonance of single defects in hBN (2021) and vector magnetometry with a single hBN spin (2024). He co-authored the review "Material platforms for spin-based photonic quantum technologies" (Nature Reviews Materials 2018). His current themes are quantum-network hardware, many-body physics in heterostructures and quantum sensing, including biosensing.

## Public statements and values

Wikipedia records that he is a prominent science communicator who has given six TEDx talks on quantum physics, light, science and diversity. It also describes him as an activist for gender equality and diversity and a well-known public scientific figure in Turkey. GQ Turkey named him a 2015 "Man of the Year" and nicknamed him "Professor Laughter". Recent titles such as "Quantum technologies with crystal defects: a tale of gemstone and light" (2026) and "How quantum biosensing is transforming healthcare" (2025) show his interest in outreach and applications.
