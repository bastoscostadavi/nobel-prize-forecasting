## Biography

Carlos Silvestre Frenk was born in Mexico City on 27 October 1951. He studied engineering and then theoretical physics at UNAM, graduating in 1976 with the Gabino Barreda Medal. He took Part III of the Mathematical Tripos at Cambridge and completed a PhD there in 1981 under Bernard Jones, on globular clusters. After postdoctoral posts at Berkeley, UC Santa Barbara and Sussex, he joined Durham in 1986 and became a professor in 1993. In 2001 he became the first Ogden Professor of Fundamental Physics and founding Director of the Institute for Computational Cosmology, which he led until 2020. His honours include FRS (2004), the RAS Gold Medal (2014), the Gruber Cosmology Prize (2011), the IOP Fred Hoyle Medal (2010), the Max Born Prize (2017), the IOP Dirac Medal (2020), the Rumford Medal (2021), a CBE (2017) and a knighthood (2026).

## Research footprint

Frenk is one of the architects of the cold dark matter (CDM) paradigm based on numerical simulation. With Davis, Efstathiou and White he wrote "The evolution of large-scale structure in a universe dominated by cold dark matter" (ApJ 1985), one of the first large CDM N-body simulation studies. Earlier work with Davis and White (1983) showed that neutrinos could not be the dark matter. With Navarro and White he derived the Navarro–Frenk–White (NFW) halo density profile, "A Universal Density Profile from Hierarchical Clustering" (ApJ 1997), one of the most cited results in the field. With White he co-founded the Virgo Consortium (1994), which produced the Millennium Simulation (Springel et al., Nature 2005). He also contributed to the EAGLE hydrodynamical simulations (Schaye et al., MNRAS 2014) and, per Wikipedia, to FLAMINGO, COLIBRE and DESI. His recent work uses simulations to test the nature of dark matter, including warm and self-interacting alternatives (inferred from general reputation).
