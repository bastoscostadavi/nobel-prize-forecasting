## Biography

Gerardus Johannes Maria Meijer was born in 1962 in Zeddam, Netherlands. He studied physics at Radboud University Nijmegen and received his PhD there in 1988 under Antoni Dymanus and Peter Andresen. After a postdoc at IBM Almaden (San Jose) with Mattanjah de Vries, working on laser desorption and fullerenes, he returned to Nijmegen. He was lecturer there and became full professor of experimental physics in 1995. He then directed the FOM Institute for Plasma Physics Rijnhuizen (2000) and the Fritz Haber Institute (from 2002). He was President of the Executive Board of Radboud University (2012–2017) and returned to his FHI directorship in 2017. His honours include:
- Bourke Award of the RSC (2009)
- van 't Hoff Prize of the Bunsen Society (2012)
- corresponding membership of the Royal Netherlands Academy (KNAW, 2004)
- membership of Academia Europaea (2013)
- honorary doctorate, University of Waterloo (2026)

## Research footprint

Meijer's group invented Stark deceleration. Time-varying electric fields slow polar molecules in a molecular beam, first shown in Bethlem, Berden and Meijer, "Decelerating neutral dipolar molecules" (*PRL* 83, 1999). The technique opened controlled production, trapping and collision studies of cold, state-selected molecules. It was followed by related methods, including trapping, storage rings, and later Zeeman and optical approaches developed elsewhere. Earlier he contributed to cavity ring-down spectroscopy and to fullerene spectroscopy and crystals. He built a program of gas-phase IR spectroscopy of clusters and biomolecules using free-electron lasers (FELIX, and later the FHI FEL). With van de Meerakker and others he pursued cold, state-resolved molecular collisions. The arc runs from laser spectroscopy and fullerenes, to cold-molecule manipulation, to precision molecular physics and FEL spectroscopy.
