## Biography

Hsu earned a B.Sc. (1999) and M.Sc. (2000) in physics at National Taiwan University. After serving as a second lieutenant in the R.O.C. Navy (2001–2003), he earned a Ph.D. at UC San Diego (2003–2008) under Frank Würthwein. He was a Chamberlain Fellow at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory (2008–2012). He joined the University of Washington as an assistant professor in 2012 and became associate professor in 2018 and full professor in 2023. From 2018 to 2023 he also held a joint associate professorship at National Tsing Hua University in Taiwan (ORCID). Since 2021 he has directed A3D3 (Accelerated AI Algorithms for Data-Driven Discovery), an NSF Harnessing the Data Revolution institute.

## Research footprint

Hsu's career runs through several collider experiments:

- **Belle (KEK), master's work:** he commissioned the Extreme Forward Calorimeter and confirmed the anomalously large B → η′K rate in early data.
- **CDF (Fermilab), Ph.D.:** he studied diboson production and searched for the Standard Model Higgs boson.
- **ATLAS:** as a Chamberlain Fellow he led Pixel data-acquisition software development, worked on inner-detector alignment and muon performance, and led the first ATLAS measurement of ZZ production. He co-convened the LHC Electroweak Working Group's diboson effort from 2011.
- **UW research:** Higgs properties, vector-boson scattering with boosted-jet substructure, and dark-matter searches with ATLAS.
- **FASER:** neutrino cross-section measurements. UW news in 2023 highlighted FASER's first detection of collider-produced neutrinos.

His focus has increasingly shifted toward hardware-accelerated machine learning (FPGAs and GPUs, the FastML community) for real-time triggering in high-energy physics, multi-messenger astrophysics and neuroscience. He collaborates with Scott Hauck's ACME Lab on the ATLAS Inner Tracker Pixel upgrade.
