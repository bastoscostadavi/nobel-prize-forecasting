## Biography

Gigan received his PhD in quantum optics from UPMC (Paris) in 2004 under Claude Fabre. He was then a postdoc at the University of Vienna in the groups of Markus Aspelmeyer and Anton Zeilinger, working on quantum optomechanics. From 2007 to 2014 he was Associate Professor at the Institut Langevin (ESPCI ParisTech). He has been Professor at Sorbonne Université since 2014, based at the Laboratoire Kastler Brossel. He is a Senior Member of the Institut Universitaire de France. His honours include the Fabry de Gramont Prize of the French Optical Society (2016), the Atos–Joseph Fourier Prize (2018), the Jean Jerphagnon Prize (2019) and the Cécile DeWitt prize of the French Academy of Sciences (2025; the LKB page writes "Cecil DeWitte Prize"). He holds ERC Starting (COMEDIA, 2011) and Consolidator (SMARTIES, 2016) grants. In 2016 he co-founded the optical-computing spin-off LightOn.

## Research footprint

As a postdoc in Vienna, Gigan was first author of "Self-cooling of a micromirror by radiation pressure" (Nature 2006), an early landmark of cavity optomechanics. He also co-authored work on optomechanical entanglement and ground-state cooling schemes. At the Institut Langevin he helped found the field of controlling light in multiply scattering media. "Measuring the transmission matrix in optics" (Popoff et al., PRL 2010) showed that the transmission matrix of an opaque medium can be measured and used to focus and transmit images through it ("Image transmission through an opaque material", Nature Communications 2010). Later highlights include single-shot imaging through scattering layers and around corners via speckle correlations (Nature Photonics 2014), the photoacoustic transmission matrix (2013), and the review "Light fields in complex media: mesoscopic scattering meets wave control" (RMP 2017). He co-authored "Inference in artificial intelligence with deep optics and photonics" (Nature 2020). The LKB page says his work has "expanded to harnessing optical disorder for high-dimensional quantum information and optical computing", with applications in neurophotonics and deep-tissue imaging.
