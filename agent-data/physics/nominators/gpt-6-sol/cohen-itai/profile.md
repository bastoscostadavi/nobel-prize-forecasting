## Biography

Cohen received a BS in physics from UCLA (1995) and a PhD in physics from the University of Chicago (2001). He was a postdoctoral associate in Physics and the Division of Engineering and Applied Sciences at Harvard from 2001 to 2005. He joined Cornell as assistant professor in 2005, became associate professor in 2011 and full professor in 2017, and now holds the Josephson Family Professorship (Cornell pages). With Melanie Dreyer-Lude he co-wrote *Finding Your Research Voice: Story Telling and Theatre Skills for Bringing Your Presentation to Life* (Springer Nature, 2020). No honours or academy memberships were found in the sources retrieved.

## Research footprint

His group, "Matter in Motion", works across several areas (group page):

- **Complex fluids.** It images particle-level structure and stresses in sheared suspensions. Examples are the microscopic structure of shear thinning and thickening (Cheng et al., *Science* 2011), tunable shear thickening, the solidification of cornstarch under impact (*Science Advances* 2020), and acoustic control of dense suspensions (arXiv 2024–25, with James Sethna and Bulbul Chakraborty).
- **Origami mechanical metamaterials.** This includes reprogrammable folded metamaterials (Silverberg et al., *Science* 2014).
- **Microscopic robots.** With Paul McEuen it built electronically integrated, mass-manufactured microscopic robots (Miskin et al., *Nature* 2020), later autonomous micromachines, and "magnetic handshake" self-assembling materials.
- **Tissue mechanics.** This includes rigidity percolation in cartilage.
- **Biolocomotion.** This includes insect flight control.

Recent theory collaborations address rigidity transitions and renormalisation-group scaling. The common thread is emergent mechanics across scales.
