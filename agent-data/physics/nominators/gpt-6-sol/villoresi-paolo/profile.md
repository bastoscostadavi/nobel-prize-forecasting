## Biography

He is a professor in the Department of Information Engineering at the University of Padua. There he leads the QuantumFuture group, whose other faculty member is Giuseppe Vallone, and directs the university's interdisciplinary QTech Center for quantum technologies. With Vallone he co-founded ThinkQuantum S.r.l. (Sarcedo, Vicenza), a company making QKD systems and quantum random number generators. Its industrial partner is Officina Stellare. His education, birth year, earlier positions and formal honours were not found in the sources consulted.

## Research footprint

He is best known for pioneering space quantum communication in Europe. With Jennewein, Aspelmeyer, Ursin, Zeilinger, Barbieri and others he demonstrated the feasibility of a quantum channel between space and Earth. The experiment used single photons reflected by a low-Earth-orbit satellite retroreflector and received at the Matera Laser Ranging Observatory (NJP 2008). The Padua group later reported experimental satellite quantum communications with polarisation qubits reflected from several orbiting satellites (Vallone et al., PRL 2015). Earlier work applied adaptive optics to quantum interferometry and entangled-state engineering with Sergienko and Bonato (2008–2012), and studied turbulence in long-range free-space links. Later directions include:

- daylight QKD at 1550 nm with integrated silicon photonics (2019)
- source-device-independent and semi-device-independent random number generators
- heralded single-photon sources and FPGA-based QKD electronics
- studies for quantum links from navigation satellites
- nonlocality tests

He co-authored the review "Advances in quantum cryptography" (Pirandola et al., 2020).
