My prediction is **Nobuhiro Kiyotaki and John Moore**, for **explaining how borrowing constraints and collateral values amplify economic fluctuations**.

Their landmark contribution is the theory of **credit cycles**: businesses borrow against assets such as land; falling asset prices reduce their borrowing capacity, forcing investment cuts and asset sales that push prices down further. This feedback can turn a modest economic shock into a deep, persistent downturn.

A plausible Nobel citation would be:

> “For their contributions to understanding the interaction between financial constraints and economic fluctuations.”

I favor this pairing because the contribution is foundational, has strongly influenced macroeconomics, and offers a clear mechanism linking financial markets to the real economy.

This is a speculative prediction, with low confidence in the exact outcome—not a claim about the eventual announcement.
