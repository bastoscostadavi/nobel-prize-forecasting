## Biography

Kamien earned a B.S. and an M.S. in physics at Caltech (1988) and a Ph.D. at Harvard (1992), where David R. Nelson supervised his thesis, "Directed Line Liquids". He was a Member of the Institute for Advanced Study (1992–1995) and a postdoctoral research associate at Penn (1995–1997). Penn appointed him assistant professor in 1997 and full professor in 2003, and he has held the Abrams chair since 2006. He was named a Simons Investigator in 2013 and received the British Liquid Crystal Society's G.W. Gray Medal in 2016. He is a Fellow of the APS and the AAAS. He also served as chief editor of *Reviews of Modern Physics*.

## Research footprint

Kamien uses differential geometry and topology to study soft matter, especially liquid-crystalline phases. His early work, with T.C. Lubensky, covered twist-grain-boundary phases, minimal surfaces and screw dislocations (PRL 1999). He also wrote on chirality ("Molecular chirality and chiral parameters", 1999; "Order and frustration in chiral liquid crystals", 2001, with J. Selinger). Other work treated entropy-driven self-assembly: "Maximizing entropy by minimizing area" (2001), "Entropically driven helix formation" (Science 2005) and a geometric theory of diblock copolymer phases. A 2002 RMP review, "The geometry of soft materials: a primer", is among his most-cited papers. Around 2010 his focus moved to focal conic domains and smectic defect topology. This period includes "The power of Poincaré" (PRL 2010), "Topological colloids" (Nature 2013, with Smalyukh and others), a Colloquium on disclination loops and point defects (RMP 2012) and a paper on smectic defect topology in *Communications in Mathematical Physics* (2019). He has also worked on mechanics and design problems: lattice kirigami (PRL 2014; PNAS 2015), inverse design of nematic elastomer sheets (PNAS 2017) and, since 2020, geometric models of knitted fabrics. His research statement stresses that "topology turns geometry into counting" and that he looks for invariants that homotopy theory alone misses.

## Public statements and values

His faculty page states that "Landau theory, broken symmetry, and Goldstone modes are the lynchpins upon which our understanding of material properties rely." It also says geometry provides "a different framework to characterize both ground states and their deformations." As an editor he co-wrote "Reviews of Modern Physics at 90" (*Physics Today*, 2019) and an RMP editorial titled "To Review Is to Be" (2023). Both point to a strong interest in review and synthesis in physics. Wikipedia notes his playful naming habits, such as "Shnerk's surface" and "Shmessel functions".
