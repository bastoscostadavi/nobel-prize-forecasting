## Biography

Mørk earned his MSc (1986) and PhD (1988) at DTU's Laboratory for Applied Mathematical Physics. His positions (ORCID):
- Tele Danmark Research, 1988–1996;
- project manager at L.M. Ericsson, 1996;
- associate research professor at DTU (Microelectronics Centre, then Department of Communications, Optics and Materials), 1996–2002;
- Professor and group leader at DTU Photonics Engineering, now Electrical and Photonics Engineering, from 2002.

He leads NanoPhoton, a Danish National Research Foundation Center of Excellence founded in April 2020 with a DKK 62 million grant. He is also affiliated with DTU's Center for Quantum Technologies. Honours and academy memberships were not found in the sources checked.

## Research footprint

His DTU research fingerprint centres on:
- quantum dots;
- photonic crystals;
- waveguides;
- semiconductor optical amplifiers;
- slow light.

**Early work.** Coming from telecom research, he studied the dynamics of semiconductor lasers and amplifiers. In the mid-2000s he developed the theory and experiments on slow and fast light in semiconductor waveguides through population pulsations.

**Fano lasers.** He proposed the photonic-crystal Fano laser (Mørk, Chen & Heuck, *PRL* 2014), a laser whose mirror is a Fano resonance, and predicted terahertz modulation and short-pulse generation. Theory of self-pulsing and in-plane Fano devices followed with Yi Yu and Kresten Yvind.

**Extreme dielectric confinement.** Through NanoPhoton he pursues "extreme dielectric confinement", meaning light localized in dielectric cavities to volumes far below the conventional diffraction-limited scale (NanoPhoton site). This work uses topology-optimized designs with Ole Sigmund and Rasmus E. Christiansen.

**Recent work (2026).** Electrically driven photonic-crystal membrane lasers, deterministic emitter integration into subwavelength nanocavities, and first-principles inverse design of nanolasers with Steven G. Johnson.

## Public statements and values

The NanoPhoton site summarizes his video presentation of the centre: novel and "highly non-intuitive" semiconductor nanostructures can concentrate light to a few nanometres, "much below what was previously considered the fundamental limit". The centre asks "How small can a photon get?" No other direct quotes were verified.
