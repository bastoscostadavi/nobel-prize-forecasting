## Biography

Per his ORCID record, Zwiller took a Master of Science at the Université de Strasbourg (to 1996) and a PhD in solid-state physics at Lund University (1996–2001). He was an assistant at EPFL (2003) and Oberassistent at ETH Zurich (2003–2005), where he worked with Ataç İmamoğlu (co-authorship). He was then assistant professor (2005–2011) and associate professor (2011–2015) at TU Delft. Since 2012 he has been Chief Science Officer of Single Quantum, a Delft company making superconducting single-photon detectors. He moved to KTH in Stockholm after 2015 (inferred from the ORCID end date and KTH affiliations on later papers). No academy memberships or major prizes were found in the sources consulted.

## Research footprint

- **Single quantum dots as photon sources.** His Lund PhD work showed antibunching from single quantum dots ("Single quantum dots emit single photons at a time", 2001).
- **Nanowire quantum dots (ETH and Delft).** Optically bright quantum dots in single nanowires (Nano Letters 2005), single-quantum-dot nanowire LEDs (2007, with Kouwenhoven), crystal-phase quantum dots (2010), bright single-photon sources in tailored nanowires (Nature Communications 2012) and strongly entangled photon pairs from nanowire quantum dots (Nature Communications 2014).
- **Other Delft work.** Quantum interference in plasmonic circuits (Nature Nanotechnology 2013) and the photothermoelectric effect in MoS2 (2013).
- **Detectors.** Superconducting nanowire single-photon detectors with high efficiency and timing resolution (2016–2017), and a widely cited SNSPD perspective (APL 2021).
- **Recent KTH work.** Hybrid integrated quantum photonic circuits (Nature Photonics 2020 review), deterministic integration of sources in silicon photonics, photon-number-resolving and mid-infrared SNSPDs, fractal SNSPDs, single-photon LIDAR, time distribution over metropolitan entanglement networks, and quantum simulation on programmable photonic processors (2026).

The trajectory runs from single-emitter physics to detector engineering and applied quantum photonics.
