## Biography

Maciej Lewenstein was born on 21 September 1955 in Warsaw. He obtained an MSc at the University of Warsaw (1978, on superradiance, with Kazimierz Rzążewski), a PhD summa cum laude at the University of Essen (1983, co-supervised by Fritz Haake and Rzążewski) and his habilitation at the Polish Academy of Sciences (1986). He held positions at the PAS Centre for Theoretical Physics, was a research associate with Roy Glauber at Harvard (1989–1992), worked at CEA Saclay (1995–1998) and was full professor at Leibniz University Hannover (1998–2005) before moving to ICFO as an ICREA professor in 2005. Honours include APS Fellowship (2004), Humboldt Research Award (2007), Hamburg Prize for Theoretical Physics (2010), Foundation for Polish Science Prize (2011), the EPS-QEOD Prize for Fundamental Research (2013), the Willis E. Lamb Award (2016) and membership of Academia Europaea (2019) (Wikipedia).

## Research footprint

Lewenstein's work spans several communities. In strong-field physics he is known for the semiclassical "Lewenstein model" of high-harmonic generation (1994, with Balcou, Ivanov, L'Huillier and Corkum), a standard theoretical tool of attosecond science. In quantum information he contributed to entanglement theory, including entanglement witnesses and separability criteria. From the late 1990s he became a leading theorist of ultracold atoms in optical lattices as quantum simulators of many-body physics, summarised in a widely used 2007 review and the 2012 Oxford monograph with Anna Sanpera and Verònica Ahufinger. Recent work (arXiv 2026) returns to strong-field physics from a quantum-optical angle: quantum states of light in high-harmonic generation, cat states and photon antibunching, electron-correlation effects in attosecond pulses and keV harmonics. It also includes lattice gauge theories on quantum computers, entanglement in semiclassical gravity models, and more speculative foundational topics.
