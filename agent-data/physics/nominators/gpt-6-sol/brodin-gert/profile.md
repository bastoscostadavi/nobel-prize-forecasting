## Biography

According to Swedish Wikipedia, which draws on Umeå University sources, he was born on 14 December 1963. He began studying mathematics and physics at Umeå University in 1983 and took his bachelor's degree in 1986. His doctorate followed four years later, with a thesis on parametric instabilities in plasmas. He spent four months at the University of Maryland and became a lecturer at Umeå in 1995. In 1999 he received the prize of the Royal Skyttean Society and an international young plasma physicist medal. He is now professor of physics at Umeå and deputy head of department (Umeå staff page). He was elected to the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences in 2012.

## Research footprint

Until about 1998 he worked mainly on wave propagation in plasmas (Umeå 2004 festschrift, via Swedish Wikipedia). He then moved to gravitational waves in plasmas and to photon–photon scattering. One noted result was a proposal to detect QED vacuum nonlinearities with microwave waveguides (Brodin, Marklund and Stenflo, *PRL* 87, 171801, 2001).

From the mid-2000s, with Mattias Marklund, he developed quantum plasma theory that includes electron spin:
- *Spin magnetohydrodynamics* (*New J. Phys.* 9, 277, 2007);
- g-factor effects in semiclassical kinetic theory (*PRL* 101, 245002, 2008);
- later, quantum kinetic theory reviewed in *Reviews of Modern Plasma Physics* (2022, with Jens Zamanian).

His recent papers with Haidar Al-Naseri and others cover:
- plasma dynamics at and beyond the Schwinger limit;
- pair creation using the Dirac–Heisenberg–Wigner formalism;
- radiation reaction in relativistic plasmas;
- anomalous conductivity from relativistic Landau quantization;
- vacuum polarization modified by plasma (*Phys. Plasmas*, 2026).

The overall arc runs from classical plasma waves to the meeting point of plasma physics, strong-field QED and relativistic quantum kinetics.

## Public statements and values

No direct quotes on prizes or science policy were found. A 2004 Umeå festschrift, quoted on Swedish Wikipedia, describes his research as "studies that are a view out into the universe." It stresses that plasma is rare on Earth but common in the cosmos and relevant to fusion and radio communication. He co-authored a 2025 obituary of plasma physicist Fernando Haas in *Physics of Plasmas*.
