## Biography

Richard Henry Friend was born in London on 18 January 1953. He was educated at Rugby School and Trinity College, Cambridge, and received his PhD in 1979 under Abe Yoffe. Wikipedia also lists Denis Jérome (Paris-Sud) as an advisor. He was Cavendish Professor of Physics at Cambridge from 1995 to 2020 and is now a Research Professor at the Cavendish. According to Wikipedia he is Tan Chin Tuan Centennial Professor at the National University of Singapore and chairs the Scientific Advisory Board of Singapore's National Research Foundation. He co-founded Cambridge Display Technology and Plastic Logic. His honours include FRS (1993), the Europhysics Prize (1996), the Rumford Medal (1998), FREng (2002), the IEE Faraday Medal and a knighthood (both 2003), the Millennium Technology Prize (2010), the Harvey Prize (2011), US National Academy of Engineering membership (2013) and the IOP Isaac Newton Medal (2024).

## Research footprint

Friend began his career on low-dimensional inorganic and organic conductors. His Royal Society citation mentions phase diagrams for charge-density waves in transition-metal dichalcogenides and TTF-TCNQ, and de Haas–van Alphen oscillations in an organic metal. He then moved to conjugated polymers. The landmark paper "Light-emitting diodes based on conjugated polymers" (Burroughes et al., Nature 1990) opened the field of polymer LEDs. "Two-dimensional charge transport in self-organized, high-mobility conjugated polymers" (Sirringhaus et al., Nature 1999) was a key result for organic transistors. His group also worked on polymer photovoltaics and directly printed transistors, and later on metal-halide perovskites. The Cavendish page describes his current focus as "spin management" in molecular semiconductors: control of singlet–triplet exchange, triplet losses in organic solar cells (Nature 2021), and luminescent radical ("spin doublet") emitters for efficient LEDs (Nature 2018, Nature 2023). His group uses ultrafast optical spectroscopy with time resolution down to 10 fs. Wikipedia reports more than 1,000 co-authored publications.
