## Biography

Awschalom was born in Baton Rouge, Louisiana, in 1956. He took a BSc in physics at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign and a PhD in experimental physics at Cornell. At the IBM Watson Research Center he was a research staff member and manager of the Nonequilibrium Physics Department. He joined UC Santa Barbara as a physics professor in 1991 and added an electrical and computer engineering appointment in 2001. At UCSB he was Peter J. Clarke Professor, director of the California NanoSystems Institute and director of the Center for Spintronics and Quantum Computation. He then moved to UChicago PME, where he is Liew Family Professor. There he is founding director of the Chicago Quantum Exchange and director of the Chicago Quantum Institute. He was also the inaugural director of the DOE's Q-NEXT center and is now its Chief Science Officer. His honours include the APS Buckley Prize (2005) and Lilienfeld Prize, the EPS Europhysics Prize (2005), the MRS Turnbull Award, the AAAS Newcomb Cleveland Prize, the IUPAP Magnetism Prize and Néel Medal, and a Falling Walls Science Breakthrough award. He belongs to the NAS (2007), the NAE (2011), the American Academy of Arts and Sciences (2006) and the European Academy of Sciences (PME page; Wikipedia).

## Research footprint

Awschalom helped found semiconductor spintronics. His early work used ultrafast optical and magnetic probes to study spin dynamics and coherence in semiconductor quantum structures. His group showed long-lived electron spin coherence in semiconductors and optically controlled spins in them. It later moved on to controlling single spins in wide-bandgap hosts. These include defects in diamond and silicon carbide, and more recently molecular spin qubits. The group now works on "quantum engineering" in the broad sense: spins of electrons, nuclei and photons used for quantum sensing, communication and networking (PME page). His 2026 preprints cover telecom-band vanadium dopants in Si-on-SiC, microwave-to-optical transduction, qubit-based nanoscale magnetic spectroscopy, and atomistic spin-dynamics simulations with Giulia Galli's group. Over his career the focus has moved from fundamental spin physics in semiconductors to scalable quantum technologies and building large institutions around them.
