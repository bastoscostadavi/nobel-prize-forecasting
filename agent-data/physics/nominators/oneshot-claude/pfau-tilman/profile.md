## Biography

Tilman Pfau was born on 25 July 1965 in Bad Cannstatt (Stuttgart). He studied physics at the University of Konstanz from 1986 (Diplom 1992) and completed his doctorate there in 1994 under Jürgen Mlynek. He was a guest researcher with Claude Cohen-Tannoudji at ENS Paris and with Wolfgang Ketterle at MIT, and habilitated at Konstanz in 1998 with work on light in atom optics. Since 2000 he has been professor at Stuttgart, heading the 5th Institute of Physics, with visiting professorships in Helsinki, Beijing, Toronto and Cambridge. Honours include AAAS Fellow (2010), an ERC Advanced Grant (2011), APS Fellow and the Gentner-Kastler Prize (2014), the Herbert P. Broida Prize (2017), the Senior BEC Award (2023) and foreign membership of the Polish Academy of Sciences (2026) (de-Wikipedia).

## Research footprint

Pfau works on ultracold gases with strong dipole–dipole interactions, both magnetic and Rydberg (de-Wikipedia). Landmarks:

- The first Bose–Einstein condensate with strong magnetic dipolar interactions, made with chromium atoms (2005; Griesmaier, Stuhler, Pfau).
- "Observation of ultralong-range Rydberg molecules" (Bendkowsky et al., Nature 2009), the first observation of "trilobite"-type molecules bound by a Rydberg electron.
- "Observing the Rosensweig instability of a quantum ferrofluid" (Kadau et al., Nature 2016), which led to the discovery of self-bound quantum droplets in dysprosium.
- "Transient Supersolid Properties in an Array of Dipolar Quantum Droplets" (Böttcher et al., PRX 2019) and subsequent observation of the associated Goldstone mode.

His programme also extends to Rydberg physics in thermal vapour cells for quantum technology (inferred from IQST scope). The arc runs from atom optics, through dipolar BEC and Rydberg molecules, to beyond-mean-field quantum fluids and supersolidity.
