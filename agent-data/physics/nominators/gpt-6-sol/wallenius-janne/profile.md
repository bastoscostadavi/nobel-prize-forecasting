## Biography

Wallenius was born in 1968 in Trollhättan, Sweden. At 18 he won the particle-physics contest on the Swedish TV quiz *Kvitt eller dubbelt*, which earned him the nickname "partikel-Janne" (Wikipedia). He studied engineering physics at Chalmers from 1987 to 1992. He received his PhD in quantum chemistry from Uppsala University in 1996, with a thesis on metastable states of muonic molecules and the muon-catalysed fusion cycle (sv.wikipedia). He joined KTH's reactor-physics division in autumn 1996 and became full professor in 2008. In 2013 he co-founded Blykalla with Jesper Ejenstam and Peter Szakalos. His honours include:
- being named one of Sweden's ten most innovative entrepreneurs (2016);
- the KTH Innovation Award (2022).

## Research footprint

Swedish Wikipedia lists three main areas: design and safety analysis of lead-cooled reactor systems, development of advanced nuclear fuels, and radiation-damage physics.

- **Radiation damage in steels (2000s).** His most-cited papers are ab initio and molecular-dynamics studies of Fe–Cr alloys: Cr interactions with point defects in bcc Fe, formation energies of Fe–Cr alloys, and displacement cascades. These fed into EU multiscale-modelling programmes for fission and fusion structural materials (Semantic Scholar).
- **Reactor design and fuels.** He designed ELECTRA, a lead-cooled research reactor with plutonium-nitride fuel that could run on natural circulation of the coolant. Through Blykalla he worked on SEALER, a small lead-cooled reactor concept for arctic communities (sv.wikipedia).
- **Recent work (2023–2026).** Uranium-nitride and U–Zr–N fuels, including accident-tolerant composites; the SUNRISE-LFR project; and transient and safety analyses for the China Initiative Accelerator Driven System (CiADS).

His focus has moved from computational materials physics toward applied reactor design and fuel development.
