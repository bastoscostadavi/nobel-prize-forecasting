My pick is **Michael Berry and Yakir Aharonov**, for their discoveries concerning **quantum phase**, particularly the Berry phase and the Aharonov–Bohm effect.

Their work revealed how the geometry of a quantum system’s evolution—and electromagnetic potentials—can produce measurable effects. These discoveries underpin substantial parts of modern quantum physics, have extensive experimental confirmation, and have not yet received a dedicated Nobel.

My runner-up would be **Pablo Jarillo-Herrero, Allan MacDonald, and Rafi Bistritzer**, for predicting and discovering remarkable electronic behavior in graphene layers twisted at a “magic angle,” including superconductivity.

This is a low-confidence prediction: Nobel deliberations are secret, and I haven’t verified the latest 2026 news. But Berry and Aharonov would be my bet.
