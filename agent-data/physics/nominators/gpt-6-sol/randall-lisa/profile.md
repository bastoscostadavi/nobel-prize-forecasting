## Biography

Randall was born in New York City on 18 June 1962 and graduated from Stuyvesant High School in 1980. That year she won first place in the Westinghouse Science Talent Search (Wikipedia). She earned a BA (1983) and PhD (1987) in physics at Harvard, the PhD under Howard Georgi. She held positions at Berkeley/LBL, MIT and Princeton, where she was the first tenured woman in physics. She returned to Harvard in 2001 as its first tenured female theoretical physicist. Her memberships include APS Fellowship (2003), the American Academy of Arts and Sciences (2004), the National Academy of Sciences (2008) and the American Philosophical Society (2010). Her honours include the Klopsteg Award (2006), the Lilienfeld Prize (2007), the Andrew Gemant Award (2012), and the J. J. Sakurai Prize and Oskar Klein Medal (both 2019). *Time* named her among its 100 most influential people in 2007.

## Research footprint

Her most-cited work (INSPIRE) is the Randall–Sundrum programme with Raman Sundrum in 1999:

- *A large mass hierarchy from a small extra dimension* (about 10,800 citations), which uses a warped extra dimension to address the hierarchy problem;
- *An alternative to compactification* (about 8,100);
- *Out of this world supersymmetry breaking* (about 2,050), which introduced anomaly mediation.

Other influential work covers supersymmetric baryogenesis (with Dine and Thomas), wino dark matter (with Moroi), brane cosmology and radion stabilization, locally localized gravity (with Karch), and holographic interpretations of warped models (with Arkani-Hamed and Porrati). Later interests include dark matter models such as a dark disk, linked to a popular book connecting dark matter and the dinosaurs' extinction. Her 2025–2026 work covers black-hole islands and Page curves, massive gravity continuity, Kaluza–Klein graviton phenomenology and a warped-dimension interpretation of an LZ event.

## Public statements and values

- On the Higgs discovery (via Wikipedia): "what an exciting thing it is that people are excited that there is something fundamentally new that has been discovered."
- On fears that the LHC could create dangerous black holes (via Wikipedia): "not even conceivable unless space and gravity are very different from what we thought."
- She is a prominent popular writer. Her books include *Warped Passages* (2005), *Knocking on Heaven's Door* (2011), *Higgs Discovery* (2013) and *Dark Matter and the Dinosaurs* (2015). She wrote the libretto for Hèctor Parra's opera *Hypermusic Prologue*.
