My best guess is **Jun Ye and Hidetoshi Katori**, for pioneering **optical lattice clocks**—atomic clocks so precise they can detect the difference in time’s passage between slightly different heights.

Their work has the ingredients of a Nobel-winning contribution: a clear scientific breakthrough, decades of experimental validation, and broad consequences for precision measurement, tests of relativity, and a future redefinition of the second.

My strongest alternative would be **Charles Kane, Eugene Mele, and Laurens Molenkamp**, for predicting and experimentally establishing topological insulators.

It’s a low-confidence forecast: Nobel deliberations are confidential, and several discoveries have strong claims. But if I had to make one bet, it would be **Ye and Katori**.
