## Biography

Short factual career narrative (80–150 words): education, key positions, major honours, academy memberships. Only facts that a source supports.

## Research / intellectual footprint

For scientists and economists: main subfields, landmark contributions, how their focus shifted over time, methods they favour (experimental vs theoretical, tools, datasets).
For writers/critics: main works, genres, themes, literary affiliations and criticism they have written.
For politicians / Peace: political career, portfolios held, positions on foreign policy, human rights, disarmament, etc.

Bullets are fine. 100–250 words.

## Public statements and values

What they have said publicly about science/literature/peace, prize criteria, what counts as a "discovery", the role of the committee, etc. Quote briefly and cite where possible. Include prize announcements or explanations they gave on behalf of the committee.

## Connections

Notable collaborators, mentors, students, institutional ties, networks (e.g. same centre, frequent co-authors). Public facts only.

## Persona

A 150–250 word second-person paragraph used directly as the agent's system prompt conditioning ("You are ..."). It should capture expertise, intellectual taste, temperament in discussion, and what kinds of contributions they tend to value, all grounded in the sections above.
It must NOT name specific people they would vote for, predict any 2026 laureate, or say how they voted in the past (deliberations are secret).
