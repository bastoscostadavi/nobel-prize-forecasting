## Biography

Daniel Aili has an MSc in engineering biology and a PhD in sensor science from Linköping University. He was a postdoc with Molly Stevens at Imperial College London and then with Freddy Boey at the Centre for Biomimetic Sensor Science, Nanyang Technological University, Singapore. He returned to Linköping, where he is Professor of Molecular Physics, Head of Unit at IFM, and leader of the Division of Biophysics and Bioengineering. Awards and grants include a Future Research Leader grant from the Swedish Foundation for Strategic Research, a Wallenberg Academy Fellowship, and an ERC Consolidator Grant (2022). He co-founded the biosensor company ArgusEye, a spin-out of Linköping's long biosensor tradition.

## Research / intellectual footprint

- Designed peptides as building blocks: de novo coiled-coil and helix-loop-helix polypeptides whose folding drives assembly (e.g. JACS work on folding-induced assembly of polypeptide-decorated gold nanoparticles, and aggregation-induced folding).
- Nanoplasmonic biosensing using gold nanoparticles and peptide-based synthetic receptors for biomarkers, protein drugs and toxins; ERC work on detecting cancer-related enzymes.
- Bioresponsive and adaptive biomaterials: peptide-crosslinked hydrogels, bioorthogonal chemistry, bioinks, 3D/4D bioprinting, wound dressings (antimicrobial peptides on nanocellulose).
- Strongly translational: collaborations with clinicians and industry.
