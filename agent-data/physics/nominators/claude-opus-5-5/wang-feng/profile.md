## Biography

Wang received a BA from Fudan University in Shanghai in 1999 and a PhD from Columbia University in 2004. He was a Miller Fellow at Berkeley's Miller Institute for Basic Research in Science from 2005 to 2007 and joined the Berkeley physics faculty in fall 2007. He is now a full professor and also a faculty scientist at LBNL. His honours include:
- a DOE Early Career Award (2010);
- a Sloan Fellowship;
- a Packard Fellowship;
- the PECASE;
- the IUPAP C10 Young Scientist Prize;
- the OCPA Outstanding Young Researcher Award;
- the APS Frank Isakson Prize for Optical Effects in Solids (2024).

The Isakson Prize cited "pioneering and trail-blazing works on the exploration of new physics and exotic phenomena in 1D and 2D quantum materials". He is an APS Fellow (Berkeley pages; DOE; Wikipedia).

## Research footprint

Wang's group studies light–matter interaction in nanoscale and low-dimensional systems with laser spectroscopy. Its techniques reach individual nanostructures with femtosecond time resolution, over a spectrum from the far-infrared to the UV (Berkeley page). Early on he worked on optical spectroscopy of individual carbon nanotubes and on plasmonics. From around 2008 he turned to graphene optics, including gate-tunable infrared transitions. The DOE article credits his early-career programme with demonstrating:
- a gate-tunable insulating state in bilayer graphene as a quantum valley-Hall insulator;
- topological valley transport at bilayer-graphene domain walls;
- ultrafast pseudo-magnetic fields for valley excitons in WSe2;
- ultrafast charge transfer in MoS2/WS2 heterostructures.

In the moiré era his group reported optical detection of Mott and generalized Wigner crystal states in WSe2/WS2 moiré superlattices (Regan et al., arXiv 2019). They then imaged generalized Wigner crystals with STM together with Crommie's group (Li et al., arXiv 2021). They also studied tunable correlated and ferromagnetic states in graphene/hBN moiré systems (Chen et al., arXiv 2020). Recent work covers terahertz phonon engineering in van der Waals heterostructures (Nature 2024) and Coulomb drag in electron–hole bilayers (2025).

## Public statements and values

In the DOE article (2019) he said: "The DOE Early Career Award played a critical role in my research career and laid the foundation for our current research activities on electrical and optical properties in two-dimensional van der Waals heterostructures." He also noted that he built a DOE core programme on "Van der Waals Heterostructures: Novel Materials and Emerging Phenomena". This points to a strong commitment to sustained, agency-supported basic research on engineered materials (inference).
