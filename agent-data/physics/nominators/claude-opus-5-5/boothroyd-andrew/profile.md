## Biography

Boothroyd took his undergraduate and graduate degrees at the University of Cambridge. He then went to the University of Warwick, first as a postdoc and then as a University Lecturer. He moved to Oxford in 1992. There he has been Head of Condensed Matter Physics, Associate Head of Department (technical services) and Vice-Provost of Oriel College, and he now serves as Head of the Department of Physics. He received the Institute of Physics Brian Pippard Prize in 2011 and was named an APS Outstanding Referee in 2017. He is the author of the textbook *Principles of Neutron Scattering from Condensed Matter* (Oxford University Press, 2020).

## Research footprint

Boothroyd's group uses neutron and X-ray scattering, supported by in-house crystal growth (much of it with Dharmalingam Prabhakaran), to study correlated and topological materials. Highlights include:

- The magnetic Coulomb phase in the spin ice Ho2Ti2O7 (Science 2009).
- Sodium-ion ordering in sodium cobaltate (Nature 2005) and rattling-mode suppression of its thermal conductivity (Nature Materials 2013).
- The structure and superconductivity of LiFeAs (2008) and magnetism in Fe(Se,Te) superconductors.
- Orbital currents in CuO (Science 2011) and femtoscale magnetically induced lattice distortions in multiferroic TbMnO3 (Science 2011).
- Ultrafast X-ray studies of photo-induced melting of magnetic and orbital order in manganites.

Since about 2019 he has worked on magnetic topological semimetals: an "ideal Weyl semimetal induced by magnetic exchange" (PRB 2019), Weyl states induced by helical order (2023) and candidate axion insulators. Recent work (2025–2026) includes altermagnetism in NiF2 and circular dichroism in resonant inelastic X-ray scattering. His methods are neutron spectroscopy, polarized neutron diffraction, resonant X-ray scattering and RIXS at central facilities.
