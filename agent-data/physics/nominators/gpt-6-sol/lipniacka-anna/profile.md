## Biography

Lipniacka's early papers (1986–1991) list Warsaw University. They are neutrino–nucleon scattering measurements (sin²θW, structure functions), and she also signed the ZEUS letter of intent and technical proposal for HERA. From 1991 she worked on the DELPHI experiment at LEP. INSPIRE also lists Stockholm University as a past affiliation, and her DELPHI supersymmetry searches were written with Stockholm colleagues (Hultqvist, Johansson). She is now Professor at the University of Bergen, where she teaches non-relativistic quantum mechanics (PHYS201) and works on ATLAS and a cosmic-muon telescope project (UiB page). INSPIRE counts nearly 1,900 papers, mostly collaboration papers. Her degree dates and exact appointment years were not found.

## Research footprint

- **DELPHI at LEP.** She searched for supersymmetric particles, for example neutralino pair production at 192–208 GeV. She wrote single-author analyses such as "Understanding SUSY limits from LEP" and "Can SUSY be found at the Tevatron run II?".
- **ATLAS at the LHC.** In the Bergen group she has worked on searches for supersymmetry and dark matter; her UiB page lists "Dark Matter, Supersymmetry".
- **Phenomenology with theorists.** She has written on charged Higgs bosons in the CP-violating type-II two-Higgs-doublet model (with Per Osland, Stefano Moretti and Farvah Mahmoudi, 2012–2013), on SUSY parameter spaces with signal-specific discovery potential, on machine-learning classification of sphalerons and black holes at the LHC (2024), and on CP violation in H → τ⁺τ⁻γ (2024, with Stefan Pokorski and Janusz Rosiek).

Her UiB competence line reads: "The smallest and the biggest structures in the Universe."
