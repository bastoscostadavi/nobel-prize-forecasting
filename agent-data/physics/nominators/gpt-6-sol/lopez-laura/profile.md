## Biography

Lopez grew up in Barrington, Illinois. She earned an S.B. in physics at MIT (2004), and then an M.S. (2007) and Ph.D. (2011) in astronomy and astrophysics at UC Santa Cruz. Her advisors there were Enrico Ramirez-Ruiz and Mark Krumholz. She was an Einstein Fellow and Pappalardo Fellow at MIT (2011–2014) and a Hubble Fellow at the Harvard–Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics (2014–2015), before joining Ohio State. Her honours include:

- the AAS Annie Jump Cannon Award (2016);
- a Cottrell Scholar Award (2019);
- the AAS High-Energy Astrophysics Division Early Career Prize (2022);
- the ASP Nancy Grace Roman Award and AAS Fellowship (2025);
- the APS Edward A. Bouchet Award (2026).

(Sources: Wikipedia and the OSU profile.)

## Research footprint

Lopez's thesis, "The tumultuous lives and deaths of stars," combined X-ray morphology of young supernova remnants with feedback physics. Key results include:

- using X-ray morphology to constrain explosion type in young remnants (*ApJ* 732, 2011);
- a multiwavelength pressure census of 30 Doradus to identify what drives the expansion of giant H II regions (*ApJ* 731, 2011);
- evidence that the Galactic remnant W49B likely comes from a jet-driven core-collapse explosion (*ApJ* 764, 2013).

She has also used NuSTAR on Tycho's remnant and Fermi gamma rays to study cosmic-ray escape in the Small Magellanic Cloud (2018). She has compared neutron-star kicks with remnant asymmetries (2017). More recently she has studied hot-gas outflows in the starburst galaxies M82, NGC 253 and NGC 4945. Over time her focus has broadened from individual remnants to feedback and winds on galactic scales, using data from radio through gamma rays.
