My pick is **Charles L. Kane, Eugene J. Mele, and Laurens W. Molenkamp**, for their work on **topological insulators**—materials that insulate internally while supporting conducting states at their boundaries.

Kane and Mele made foundational theoretical predictions; Molenkamp’s group supplied landmark experimental confirmation. The work opened a major field, connects fundamental quantum physics with real materials, and has had time to establish its importance—all strong Nobel credentials.

My runner-up would be **Pablo Jarillo-Herrero, Allan MacDonald, and Rafi Bistritzer**, for “magic-angle” twisted bilayer graphene, where rotating two carbon sheets slightly produces striking electronic behavior, including superconductivity.

**Confidence is low.** Nobel nominations remain confidential for 50 years, and predicting the award’s timing is considerably harder than identifying deserving discoveries.
