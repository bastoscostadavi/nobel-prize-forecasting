## Biography

Brataas earned his PhD in physics at NTNU in 1997. He was then a postdoctoral fellow at TU Delft (1997–1999) and at Harvard University (1999–2002), and he has been a professor at NTNU since 2002 (NTNU page). He was Divisional Associate Editor of Physical Review Letters from 2007 to 2014 and chaired the Kavli Prize Committee in Nanoscience from 2013 to 2019. He received an ERC Advanced Grant in 2015. He was the founding director of QuSpin, the Research Council of Norway Centre of Excellence for quantum spintronics, from 2017 to 2023. He is still one of its Primary Investigators (NTNU and QuSpin pages).

## Research footprint

Brataas works on quantum transport and spin dynamics in metals, insulators, semiconductors and superconductors (NTNU page). He is best known for theories of how spin currents couple to magnetization dynamics in nanoscale magnetic structures. Two lines of that work are well known. The first is magnetoelectronic circuit theory, developed with Yuli Nazarov and Gerrit Bauer during and after his Delft period. The second is spin pumping and enhanced Gilbert damping, developed with Yaroslav Tserkovnyak and Bauer (Phys. Rev. Lett. 2002; Rev. Mod. Phys. 2005). With Andrew Kent and Hideo Ohno he co-wrote a widely cited review of current-induced torques (Nature Materials 2012). The group does no experiments; it works with analytical and mathematical methods (norwegianscitechnews). Its 2025–2026 papers cover antiferromagnetic insulators and magnon spin transport, spin–orbit effects on electron magnetic moments, and hybrids of unconventional magnets and superconductors (NTNU publication list). Over time his focus has moved from ferromagnetic metal heterostructures to insulating and antiferromagnetic magnonics and to "quantum" spintronics.
