## Biography

Born 9 March 1959 in Higashimatsuyama, Saitama. He graduated from Saitama University (1981) and received a PhD from the University of Tokyo (1986) under Masatoshi Koshiba, with a thesis on nucleon decay searches. He joined ICRR in 1988, became professor in 1999, and led its Research Center for Cosmic Neutrinos. He became ICRR director (2008 per Japanese Wikipedia; NobelPrize.org says 2015). He is also a principal investigator at Kavli IPMU. From October 2020 he was President of the Science Council of Japan. He chaired IUPAP's astroparticle physics commission (C4) from 2021 to 2024. His honours include the 2015 Nobel Prize in Physics (with Arthur McDonald), the Order of Culture (2015), the Breakthrough Prize (shared with his collaboration), and the IUPAP Homi Bhabha award (2019).

## Research footprint

Kajita started in the Kamiokande proton-decay experiment. While studying atmospheric neutrinos as a background, he noticed an anomaly in the muon-neutrino flux. In 1998 he led the Super-Kamiokande analysis showing zenith-angle–dependent disappearance of atmospheric muon neutrinos. This was the first convincing evidence for neutrino oscillations and thus for non-zero neutrino mass, which is physics beyond the Standard Model. He then moved into gravitational-wave physics as principal investigator of KAGRA, the underground cryogenic interferometer in Kamioka, and has promoted large Japanese facilities such as Hyper-Kamiokande. His work is in large collaborative experiments built on long-term, careful analysis of data.

## Public statements and values

- On his discovery: "this is clearly the physics that is beyond the standard model of particle physics" (NobelPrize.org telephone interview, 2015).
- He described how "there is something strange happening" in the atmospheric-neutrino background, which led him from proton decay to oscillations (same).
- "As an experimentalist I was always excited in working in Super Kamiokande and also Kamiokande experiments" (same).
- Wikipedia links an Editage interview titled "In my days, nobody felt rushed just because research was making slow progress". This suggests he is concerned about short-term pressure on young researchers (inference from the title).
- As Science Council of Japan president from 2020, he led the council during its dispute with the government over the rejection of six nominated members (general public record; details not re-verified here).

## Connections

- Doctoral advisor: Masatoshi Koshiba. Long-time Super-K colleagues include Yoichiro Suzuki and the late Yoji Totsuka. Co-laureate: Arthur McDonald.
- Networks: ICRR, Kavli IPMU, the KAGRA and Super-Kamiokande collaborations, Science Council of Japan, IUPAP C4. Japanese Wikipedia also lists him as a member of the Cabinet Office's Council for Science, Technology and Innovation and as holding an honorary chair at National Tsing Hua University (Taiwan).
