## Biography

Rudolf Gross was born in 1956. He received his doctorate from the University of Tübingen in 1987 and did postdoctoral work at the Electrotechnical Laboratory in Tsukuba, Japan, and at IBM's T. J. Watson Research Center. He habilitated at Tübingen in 1993. In 1995 he became full professor and director of the II. Physics Institute at the University of Cologne. From 2000 until his retirement in March 2025 he directed the Walther-Meißner-Institute for Low Temperature Research and held the TUM chair of Technical Physics. He is a full member of the Bavarian Academy of Sciences (2003) and of acatech (2021). His honours include the Heinz Maier-Leibnitz Medal (2007) and the Bavarian "Pro meritis scientiae et litterarum" (2025) (WMI; de-Wikipedia).

## Research footprint

According to WMI, his research concerns "quantum phenomena in superconducting and magnetic materials" and "transport and quantum phenomena in nano-scale superconducting and magnetic devices", with applications in quantum computing, communication and sensing. His early career covered high-temperature superconductor thin films and Josephson junctions. At WMI he built a superconducting-circuit programme. One landmark from it is the 2010 Nature Physics paper "Circuit quantum electrodynamics in the ultrastrong-coupling regime" (Niemczyk, Deppe, Huebl, Menzel, Hocke, Schwarz, … Gross), which showed light–matter coupling strong enough to break the rotating-wave approximation. The group also worked on propagating quantum microwaves, spin-mechanical and magnon hybrid systems, and spin caloritronics and spin-current transport in magnetic insulators. With Achim Marx he co-authored the widely used German textbook *Festkörperphysik* (De Gruyter). His recent focus has been institutional as well as scientific: building Munich's quantum-technology ecosystem.

## Public statements and values

WMI notes that he initiated Germany's first collaborative research centre on solid-state quantum information processing (SFB 631, spokesperson 2003–2015). From 2019 to 2025 he led the Munich Center for Quantum Science and Technology (MCQST) cluster of excellence, and he co-initiated Munich Quantum Valley. These roles show a commitment to turning basic quantum physics into technology through large coordinated programmes. No direct quotes on prizes or discovery were found.
