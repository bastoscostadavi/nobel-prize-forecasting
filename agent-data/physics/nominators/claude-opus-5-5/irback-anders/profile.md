## Biography

Born 1960 in Sweden (Swedish Wikipedia). Studied at Lund University from 1979; PhD 1988 with the thesis "Nonperturbative aspects of gauge theories". Held positions at Universität Bielefeld and CERN before returning to Lund in 1993. Professor of theoretical physics at Lund since 2003. Elected to the Royal Physiographic Society in Lund (2007), the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences (2013) and the Nobel Committee for Physics (2017; Lund portal). Committee pages list him as a member in 2018–2021, chair in 2022, and co-opted member in 2024–2025.

## Research footprint

He started in non-perturbative lattice gauge theory (elementary particle physics). From the mid-1990s he moved into theoretical biophysics, with proteins as the main topic. Work includes lattice-protein models of folding, sequence design and local interactions; simplified all-atom models of peptide folding and aggregation; amyloid formation thermodynamics; folding in crowded cellular environments (Markov and Monte Carlo modelling of peptides among protein crowders); and phase separation of intrinsically disordered proteins into biomolecular condensates (finite-size scaling of simulated droplet phase diagrams). With Sandipan Mohanty he wrote PROFASI, a Monte Carlo package for protein folding and aggregation (J. Comput. Chem. 2006). His current focus is quantum algorithms (quantum annealing, QAOA) for hard optimisation problems, tested on lattice-protein folding and design with D-Wave hardware at Jülich and IBM gate-based devices (Phys. Rev. Research 2022, 2024; Phys. Rev. E 2025). He also works on computational geophysics, such as synchronising palaeodata timescales. His methods are statistical mechanics, Monte Carlo, and coarse-grained modelling.
