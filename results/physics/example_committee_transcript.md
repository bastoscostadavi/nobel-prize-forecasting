**1. B033 — Gamma-ray burst afterglows and their cosmological origin** (proposed: Enrico Costa, Filippo Frontera, Shrinivas R. Kulkarni)

- *Nobel worthiness:* The detection of X-ray, optical and radio afterglows of gamma-ray bursts settled a decades-old puzzle: bursts are cosmological, among the most energetic explosions known. It is a clean, discovery-centred result that opened a new field and still anchors transient and high-redshift astrophysics.
- *Attribution:* The decisive steps were the BeppoSAX localisation and X-ray afterglow detection (Costa, Frontera and the Italian-Dutch team) and the optical/radio follow-up establishing distance and energetics (Kulkarni and collaborators). Jan van Paradijs, a key figure in the optical identification, is deceased and cannot be honoured, and others such as Djorgovski, Metzger and Frail also contributed. The three-person split is defensible but leaves some unresolved credit questions.
- *Maturity:* Discovered in 1997 and confirmed by thousands of later afterglows, with the cosmological interpretation fully stable. Recognition is mature and arguably overdue.
- *Uncertainties:* Relative weight of the X-ray localisation versus the optical redshift measurement is not settled by the supplied text. Whether other optical and radio pioneers should be included instead of the three listed is uncertain; I am relying on memory of the history rather than independent verification.

**2. B010 — High-energy astrophysical neutrinos and IceCube neutrino astronomy** (proposed: Francis Halzen, Albrecht Karle, Shigeru Yoshida)

- *Nobel worthiness:* The detection of a diffuse flux of astrophysical neutrinos at TeV-PeV energies, and the later association with a flaring blazar and the Galactic plane, opened neutrino astronomy and is a genuine observation of new physics-relevant messengers. Instrumenting a cubic kilometre of Antarctic ice was a major experimental achievement.
- *Attribution:* Halzen was the intellectual driver of IceCube and the concept, Karle led key detector and analysis development, and Yoshida contributed to the analysis and the Askaryan/radio and cascade efforts. Credit is entangled with a very large collaboration and with earlier AMANDA and the ANTARES/Baikal pioneers, so a clean three-person selection is somewhat arbitrary.
- *Maturity:* The 2013 evidence was confirmed with independent channels and the 2017 TXS 0506+056 coincidence and NGC 1068 results. Source population is still incompletely understood, but the discovery of the flux itself is solid and timely.
- *Uncertainties:* Whether the committee views the discovery or the instrument as the prize-worthy element. The individual-level attribution inside a large collaboration is uncertain, and I cannot verify exact roles.

**3. B040 — Cosmic inflation and the quantum origin of cosmological structure** (proposed: Alan H. Guth, Andrei Linde, Viatcheslav Mukhanov)

- *Nobel worthiness:* Inflation explains horizon, flatness and the origin of primordial fluctuations, and its quantum-fluctuation seeds match the CMB spectrum in remarkable detail. It is among the most influential ideas in modern cosmology, in the area of my own research field, though I remain aware that the specific inflationary mechanism is still not pinned down.
- *Attribution:* The supplied trio omits Starobinsky, whose early work was independent and important, and Albrecht and Steinhardt, Sato and others also contributed. Mukhanov's structure-formation calculation is arguably as essential as the original idea, so the three names support a fair but contested configuration.
- *Maturity:* The predictions of nearly scale-invariant adiabatic Gaussian perturbations are well supported by WMAP and Planck, but no primordial gravitational-wave signature has been found and alternatives are not excluded. The idea is mature in influence but not decisively confirmed as the mechanism.
- *Uncertainties:* No direct detection of primordial B-modes; the inflaton and the model remain unidentified. Credit allocation, especially Starobinsky's omission, is a serious unresolved problem. The theory's status in a fundamental framework such as string theory is not settled, which I weigh cautiously.

- *Nobel worthiness:* Magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene produced flat bands, correlated insulators and superconductivity in a tunable carbon platform, launching twistronics and a large body of work on moire materials. The combination of a sharp prediction and striking experiment is attractive.
- *Attribution:* Bistritzer and MacDonald made the flat-band prediction in 2011, and Jarillo-Herrero's group delivered the 2018 experiments. Cao and Fatemi, the first authors, and Dean's group and others contributed to the experimental establishment; the theory-plus-experiment pairing is clean, but junior experimental contributors cannot be credited.

- *Uncertainties:* Mechanism of the superconductivity is not established. Reproducibility of device-to-device properties and the role of disorder remain open.

- *Attribution:* Katori proposed and first demonstrated the magic-wavelength lattice clock, and Ye developed the strontium clock to record accuracy and many-body-physics-enabled performance. The two-person configuration is clean, though it overlaps with earlier laser-cooling and frequency-comb prizes.

It is a clear case where theory gave a distinct observable and surveys then found it, and current precision results (including recent hints on dark-energy evolution) rely on it.
- *Attribution:* Sunyaev with Zeldovich and Peebles with Yu developed the physical origin; Eisenstein et al. (SDSS) and Cole et al. (2dFGRS) made the 2005 detections. Peebles already received a Nobel Prize in 2019, and others such as Percival and Blake have credit claims, so the supplied trio is only one of several possible configurations.
- *Maturity:* Detected in 2005, strongly confirmed by BOSS, eBOSS and DESI. Mature, though the cosmological conclusions are still being refined.
- *Uncertainties:* Credit is diffuse across theory and survey teams, and I cannot verify the priority claims. The ruler relies on the standard cosmological model, so the prize case is partly tied to its interpretation.

### Olle Eriksson

**1. B019 — Dynamical mean-field theory of strongly correlated electrons** (proposed: Antoine Georges, Dieter Vollhardt, Gabriel Kotliar)

- *Nobel worthiness:* DMFT turned the strongly correlated electron problem into a controlled, computable framework: the limit of infinite dimensions, a local self-energy mapped onto a quantum impurity problem, and a clear description of the Mott transition and the coexistence of Hubbard bands and quasiparticles. Its lasting impact is large, since it underlies DFT+DMFT, now the standard route to realistic correlated materials such as transition-metal oxides, f-electron systems and iron-based superconductors. This is a conceptual advance in how we compute and understand quantum matter, not a single measurement.
- *Attribution:* Vollhardt (with Metzner) opened the infinite-dimensional limit; Georges and Kotliar made the mapping to the impurity problem and the self-consistent scheme. The credited trio is a natural and fairly clean configuration. Müller-Hartmann, Metzner and Jarrell, Rozenberg and Krauth are also important to the early development, so some credit is necessarily left out.
- *Maturity:* Over thirty years of development with extensive cross-validation against spectroscopy and transport. Recognition is timely if not overdue. Methodological theory is less often prized, but the demonstrated reach into materials physics is broad.
- *Uncertainties:* Whether the committee will treat a theoretical-method prize as sufficiently discovery-like. Allocation among Metzner, Müller-Hartmann, Jarrell, Rozenberg and Krauth is not fully resolved by the supplied names. I am relying on my own knowledge of the history rather than verified sources.

**2. B029 — Magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene: flat bands, correlated insulators and superconductivity** (proposed: Pablo Jarillo-Herrero, Allan H. MacDonald, Rafi Bistritzer)

- *Nobel worthiness:* The prediction of flat bands at the magic angle and their experimental realisation as correlated insulators and superconductivity opened a tunable, clean platform for strong correlations, built on a theory-first idea. It changed expectations for what two-dimensional materials can host and has spawned moire physics across many material families. The impact is large and still broadening.
- *Attribution:* Bistritzer and MacDonald supplied the continuum-model prediction of the magic angle; Jarillo-Herrero's group delivered the decisive 2018 experiments. The theory-plus-experiment pairing is a natural prize structure. Credit questions include earlier theory by Lopes dos Santos, Peres and Castro Neto, and the contributions of Cory Dean and others to related experiments, none of which are in the supplied names.
- *Maturity:* Less than a decade old, but independently reproduced by many groups and the main features are stable. The microscopic origin of the superconductivity remains debated, so a prize would need to honour the flat band and correlated insulator discovery rather than the pairing mechanism.
- *Uncertainties:* Mechanism of superconductivity is still unsettled. Priority between the continuum-model theory and earlier twisted-bilayer work. Possibility that recognition is somewhat early compared with the field's maturation.

- *Uncertainties:* Priority relative to independent contemporaneous proposals. Independence and breadth of experimental confirmation across materials. Absence of a demonstrated application or a consolidated body of data.

- *Nobel worthiness:* Optical lattice clocks, built on Katori's magic-wavelength proposal, turned optical frequency standards into a many-atom measurement tool with fractional uncertainties near 1e-18, far beyond the microwave definition of the second. **4. B023 — Circuit quantum electrodynamics** (proposed: Alexandre Blais, Robert J. Schoelkopf, Andreas Wallraff)

- *Nobel worthiness:* Circuit quantum electrodynamics created strong-coupling light-matter physics on chip and underlies today's superconducting quantum processors: dispersive readout, cavity-mediated gates and quantum-limited amplification chains. The decisive work is collective, and several other leaders (for example the collaboration's founding experimentalists and the earlier-generation pioneers of deep-water and deep-ice neutrino telescopes) are not on the supplied credit list. A three-person configuration is defensible but the allocation is genuinely contested.
- *Maturity:* Mature enough: the diffuse flux was first reported in 2013 and has been confirmed by independent analysis channels and by other detectors; source evidence from 2018 to 2023 is steadily accumulating, with multi-messenger follow-up. Individual source identifications are still at moderate significance, but the discovery of the astrophysical flux itself is secure.
- *Uncertainties:* Source associations (TXS 0506+056, NGC 1068) rest on 3-4 sigma level evidence and the origin of most of the diffuse flux remains unknown. Which individuals deserve credit relative to the collaboration is unresolved; I rely on memory for the roles of the named people. Independent confirmation by a second large detector (KM3NeT, Baikal-GVD) is still developing.

**2. B008 — Topological insulators and the quantum spin Hall effect** (proposed: Charles L. Kane, Laurens W. Molenkamp, M. Zahid Hasan)

- *Nobel worthiness:* The theoretical prediction and experimental realisation of the quantum spin Hall effect and three-dimensional topological insulators created a new class of electronic matter with protected surface and edge states. It reshaped condensed matter physics, with broad influence on spintronics, superconductivity and quantum computing proposals. The originality and clarity are very high.
- *Attribution:* Kane (with Mele) supplied the decisive theoretical formulation and Z2 classification; Molenkamp's group gave the first experimental observation of the quantum spin Hall effect in HgTe quantum wells; Hasan identified the first three-dimensional topological insulators by photoemission. Mele and other theorists (Bernevig, Hughes, Zhang, Fu) are important to the credit story; limiting to three names forces a difficult choice.
- *Maturity:* Highly mature: more than fifteen years of independent confirmation across materials and techniques, with a stable interpretation. Recognition is arguably overdue.
- *Uncertainties:* Dividing credit between Kane and Mele, and the position of the Bernevig-Hughes-Zhang proposal for HgTe, is unresolved. Whether the prize should address the two- and three-dimensional cases together is a judgement call.

**3. B033 — Gamma-ray burst afterglows and their cosmological origin** (proposed: Enrico Costa, Filippo Frontera, Shrinivas R. Kulkarni)

- *Nobel worthiness:* Localisation of X-ray afterglows with BeppoSAX followed by optical and radio detection and redshift measurement established that gamma-ray bursts are cosmological explosions, settling a thirty-year puzzle and opening time-domain high-energy astrophysics. This is a clear, consequential discovery that combined instrument capability with rapid follow-up. I declare my own field's proximity (gamma-ray instrumentation) and have tried not to let it inflate the ranking.
- *Attribution:* Costa and Frontera led the BeppoSAX discovery of the X-ray afterglow; Kulkarni's group delivered the key radio and optical follow-up and distance evidence. Credit is shared with others such as van Paradijs (deceased), Piro, Djorgovski and Metzger, so the supplied trio does not capture everyone and the ordering of contribution is debatable.
- *Maturity:* Very mature: 1997 discovery, confirmed thousands of times, with later links to supernovae and neutron-star mergers. Timing is overdue, with the risk that eligible people are lost over time.
- *Uncertainties:* Eligibility is complicated by the deceased and the many contributors to the optical identification. My recollection of the relative roles of the optical groups is imperfect.

**4. B043 — Optical lattice clocks** (proposed: Hidetoshi Katori, Jun Ye)

- *Nobel worthiness:* The proposal of the magic-wavelength optical lattice clock and its development to the 10^-18 fractional-uncertainty regime transformed time and frequency metrology, with consequences for tests of fundamental physics, relativistic geodesy and a possible redefinition of the second.
- *Attribution:* Katori proposed the magic-wavelength concept and demonstrated early strontium lattice clocks; Jun Ye's group pushed the accuracy and coherence to record levels. This yields a clean two-person configuration, although other clock groups (for example in Boulder, Paris and Braunschweig) contributed.
- *Maturity:* Mature in science, with many independent clocks agreeing at high precision; the redefinition of the second has not yet happened, so impact is still unfolding.
- *Uncertainties:* Overlap with prior frequency-comb and ion-clock prizes may weigh against it. Whether a prize should wait for the redefinition of the second is a judgement.

**5. B006 — Quantum simulation of Hubbard models with ultracold atoms in optical lattices** (proposed: Immanuel Bloch, Juan Ignacio Cirac, Peter Zoller)

- *Nobel worthiness:* Ultracold atoms in optical lattices realised the Hubbard model in a clean, tunable setting, observing the superfluid to Mott-insulator transition and later quantum-gas-microscope resolution of correlations. This created a quantum-simulation platform of broad importance for many-body physics.
- *Attribution:* Zoller and Cirac with Jaksch gave the theoretical blueprint for atomic Hubbard simulation; Bloch's group delivered the experimental breakthrough in 2002 and subsequent developments; Greiner was a lead author of the first Mott-insulator observation and the microscopes. Jaksch is not on the list and Greiner's role is meaningful, so the choice among four named people is sensitive.
- *Maturity:* Mature: roughly twenty years of replication and extension. Quantitative simulation beyond classical reach is still being demonstrated, so the final impact is partially open.
- *Uncertainties:* Trade-off between theory and experiment credit, and the omission of Greiner or Jaksch, is unresolved. Adjacent to previous prizes on cold atoms and quantum technologies.

**6. B029 — Magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene: flat bands, correlated insulators and superconductivity** (proposed: Allan H. MacDonald, Pablo Jarillo-Herrero)

- *Nobel worthiness:* The theoretical prediction of flat bands at the magic angle and the experimental discovery of correlated insulators and superconductivity in twisted bilayer graphene opened the field of moire quantum materials, a very active frontier with high originality.
- *Attribution:* Bistritzer and MacDonald calculated the magic-angle flat bands; Jarillo-Herrero's group reported the correlated and superconducting states in 2018. I chose MacDonald and Jarillo-Herrero to pair theory and experiment, but Bistritzer's early role and Cory Dean's and others' parallel work complicate the picture.
- *Maturity:* Moderately mature: reproduced by many groups since 2018, with a rich phase diagram, but the nature of the superconductivity is still debated.
- *Uncertainties:* Mechanism of superconductivity and correlated states remains contested. Possibly early compared with the surrounding moire and fractional Chern insulator literature.

**7. B023 — Circuit quantum electrodynamics** (proposed: Robert J. Schoelkopf, Andreas Wallraff, Alexandre Blais)

- *Nobel worthiness:* Circuit quantum electrodynamics provides strong, controllable coupling of superconducting artificial atoms to microwave photons, and underlies the readout and control architecture of superconducting quantum processors. Its importance is high.
- *Attribution:* Blais, Girvin, Schoelkopf and collaborators proposed the architecture; Wallraff and Schoelkopf's groups demonstrated strong coupling in 2004. I favour two experimentalists and a lead theorist, leaving Girvin out with some reluctance.
- *Maturity:* Mature as a technique, with wide independent replication. However, the 2025 prize on superconducting circuits is closely adjacent, which affects timing and could appear redundant.
- *Uncertainties:* Recency and overlap with the 2025 prize on macroscopic quantum effects in circuits. Exclusion of Girvin from a limited trio.

**8. B009 — Quantum cascade laser** (proposed: Jérôme Faist, Federico Capasso, Alfred Y. Cho)

- *Nobel worthiness:* The quantum cascade laser used band-structure engineering in semiconductor superlattices to produce a compact mid-infrared and terahertz source, with lasting value for spectroscopy and sensing. It is an elegant example of designed quantum devices turning into widely used instruments.
- *Attribution:* Faist, Capasso and Cho, with Sirtori, Hutchinson and Sivco at Bell Labs, made the first device in 1994; Cho's molecular beam epitaxy was essential. Kazarinov and Suris proposed the underlying idea in 1971 and are not in the supplied list, so the credit is incomplete.
- *Maturity:* Very mature: three decades of development and commercial deployment, although some may regard it as engineering rather than discovery physics.
- *Uncertainties:* The prior-art role of Kazarinov and Suris is unresolved. Physics versus technology boundary for this prize.

### Bernhard Mehlig
