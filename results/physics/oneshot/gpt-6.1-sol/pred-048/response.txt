My best guess is **Charles L. Kane, Eugene J. Mele, and Laurens W. Molenkamp**, for their work on **topological insulators**—materials that insulate internally while supporting conducting states at their surfaces or edges.

Kane and Mele developed foundational theory; Molenkamp led experiments demonstrating the closely related quantum spin Hall effect. It’s an established breakthrough with roughly two decades of scientific impact, which makes it a plausible Nobel choice.

My runner-up would be **John Pendry and David Smith**, for metamaterials that manipulate electromagnetic waves in unusual ways.

Confidence is low: I’d put topological insulators first, but Nobel predictions are inherently speculative.
