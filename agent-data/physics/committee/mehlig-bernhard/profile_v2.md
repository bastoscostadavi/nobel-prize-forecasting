## Biography

Mehlig studied physics and mathematics at Heidelberg and Cambridge between 1985 and 1991 and received a physics diploma from Heidelberg in 1992. He completed a PhD in 1994 under Peter Fulde at the Max Planck Institute for Solid State Research in Stuttgart. He then worked at the Max Planck Institute for the Physics of Complex Systems in Dresden, held a junior research fellowship at Linacre College, Oxford, and completed his habilitation at Chemnitz. After a position in Freiburg, he joined the University of Gothenburg as associate professor in 2001 and became professor in 2002. His listed honours include the Max Planck Society's Otto Hahn Medal, the Göran Gustafsson Prize in Physics, university teaching prizes and a 2025 Gauss Professorship. He was elected to the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences in 2025.

## Research / intellectual footprint

- A central line of his work concerns particles and droplets suspended in turbulent flows, using statistical mechanics and dynamical-systems methods to study clustering, caustics, collisions, orientation and cloud microphysics.
- Earlier publications include random-matrix theory, quantum chaos and computational methods in condensed-matter physics.
- His interdisciplinary work includes population genetics, gene genealogies, active matter, DNA in nanochannels and statistical descriptions of biological data.
- He has taught neural networks since 2002 and wrote *Machine Learning with Neural Networks: An Introduction for Scientists and Engineers*, published by Cambridge University Press in 2021.
- Recent publications listed by Gothenburg include atmospheric particles, cloud-edge mixing, deep neural networks, uncertainty in neural-network predictions and applications of computational memory.
