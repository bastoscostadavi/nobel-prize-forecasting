## Biography

Born 11 September 1960 in Hamamatsu, Shizuoka. He received his B.Eng. (1983), M.Eng. (1985) and D.Eng. (1989) from Nagoya University, working with Isamu Akasaki. He became a research associate at Nagoya in 1988. When Akasaki's laboratory moved, he followed to Meijo University (1992–2010), rising to professor. He returned to Nagoya University as professor in 2010. His honours include the Rank Prize for Optoelectronics (1998), the 2014 Nobel Prize in Physics (with Akasaki and Nakamura) and the Order of Culture. He is a Fellow of the Japan Society of Applied Physics and the APS, an international member of the US National Academy of Engineering (2016), a foreign academician of the Chinese Academy of Engineering (2019), and a member of the Japan Academy (2022). He is also a special fellow at NIMS (Japanese Wikipedia).

## Research footprint

With Akasaki, Amano developed a low-temperature AlN buffer-layer method for growing high-quality GaN crystals by MOVPE. In 1989 he achieved p-type GaN by activating Mg-doped GaN through low-energy electron-beam irradiation (LEEBI). This allowed the first GaN p–n junction UV/blue LED. That materials work underlies efficient blue and white LED lighting. His Nobel biography describes years of failed attempts with Zn doping before the Mg breakthrough. In recent years his centre at Nagoya has turned to GaN for power electronics, high-frequency devices, and energy-saving semiconductor technology as part of a wider "future electronics" programme.

## Public statements and values

- "We can contribute to the energy savings for the humans, so I'm very very happy to contribute to the energy saving issues" (NobelPrize.org telephone interview, 2014).
- On Akasaki: "he teached me that the nitride, gallium nitride, was very, is very promising material for the blue LED. So I could concentrate on the gallium nitride and not other materials like zinc selenide and silicon carbide" (same).
- After the prize: "now I'm wondering how to act. Because many people are watching me so I should act very... responsible" (same).
- His laboratory was nicknamed the "castle that never sleeps" (不夜城) because its lights stayed on late (Japanese Wikipedia).
