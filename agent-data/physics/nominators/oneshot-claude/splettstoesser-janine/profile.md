## Biography

Splettstoesser studied physics at Karlsruhe, receiving a diploma in 2003 (ORCID). She then completed a joint doctorate in 2007: a *Perfezionamento* at Scuola Normale Superiore in Pisa and a Dr. rer. nat. at Ruhr-Universität Bochum. After a postdoc at the University of Geneva (2007–2009), she became an associate professor (W2) at RWTH Aachen (2009–2013). She moved to Chalmers as assistant professor in 2013, became associate professor in 2016 and full professor in 2019. She currently leads Chalmers' Nanoscience and Nanotechnology Area of Advance.

## Research footprint

Her group, "Dynamics and thermodynamics of nanoscale devices", studies how charge and heat move through driven and interacting quantum conductors. Her early work covered adiabatic quantum pumping and time-dependent transport through interacting quantum dots. Later she worked on single-electron sources and on electron quantum optics in the scattering-theory tradition of the Geneva school.

Her recent focus is quantum thermodynamics of nanoscale devices. Examples include:
- thermoelectric conversion and nanoscale heat engines;
- fluctuation–dissipation bounds out of equilibrium (Tesser & Splettstoesser, *PRL* 2024, an Editors' Suggestion);
- kinetic uncertainty relations for quantum transport (Palmqvist, Tesser & Splettstoesser, *PRL* 2025).

She has also moved into waveguide QED, for example with "giant superatoms" in work with Anton Frisk Kockum (*PRL* 2025). Her methods are scattering theory, real-time diagrammatics and master equations.
