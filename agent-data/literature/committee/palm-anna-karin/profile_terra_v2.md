## Career background

Born 1961 and raised in Stockholm. Attended a Waldorf school, then studied literature, philosophy and English at Stockholm University and began doctoral studies in literature (at Uppsala per Wikipedia), which she left for fiction. Debuted in 1991 with the novel *Faunen*. Has worked as a freelance publisher's editor, reader and Dagens Nyheter columnist, and served on the editorial board of the journal *80TAL/90TAL*. Awards include De Nio's Special Prize (1998), De Nio's Winter Prize (2015), the Lotten von Kræmer Prize (2019) and the Dobloug Prize (2022). Elected to the Academy in 2023 on seat 16, succeeding the poet and literary historian Kjell Espmark.

## Statements on record

- After the 2024 announcement, as co-opted member, she gave the Nobel Prize Museum interview: the laureate "writes intense, lyrical prose that is both tender and brutal," and uses history by letting characters reflect events "both then and in the present," showing "how the living and the dead are always intertwined."
- Academy essay for International Women's Day 2025 on the first women in the Academy (Lagerlöf, Wägner, Ekman): "När sedan fler kvinnor valdes in i Akademien började det också bli fler kvinnliga litteraturpristagare."
- On election (2023): "Det är omtumlande, men väldigt roligt och hedrande."

## Publications and literary practice

- **Fiction:** *Faunen* (1991; three stories from different eras forming one whole); *Utanför bilden* (1992, stories); *Målarens döttrar* (1997, breakthrough, translated widely), about artistic freedom and female identity; *Herrgården* (2005); *Snöängel* (2011), a novel linking 18th- and 20th-century Sweden; *Jaktlycka* (2014, stories on femininity, sexuality, independence and being objectified); *Trakten* (2025, stories set mostly in rural Hälsingland that mix the everyday with folk-tale tones).
- **Non-fiction:** *Om vänskap* (2007, dialogic essays with Kate Larson); *Jag vill sätta världen i rörelse* (2019), a large Selma Lagerlöf biography focused on her creative process (August Prize nominee); *Jag skriver över ditt ansikte* (2021), on her mother's Alzheimer's disease and family history.
- **Children's books:** *Lekplats* (1999) and the Vilma books.
- **Form (Academy):** parallel narrative structures, ekphrasis, myths and fairy tales; she is placed in "a British tradition of storytelling, in elective affinity with Virginia Woolf among others."

## Professional ties

Elected together with David Håkansson (2023). Collaborator Kate Larson. Has worked closely on Selma Lagerlöf, the first woman laureate and first woman Academy member. Committee colleagues Olsson, Mattson, Swärd, Sem-Sandberg.
