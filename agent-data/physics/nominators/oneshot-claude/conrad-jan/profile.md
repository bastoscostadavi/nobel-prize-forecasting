## Biography

According to Wikidata, Jan Conrad was born in 1973. INSPIRE records undergraduate study at Hamburg. Wikidata links him to the University of Bonn and lists Hans Peter Nilles and Rainald Flume as advisors, probably for a diploma. He completed a PhD at Uppsala University in 2003, with Allan Hallgren and Olga Botner listed as advisors (INSPIRE). He then held postdoctoral positions at KTH and at CERN. He is now Professor in Astroparticle Physics at Stockholm University, and ORCID lists him as Head of the Department of Physics. He was a member of the Young Academy of Sweden from 2011 to 2014 (Wikidata). From 2015 to 2018 he was a guest professor in SU's mathematics department (SU profile).

## Research footprint

Conrad's career runs through several major astroparticle and particle experiments (SU profile):
- the AMANDA/IceCube neutrino telescope (1999–2003);
- ALICE at CERN (2004–05);
- the PAMELA cosmic-ray experiment (2006–07).

Since then he has focused on searches for particle dark matter:
- gamma-ray searches with the Fermi Large Area Telescope and H.E.S.S.;
- deep-underground xenon experiments: XENON1T and XENONnT, and the planned XLZD;
- searches for axion dark matter with resonant cavities in the ALPHA project.

He has a strong methodological interest in statistics. Examples are confidence-interval construction with systematic uncertainties, profile-likelihood tests (Algeri, Aalbers, Morå & Conrad, *Nature Reviews Physics* 2020), and machine-learning-assisted event reconstruction. He collaborates with professional statisticians. Over time his focus has shifted from indirect astrophysical searches towards direct detection and axions.
