## Biography

Robert C. Myers studied at the University of Waterloo and received his PhD from Princeton University in 1986. After postdoctoral work at the Institute for Theoretical Physics (now KITP), UC Santa Barbara (1986–1989), he was a professor at McGill University (1989–2001). He was a founding faculty member of the Perimeter Institute in 2001, served as Faculty Chair (2011–2018) and as Director (2019–2024), and is now Director Emeritus. His honours include the CAP Herzberg Medal (1999), the CAP-CRM Prize in Theoretical and Mathematical Physics (2005), Fellowship of the Royal Society of Canada (2006), the CAP-TRIUMF Vogt Medal (2012), the CAP Medal for Lifetime Achievement in Physics (2023) and CAP Fellowship (2024). Clarivate listed him as a highly cited researcher in 2016, 2017 and 2021 (Perimeter; Wikipedia).

## Research footprint

Myers' early fame came from the Myers–Perry metric (1986, with Malcolm Perry), the rotating black hole solution in higher dimensions. In the late 1990s he worked on D-brane physics, including the dielectric-brane mechanism (the "Myers effect"), cited in his Herzberg Medal for contributions to "black hole microphysics and D-branes". After AdS/CFT he became a leading figure in holography: holographic renormalisation, higher-curvature gravity duals, holographic c-theorems (with Aninda Sinha), and a derivation of holographic entanglement entropy with Horacio Casini and Marina Huerta. From the 2010s he moved into quantum-information ideas in field theory and gravity, including circuit complexity in quantum field theory and holographic complexity. His 2024–2026 arXiv papers deal with modular flows, non-minimal Ryu–Takayanagi surfaces and holographic scattering, Rényi second laws for black holes, entanglement harvesting, flat-space entanglement and boundary duals of bulk detectors.
