## Biography

Italian experimental atomic physicist. He built his career at LENS and the University of Florence in Massimo Inguscio's quantum-gas school. He was involved in the first Bose–Einstein condensate of potassium, produced by sympathetic cooling (Science 2001), and in early degenerate Fermi–Bose mixtures. He is now a full professor at Florence and Director of LENS. The Director is appointed by the Rector for a renewable three-year term. He leads the Dysprosium (Dy) and potassium (K) quantum-gas experiments, run jointly between LENS and CNR-INO in Pisa, and holds an ERC Advanced Grant ("Supersolids"). Google Scholar reports about 15,000 citations and an h-index of 50.

## Research footprint

His early work covered degenerate Fermi–Bose mixtures of potassium and rubidium, including the collapse of a degenerate Fermi gas (Science 2002) and atom interferometry with trapped Fermi gases. With Feshbach-tunable 39K he made a BEC with tunable interactions and used it to observe Anderson localisation of a non-interacting BEC in a quasi-periodic lattice (Roati et al., Nature 2008), his most-cited paper. He then studied interaction-driven delocalisation (Nature Physics 2010) and an Efimov spectrum (Nature Physics 2009). Later work covered self-bound quantum droplets in mixtures (PRL 2018). With dysprosium, his group was among the first to observe dipolar supersolid states (PRL 2019). They then showed supersolid symmetry breaking from compressional oscillations (Nature 2019), superfluidity from non-classical rotational inertia (Science 2021), and a measurement of the superfluid fraction by the Josephson effect (Nature 2024).
