My best guess is **Jun Ye and Hidetoshi Katori**, for their pioneering work on **optical lattice clocks**.

These clocks use atoms trapped by laser light to measure time with extraordinary precision. Their work combines a clear experimental breakthrough with broad scientific consequences: better timekeeping, precise tests of relativity, and searches for new physics. That makes it a strong Nobel candidate.

My runner-up would be **Pablo Jarillo-Herrero, Allan MacDonald, and Rafi Bistritzer**, for “magic-angle” twisted bilayer graphene, where rotating two graphene sheets slightly produces remarkable electronic behavior, including superconductivity.

**Confidence: low.** Physics Nobel predictions are particularly difficult because the committee can recognize discoveries made decades ago. But if I had to choose one area for 2026, I’d choose optical clocks.
