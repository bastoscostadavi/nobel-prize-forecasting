## Biography

According to his ORCID record, Baldini studied electronic engineering (B.Sc., 2010) and quantum electronics (M.Sc., 2012) at the University of Pavia. He then earned a Ph.D. in physics at EPFL (2012–2017) and was a postdoctoral fellow at MIT (2017–2022) before joining UT Austin as an assistant professor in 2022. UT Austin reports that he is a faculty researcher in the Texas Quantum Institute, the Center for Complex Quantum Systems, the Texas Materials Institute and the Center for Dynamics & Control of Materials (an NSF MRSEC). His early-career awards include a Sloan Research Fellowship, an NSF CAREER award, an AFOSR Young Investigator award and a Keck Foundation award. He received the Ludwig-Genzel-Prize 2025 for condensed-matter spectroscopy.

## Research footprint

Baldini uses ultrafast optical, terahertz and photoemission probes to detect and control collective modes in correlated and magnetic materials. His EPFL-era papers (per ORCID) cover exciton and electron-phonon dynamics in anatase TiO2 and related materials. At MIT his work includes:

- first-authored "Discovery of the soft electronic modes of the trimeron order in magnetite" (*Nature Physics* 16, 2020, with N. Gedik);
- co-authorship of "Terahertz field-induced ferroelectricity in quantum paraelectric SrTiO3" (*Science* 364, 2019, K. A. Nelson group);
- time- and angle-resolved photoemission at 11 eV;
- excitons in van der Waals antiferromagnets such as NiPS3.

At UT Austin his focus has moved further toward nonlinear spectroscopy of magnetic systems. The Genzel prize committee cited the magnetoelectric coupling he measured in atomically thin multiferroics, the chiral magnetoelectric oscillations his group found there, and the natural optical activity of magnon modes in 2D materials. Recent papers include dispersive dark excitons in CrI3 and CrSBr (*PRX* and *PRL*, 2025), and terahertz-driven parametric downconversion of magnons (*Science Advances*, 2025).
