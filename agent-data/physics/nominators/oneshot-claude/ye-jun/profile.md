## Biography

Jun Ye was born in 1967 in Shanghai. He earned a BS in physics from Shanghai Jiao Tong University (1989), an MS at the University of New Mexico (1991, theoretical quantum optics with Marlan Scully) and a PhD at the University of Colorado Boulder (1997) with John L. Hall. After a Millikan postdoctoral fellowship at Caltech with Jeff Kimble (1997–1999), he joined JILA and NIST in 1999, becoming a JILA Fellow in 2001. He was elected to the US National Academy of Sciences (2011) and as a foreign member of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (2017). Honours include the I. I. Rabi Prize (2007), the Norman F. Ramsey Prize (2019), five Department of Commerce Gold Medals, a Vannevar Bush Fellowship (2022) and the 2022 Breakthrough Prize in Fundamental Physics (shared with Hidetoshi Katori) (Wikipedia; NIST).

## Research footprint

Ye's work sits at the junction of laser stabilisation, precision spectroscopy and ultracold matter. He contributed to the development of optical frequency combs and ultrafast laser techniques, and his group built strontium optical lattice clocks that repeatedly set records for stability and accuracy; JILA reported a frequency precision of 2.5 × 10⁻¹⁹ with a 3D quantum-gas clock in 2018 (Wikipedia). He pioneered, with the late Deborah Jin, the production of ultracold polar molecules and studies of their quantum chemistry and dipolar interactions. More recent directions include using clocks as quantum many-body systems and sensors (for example spin-squeezed and entangled clock ensembles), extending frequency combs into the vacuum ultraviolet and toward nuclear-transition spectroscopy, and tests of fundamental physics with precision measurement. His 2026 arXiv papers include comparisons of all-optical time scales, ultra-low-noise lasers for sub-hertz clock spectroscopy, a Fermi gas of polar molecules in the Pauli-blocked regime and high-purity VUV laser sources. He leads the NSF-funded Q-SEnSE quantum centre (NIST).
