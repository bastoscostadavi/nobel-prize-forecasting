## Biography

Frebel was born in Berlin in 1980 and grew up in Göttingen (Wikipedia). She began physics studies in Freiburg, then moved to Australia. There she completed a PhD in astronomy and astrophysics at the Australian National University's Mount Stromlo Observatory under John E. Norris; the thesis year is given as 2006 (Wikipedia) or 2007 (MIT). She was a W. J. McDonald Postdoctoral Fellow at the University of Texas at Austin (2006–2008/9) and a Clay Postdoctoral Fellow at the Harvard–Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics (2009–2011/12). She joined MIT in 2012, was tenured in 2018, became full professor in 2022 and has headed the Astrophysics Division since 2023. Her honours include:

- the Charlene Heisler Prize (2007);
- the Ludwig Biermann Award of the German Astronomical Society (2009);
- the AAS Annie Jump Cannon Award (2010);
- an NSF CAREER award (2013);
- a Science News "Ten Scientists to Watch" listing (2016);
- APS Fellowship (2022).

## Research footprint

Frebel uses high-resolution spectroscopy, often with the Magellan telescopes in Chile, to find and analyse the most metal-poor stars in the Milky Way and its dwarf satellites. These stars serve as records of the first stars and early nucleosynthesis (MIT). Discoveries include HE 1327-2326, one of the most iron-deficient stars known (2005), and HE 1523-0901, a strongly r-process-enhanced star with detected uranium (2007) whose age was estimated at about 13.2 billion years. Her most-cited paper (INSPIRE) is *Nucleosynthetic signatures of the first stars* (2005). With her student Alexander Ji she showed r-process enrichment from a single rare event in the ultra-faint dwarf galaxy Reticulum II (2016). She has contributed to broad r-process reviews connecting rare-isotope beam facilities with astrophysics. Recent work extends to ultra-faint dwarf galaxy metallicity distributions and Milky Way dark-matter profiles (2024–2026).
