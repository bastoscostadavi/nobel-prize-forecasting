## Biography

Thorlacius received a BS in physics from the University of Iceland (1984) and a PhD from Princeton (1989). INSPIRE lists Curtis Callan as his doctoral advisor. He was a postdoctoral scholar at Stanford (1989–1993) and at the Institute for Theoretical Physics, UC Santa Barbara (1993–1995), and an assistant professor at Princeton (1995–1999) (ORCID). He has been Professor of Physics at the University of Iceland since 1998. He was Director of Nordita, the Nordic Institute for Theoretical Physics, from 2008 to 2014, during its move to Stockholm. He was a guest professor at Stockholm University (2014–2019) and a visiting professor at Stanford (2004–05, 2018). No information on prizes or academy memberships was checked.

## Research footprint

Thorlacius's most-cited paper is *The stretched horizon and black hole complementarity* (Susskind, Thorlacius and Uglum, 1993; about 1,200 INSPIRE citations). It is one of the founding papers of the black-hole complementarity principle. Related work with Susskind, Russo, Uglum, Lowe and Polchinski covered the endpoint of Hawking radiation, evaporation in 1+1 dimensions, cosmic censorship in 2D gravity, gedanken experiments and complementarity versus locality (1992–1995). He also worked on D-branes and boundary CFT, including with Polchinski ("free fermion representation of a boundary CFT", 1994) and Klebanov ("the size of p-branes", 1996). With Ulf Danielsson he moved into Lifshitz holography around 2009–2010: black holes in Lifshitz spacetime and holographic superconductors. He later returned to black hole information. Recent work includes a Page curve for an evaporating black hole (2020), complexity of JT gravity (with Susskind and others, 2019) and 2025–2026 papers with David Lowe on four-dimensional black hole evaporation and the breakdown of semiclassical gravity. He has also written on spacetime from operator algebras (2026).
