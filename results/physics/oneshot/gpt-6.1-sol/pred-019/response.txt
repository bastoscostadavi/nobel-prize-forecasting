My speculative pick is **Michael Berry**, possibly sharing the prize with **Yakir Aharonov**, for their work on quantum phases—particularly the Berry phase and the Aharonov–Bohm effect.

These discoveries showed how geometry and electromagnetic potentials produce measurable quantum effects. They’ve become foundational across condensed-matter physics, optics, and quantum technology, giving them the combination of experimental confirmation and lasting influence that Nobel committees often recognize.

My confidence is low: nominations remain confidential for 50 years, and predicting the committee’s choice is difficult. **John Pendry and David R. Smith**, for metamaterials and negative refraction, would be another plausible pairing.
