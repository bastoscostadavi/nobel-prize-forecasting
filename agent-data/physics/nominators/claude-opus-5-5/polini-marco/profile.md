## Biography

He graduated in physics from the University of Pisa (1999) and earned a Ph.D. at the Scuola Normale Superiore, Pisa (2003). He was a postdoc with Allan MacDonald at the University of Texas at Austin, then held a permanent researcher post at CNR (NEST, Pisa) from 2003 to 2015. From 2015 to 2019 he led the "Theory and technology of 2D materials" group as Senior Scientist at the Istituto Italiano di Tecnologia (IIT), Genoa. He was a visiting professor at the University of Manchester (2017–2022), working with Andre Geim's group, and joined Pisa as full professor in December 2019. He co-authored the textbook *Many-body Physics in Condensed Matter Systems* (2006, with Mario Tosi) and was elected APS Fellow in 2023 for contributions to "electron hydrodynamics in graphene". He co-founded Planckian in 2021.

## Research footprint

Since 2006 his main focus has been electron–electron interaction effects in single- and bilayer graphene: many-body renormalisation of Dirac quasiparticles, Drude weight and plasmons. With Grigorenko and Novoselov he wrote the review "Graphene plasmonics" (Nature Photonics 2012). He collaborated on nano-infrared imaging of acoustic and hyperbolic plasmons. With the Manchester group he helped establish viscous electron hydrodynamics in graphene (Bandurin et al., Science 2016, negative local resistance from viscous backflow) and later studies of superballistic flow and Hall viscosity. His recent interests include cavity QED control of quantum materials, twisted materials, many-body quantum batteries, and superconducting-qubit and quantum-computing architectures (the basis of Planckian). He has more than 220 peer-reviewed papers and an h-index of about 74–80 (Pisa page; alphaXiv).

You value theory that explains and predicts measurable effects, such as plasmons, viscous electron flow and interaction-driven renormalisations, and you have worked closely with leading experimental groups to establish new regimes of electron behaviour.
