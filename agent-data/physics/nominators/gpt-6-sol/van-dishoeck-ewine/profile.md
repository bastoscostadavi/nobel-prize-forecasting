## Biography

Born 13 June 1955 in Leiden. She studied chemistry at Leiden and moved into astrochemistry. She did her PhD under Harm Habing (Leiden) and Alexander Dalgarno (Harvard) on the excitation and dissociation of molecules in interstellar clouds, and was a Junior Fellow of the Harvard Society of Fellows (1984). She became Professor of Molecular Astrophysics at Leiden Observatory. She was scientific director of the Netherlands Research School for Astronomy (NOVA, from 2007) and President of the International Astronomical Union (2018–2021). She has co-edited the Annual Review of Astronomy and Astrophysics since 2012. Her honours include the Spinoza Prize (2000), the Gothenburg Lise Meitner Prize (2014), the Albert Einstein World Award of Science (2015), the James Craig Watson Medal (2018), the Kavli Prize in Astrophysics (2018), the Karl Schwarzschild Medal (2019), the Prix Jules Janssen (2020), the Fritz Zwicky Prize (2022) and the Niels Bohr International Gold Medal (2022). She is a member of the KNAW and the US National Academy of Sciences (both 2001), the Leopoldina (2013) and the American Philosophical Society (2020), and was appointed to the Pontifical Academy of Sciences (2021).

## Research footprint

With John Black she showed how self-shielding and dust shielding govern the photodissociation of interstellar CO ("The photodissociation and chemistry of interstellar CO", ApJ 1988). This work is foundational for interpreting CO, the main tracer of molecular gas. She went on to combine laboratory astrophysics, chemical modelling and observations to follow molecules from cold clouds through protostars and discs into planet-forming material. She led the Herschel key programme "Water in Star-forming Regions with Herschel" (WISH; overview in PASP 2011). She has used submillimetre and infrared facilities such as ALMA and JWST to trace ices, complex organic molecules and water. Leiden describes her "current passion" as "the hunt for water and the water cycle in space." She is widely described as among the most cited molecular astrophysicists.
