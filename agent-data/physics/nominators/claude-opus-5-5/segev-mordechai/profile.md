## Biography

Segev was born in Romania in 1958 and moved to Israel at age three. He earned his B.Sc. (1985) and Ph.D. (1990) at the Technion. He was a postdoc at Caltech and then joined Princeton in 1994 as an assistant professor, becoming full professor in 1999. He returned to the Technion in 1998 and has held the Shillman Distinguished Professorship since 2009. His honours include:
- EPS Quantum Electronics Prize (2007)
- Max Born Award (2009)
- Arthur L. Schawlow Prize in Laser Science (2014)
- Israel Prize (2014)
- EMET Prize (2019)
- Rothschild Prize in Physics (2024)

He is a member of the Israel Academy of Sciences and Humanities (2011), the US National Academy of Sciences (2015) and the American Academy of Arts and Sciences (2021) (Wikipedia).

## Research footprint

In the 1990s Segev discovered photorefractive spatial solitons and incoherent ("random-phase") solitons, showing that even white light can self-trap. His group went on to find solitons in two-dimensional photonic lattices. With Schwartz, Bartal and Fishman, he observed transverse Anderson localization of light in disordered 2D photonic lattices (*Nature* 446, 2007). He helped found topological photonics: the helical-waveguide "photonic Floquet topological insulators" (Rechtsman et al., *Nature* 496, 2013) and the topological insulator laser (Bandres et al., *Science* 359, 2018, with the Khajavikhan and Christodoulides groups). His homepage lists current topics:
- topological photonics and artificial gauge fields
- light and disorder (hyper-transport, deep-subwavelength disorder)
- sparsity-based sub-wavelength imaging and super-resolution, with Oren Cohen and Yonina Eldar
- curved-space photonics inspired by general relativity
- nonlinear optofluidics and nanophotonics

The arc runs from nonlinear waves, to wave transport in periodic and disordered media, to topology and quantum and structured light.
