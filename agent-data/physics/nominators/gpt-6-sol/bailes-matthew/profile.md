## Biography

Matthew Bailes earned his PhD at the Australian National University in 1989 with a thesis on the origin of pulsar velocities. He has been at Swinburne University of Technology's Centre for Astrophysics and Supercomputing since at least 1998 (INSPIRE) and is Director of OzGrav, the ARC Centre of Excellence for Gravitational Wave Discovery. He received an ARC Laureate Fellowship in 2015 to work on fast radio bursts. He was elected Fellow of the Australian Academy of Science in 2022 and shared the 2023 Shaw Prize in Astronomy with Duncan Lorimer and Maura McLaughlin for the discovery of fast radio bursts. He received the 2024 Prime Minister's Prize for Science (Wikipedia).

## Research footprint

Bailes works on pulsars and the instruments used to find and time them. His early work was on the birth and evolution of binary and millisecond pulsars. He was a co-author of the 2007 Science paper by Lorimer et al. that reported the first fast radio burst ("A bright millisecond radio burst of extragalactic origin"), and of the 2013 Science paper by Thornton et al. showing a population of FRBs at cosmological distances. He helped build pulsar timing arrays to detect nanohertz gravitational waves (International Pulsar Timing Array, 2010; Parkes PTA background search, 2023). He also works on precision tests of gravity with relativistic binaries (e.g. PSR J1738+0333, 2012). As an OzGrav member he appears on the LIGO/Virgo GW170817 papers. He has driven real-time digital signal-processing systems, including the re-engineering of the Molonglo Observatory Synthesis Telescope for FRB and pulsar work, and sits on the CASPER advisory board (Wikipedia). His 2026 papers cover MeerKAT pulsar timing, the Thousand-Pulsar-Array programme, FRB searches with FAST, and JAX-based pulsar timing software.
