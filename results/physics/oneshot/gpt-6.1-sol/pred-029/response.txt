My best guess is **Jun Ye and Hidetoshi Katori**, for their pioneering work on **optical lattice clocks**.

These clocks measure time using the optical frequencies of atoms held in laser-generated traps. They achieve extraordinary precision, enabling sensitive tests of relativity, measurements of gravitational effects, and searches for changes in fundamental constants. The field has the combination I’d look for in a Nobel contender: decades of development, demonstrated results, and a major impact on experimental physics.

My strongest alternative is **Yakir Aharonov and Michael Berry**, for discoveries concerning quantum phases—particularly the Aharonov–Bohm effect and the Berry phase.

**Confidence: low.** Nobel nominations remain confidential for 50 years, so this is a judgment about Nobel-worthy work, not knowledge of the committee’s shortlist. But if I had to make one prediction, I’d choose **Ye and Katori**.
