## Biography

Lukin was born in Moscow on 10 October 1971. He graduated from the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology in 1993 and received his PhD from Texas A&M University in 1998 under Marlan Scully. His thesis was "Quantum Coherence and Interference in Optics and Laser Spectroscopy" (Wikipedia). He was a visiting scientist at MPQ Garching and later a fellow and co-director of ITAMP at the Harvard–Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics. He joined Harvard's faculty in 2001, became full professor in 2004, and was named Joshua and Beth Friedman University Professor in 2023. He co-directs the Harvard Quantum Initiative and the Harvard–MIT Center for Ultracold Atoms (Optica). His honours include the Adolph Lomb Medal (2000), the APS Rabi Prize (2009), an Alexander von Humboldt Professorship (2009), NAS membership (2018), the Optica Townes Medal (2021) and the APS Ramsey Prize (2022). He co-founded QuEra Computing and Quantum Diamond Technologies.

## Research footprint

His early theoretical work on electromagnetically induced transparency (EIT) produced key results on how atomic ensembles interact with light (Optica). These included dark-state polaritons and the storage and retrieval of light pulses (the "slow/stopped light" program). He co-proposed Rydberg-blockade quantum gates and many-atom dipole blockade. His group later built optical systems that are nonlinear at the single-photon level, including "photonic molecules" with Vladan Vuletić (2013). In solid-state physics, the group developed nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centre quantum sensing and nanoscale magnetometry. In the late 2010s the lab turned to programmable Rydberg-atom arrays in optical tweezers. That platform produced a 51-atom quantum simulator (2017), studies of topological/spin-liquid states, and, from 2023, demonstrations of error-corrected logical qubits. The trajectory runs from theoretical quantum optics toward large-scale experimental quantum simulation and fault-tolerant quantum computing.
