## Biography

Pearce was born in the United Kingdom in 1970 and completed a PhD in experimental particle physics through the University of Birmingham and CERN. He moved to Sweden in 1996 as a guest researcher at KTH and became professor of astroparticle physics there in 2007. He has held educational and administrative responsibilities in KTH's physics organisation and School of Engineering Sciences. In 2010 the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences awarded him the Wallmark Prize for instrument development associated with findings on antimatter in cosmic radiation. He was elected to the Academy's Class for Physics in January 2020. Public reporting placed him at the 2023 and 2025 physics-prize announcements, and the official 2026 roster identifies him as committee Chair.

## Research / intellectual footprint

- His doctoral background is in experimental particle physics at an accelerator laboratory; his later work shifted to instruments flown on satellites and high-altitude balloons.
- He co-authored results from the PAMELA satellite, including measurements of cosmic-ray positrons, antiprotons, protons, helium nuclei and electrons.
- He is a co-author of the Fermi Large Area Telescope instrument paper and of proposed gamma-ray mission studies.
- He led the PoGOLite and PoGO+ balloon experiments for hard-X-ray polarimetry, which measured sources including the Crab and Cygnus X-1.
- He has led Swedish participation in XL-Calibur and has described plans for a small-satellite gamma-ray polarimeter aimed at gamma-ray bursts and black-hole formation [S4].
- This record is included solely to document experience in detectors, collaborations and astroparticle observations.
