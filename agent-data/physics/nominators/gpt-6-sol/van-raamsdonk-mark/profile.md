## Biography

Van Raamsdonk earned a combined honours B.Sc. in mathematics and physics at UBC (1995). He then earned an M.Sc. (1997) and Ph.D. (2000) at Princeton under Washington Taylor, with the thesis "Making the most of zero branes and a weak background." After a postdoc at Stanford (2000–2002), he joined UBC as an assistant professor and Canada Research Chair in 2002. He became associate professor in 2007 and full professor in 2013. His honours include:

- a Sloan Fellowship (2005–2009);
- first prize in the Gravity Research Foundation essay competition (2010);
- the CAP/CRM Medal in Theoretical and Mathematical Physics (2014);
- a Simons Investigator award (2015–2020);
- a UBC Killam Faculty Research Award (2016).

He is a member of the Simons "It from Qubit" collaboration.

## Research footprint

His early work addressed D-brane matrix theory and noncommutative field theory. With Shiraz Minwalla and Nathan Seiberg he wrote "Noncommutative Perturbative Dynamics" (JHEP, 2000), which introduced UV/IR mixing. With Aharony, Marsano, Minwalla and Papadodimas he studied the Hagedorn/deconfinement transition in weakly coupled large-N gauge theories (2004). From a 2009 sabbatical onward (*Nature* news, 2015) he turned to the link between entanglement and spacetime. His essay "Building up spacetime with quantum entanglement" (*Gen. Rel. Grav.*, 2010) argued that disentangling boundary degrees of freedom pulls apart the dual spacetime. The idea is widely associated with ER=EPR. With Faulkner, Guica, Hartman and Myers he derived the linearised Einstein equations from entanglement in holographic CFTs (JHEP, 2014). Later work covers black-hole microstate cosmology and holographic cosmologies. He wrote *Lectures on Gravity and Entanglement* (2016).
