## Biography

Mølmer was born in Vejle, Denmark, in 1963. He received his cand.scient. degree in 1987 and his PhD in 1990, both from Aarhus University. He was a Humboldt postdoctoral fellow at the Max Planck Institute of Quantum Optics in Garching. He returned to Aarhus as associate professor in 1991, became full professor in 2000, and moved to the Niels Bohr Institute in 2022. He has held visiting professorships at Université Paris-Sud and Innsbruck. His honours include:
- the EliteForsk Award (2007);
- APS Fellowship (2008);
- the Villum Kann Rasmussen Annual Award (2012);
- the Humboldt Research Award (2023);
- the UNSW Dirac Medal (2023);
- the Carlsberg Foundation Research Prize (2025).

## Research footprint

Mølmer is a theorist of quantum optics, atomic physics and quantum information. Two contributions are especially widely used.

- **Monte Carlo wave-function method (quantum-jump method).** Developed with Yvan Castin and Jean Dalibard, it is a standard way to simulate dissipative quantum systems under continuous measurement.
- **Mølmer–Sørensen gate.** Proposed with Anders Sørensen in 1999, this entangling gate for trapped ions is insensitive to thermal motion. It became a standard tool in ion-trap quantum computing, including commercial platforms (Wikipedia).

He has also worked on:
- measurement theory and conditional dynamics, including "past quantum states";
- entanglement depth and spin squeezing;
- quantum metrology;
- quantum networks and hybrid systems.

His 2026 papers cover adiabatic state preparation, entangled phase sensing with Ana Maria Rey's group, superradiance, time crystals and quantum resource theory (KU portal).
