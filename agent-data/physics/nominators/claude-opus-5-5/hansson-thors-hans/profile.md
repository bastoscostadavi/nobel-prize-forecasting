## Biography

Born 1950. PhD in 1979 at the University of Gothenburg with a thesis in elementary-particle theory ("Multiquark states"). Professor of theoretical physics at Stockholm University, working at AlbaNova/Nordita. He became director of Nordita in early 2016 (English Wikipedia, citing Nordita). Elected to the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences in 2009. Nobel Committee for Physics member from about 2015, chair in 2021, and still a member in 2022. At the 2016 physics prize announcement he gave the committee's explanation, using a cinnamon bun, a bagel and a pretzel to illustrate topology, which was widely reported. He is active in science popularisation, including newspaper articles and the Swedish sceptics' magazine *Folkvett*.

## Research footprint

He began in particle theory (multiquark states, hot QCD correlators with Ismail Zahed, the QCD trace anomaly, and 2D QCD in the early 1990s). He then moved decisively into condensed matter, starting with anyons: coherent-state path integrals, anyons on higher-genus surfaces, the Calogero model (with Brink, Konstein and Vasiliev), and field theory of anyons in the lowest Landau level (1994). His main contributions concern the fractional quantum Hall effect: field theories of partially polarised states, the quantum Hall matrix model, conformal-field-theory constructions of composite-fermion and hierarchy wave functions (with Viefers, Hermanns, Jain and Regnault), the thin-torus/Tao–Thouless limit (with Karlhede and Bergholtz), Hall viscosity, and the review "Quantum Hall physics: hierarchies and CFT techniques" (arXiv 2016, with Hermanns, Simon and Viefers). He also argued that "Superconductors are topologically ordered" (with Oganesyan and Sondhi, 2004) and has worked on topological field theory of p-wave superconductors, chiral superconductors (with Wilczek), exclusion statistics, QFT anomalies in condensed matter, and fractional charge versus statistics. He remains productive in 2024–2026.
