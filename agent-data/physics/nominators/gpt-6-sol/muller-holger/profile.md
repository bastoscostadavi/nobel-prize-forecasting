## Biography

According to his Berkeley page, Müller filed his first successful patent at 14. He did his undergraduate thesis with Jürgen Mlynek at the University of Konstanz. He took his doctorate at Humboldt University Berlin with Achim Peters as advisor. With an Alexander von Humboldt fellowship, he joined Steven Chu's group at Stanford as a postdoc. He joined the Berkeley physics faculty in July 2008 and is now a full professor. He is also affiliated with QB3 and Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory. His honours and academy memberships were not retrieved from the sources available.

## Research footprint

Müller's group uses atomic, molecular and optical techniques to probe nature at the highest sensitivity. With Peters and Chu he reinterpreted atom-interferometer gravimetry as a precision measurement of the gravitational redshift (*Nature* 2010), which drew published debate. At Berkeley his group measured the fine-structure constant with atom interferometry as a test of the Standard Model (Parker et al., *Science* 2018). It set constraints on chameleon dark energy (Hamilton et al., *Science* 2015, with Justin Khoury). It also held atoms in an optical-lattice interferometer for 20 seconds and later about a minute to probe gravity (Xu et al., *Science* 2019; Panda et al. 2023). Current projects include drone-mounted mobile gravimeters and interferometers designed to test whether gravity can entangle masses. A second line of work uses intense continuous-wave lasers to shape free-electron wavefunctions for laser phase-contrast cryo-electron microscopy, done with Robert Glaeser and Yifan Cheng. The group is also developing label-free optical readout of neuronal action potentials and buffer-gas-loaded optical dipole traps meant as a "universal" trap for atoms and molecules (Berkeley page).
