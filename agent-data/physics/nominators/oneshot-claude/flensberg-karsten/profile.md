## Biography

These details come from the CV on his KU portal. Flensberg earned his PhD at the Technical University of Denmark (1987–1989). He was a postdoc at the University of Tennessee and Oak Ridge National Laboratory (1989–91) and then at Nordita (1991–93). He held a Carlsberg Fellowship at MIC, DTU (1993–95) and was a staff scientist at Danish Fundamental Metrology (1995–98). He joined the Niels Bohr Institute as associate professor in 1999 and became professor in 2012. He was a visiting professor at Cornell (2002–03) and Harvard (2011–12), and a DPG Mercator Fellow in 2019. Since 2019 he has directed QDev, a Danish National Research Foundation centre of excellence. Since 2020 he has been a PI on an ERC Synergy grant.

## Research footprint

Flensberg's research covers quantum effects in electron transport through nanoscale devices. His keywords include mesoscopic physics, molecular electronics, spintronics and quantum information. He co-wrote the graduate textbook *Many-Body Quantum Theory in Condensed Matter Physics* with Henrik Bruus.

For roughly the past fifteen years his main subject has been topological superconductivity in superconductor–semiconductor hybrids and Majorana bound states. This includes:
- introductory and review articles on Majorana physics (with Martin Leijnse, and with Felix von Oppen and Ady Stern);
- scalable designs for Majorana-based qubits;
- readout and braiding protocols.

His 2026 papers cover non-Abelian braiding of imperfect Majorana states, parity control of Cooper-pair tunnelling in hybrid superconducting qubits, and Andreev crystals in Josephson junction arrays (KU portal). His work is closely tied to experiments in Copenhagen and has moved progressively from transport theory towards quantum-device architecture.
