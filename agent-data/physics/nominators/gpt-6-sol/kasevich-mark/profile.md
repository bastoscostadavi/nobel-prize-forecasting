## Biography

Kasevich received a BA in physics from Dartmouth College (1985). As a Rhodes Scholar at Oxford he took a BA in physics and philosophy (1987), and he earned a PhD in applied physics at Stanford (1992) (Stanford Bio-X; Wikidata). He joined Stanford's physics faculty in 1992, was on the Yale physics faculty from 1997 to 2002, and returned to Stanford in 2002. He is now William R. Kenan, Jr. Professor of Physics and Applied Physics. He received the APS I. I. Rabi Prize (2003) for atom-interferometer inertial sensors of unprecedented precision and for pioneering Bose–Einstein condensate studies, including non-classical spin states and a mode-locked atom laser. He has an entry in the National Academy of Sciences member directory.

## Research footprint

As a graduate student at Stanford he developed light-pulse atom interferometry using stimulated Raman transitions (Kasevich & Chu, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 1991). That technique became the basis for cold-atom gravimeters, gyroscopes and gradiometers. His later work applied these sensors to precision tests of gravitation and quantum mechanics, including very long-baseline atom interferometry in a 10 m fountain. He also studied squeezing and entanglement in BECs and entangled-state metrology (Bio-X profile). His interests have broadened to quantum-optimal imaging, including high-speed fluorescence-lifetime microscopy, multipass electron microscopy and multipass Raman microscopy, and to networked entangled atomic clocks and interferometers. The Applied Physics profile also lists ultrastable lasers, ultrafast electron sources and quantum simulation with atoms. The Q-SEnSE page adds ultrafast laser-induced phenomena and advanced microscopy.
