## Biography

Friberg was born in 1967 in Gothenburg. He earned an MSc (1992) and a PhD (1997) at SSE; Robert Feenstra was the discussant on his thesis, *Prices, profits and exchange rate uncertainty*. He was a Fulbright scholar at MIT (1994–95) and a Wallander postdoc at SSE (1998–2000). At SSE he was assistant professor (2000), docent (2001) and professor (2008), and he has been Jacob Wallenberg Professor since 2009. He had visiting spells at UC Berkeley (1999), Princeton (2002) and IFN (2008). He edited the Scandinavian Journal of Economics (2009–13) and was an associate editor until 2019. At SSE he headed the Department of Economics (2014–17) and the PhD programme (2010–14), and he sat on the SSE board (2020–21). He has organized the Ohlin Lectures since 2023. His honours include the IO Society's Lanzillotti Prize (2012), the SJE "Best paper of 2024" award, and SSE teaching awards (2008, 2014).

## Research / intellectual footprint

- **International pricing.** "In which currency should exporters set their prices?" (JIE 1998) and a questionnaire study on export invoicing currency (JIE 2007). He has also written on the law of one price in Scandinavian duty-free stores (AER 2001) and on market integration.
- **Price-setting microfoundations.** He surveyed Swedish firms on price adjustment (JMCB 2005) and studied gasoline retail price adjustment. His latest paper, "Menu Costs and Asymmetric Price Adjustment" (IJIO 2026), is co-authored with Ellingsen and Hassler.
- **Demand and cross-border shopping.** "Demand and distance" (JPubE 2006). Natural-experiment work on alcohol and commodity-tax revenue (SJE 2024; RIE 2025).
- **Competition policy.** A beer-market divestiture case (IJIO 2015). A review of algorithmic pricing in fuel markets for the Swedish Competition Authority (2024). An SNS report on digitalization and competition (2021).
- **Risk and uncertainty.** *Managing Risk and Uncertainty* (MIT Press 2015). A paper on risk and ambiguity in 10-Ks (JCF 2017). "Corporate responses to stock price fragility" (JFE 2024). An essay on Knightian uncertainty (Critical Review 2025).
- **Teaching.** An open-access textbook, *Microeconomics: An Open Introduction* (Routledge 2025).
- **Method:** applied micro-econometrics on transaction-level and firm-level data, surveys of firms, and natural experiments, grounded in IO theory.
