## Research footprint

Alava is a statistical physicist who works on disorder, fluctuations and non-equilibrium dynamics in real materials. His most-cited works, according to Semantic Scholar, include:

- the reviews *Statistical models of fracture* (with Nukala and Zapperi, Advances in Physics, 2006), *The physics of paper* (with Niskanen, Reports on Progress in Physics, 2006) and *Imbibition in disordered media* (2004);
- lattice-Boltzmann work on permeability of random fibre webs (1998);
- papers on crackling noise and avalanches, e.g. the universality of average avalanche shape (Nature Communications, 2013) and dislocation avalanches (PRL, 2013);
- network science, including clustering and structural transitions in scale-free networks (with Szabó and Kertész).

He has also worked on artificial spin ice and evolutionary game dynamics. In 2020 he co-authored a widely cited multidisciplinary study modelling aerosol transport and SARS-CoV-2 exposure. Over time his work has moved from interface growth and paper physics toward plasticity, dislocation dynamics and materials for extreme environments (the NOMATEN link). Throughout, it uses simulation, scaling theory and experiment together.
