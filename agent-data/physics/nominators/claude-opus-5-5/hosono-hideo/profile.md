## Biography

Hideo Hosono was born on 7 September 1953 in Kawagoe, Saitama Prefecture. He graduated in industrial chemistry from Tokyo Metropolitan University (1977) and earned a doctorate in engineering there in 1982. He became a research associate at the Nagoya Institute of Technology in 1982 and associate professor in 1990, then moved to Tokyo Institute of Technology as associate professor (1993) and professor (1999) in the Materials and Structures Laboratory. He led the ERATO Hosono Transparent Electro-Active Materials project. Honours include the Otto Schott Research Award (1991), W. H. Zachariasen Award (1994), Bernd T. Matthias Prize (2009), Medal with Purple Ribbon (2009), Asahi Prize (2010), Nishina Memorial Prize (2012), Imperial Prize of the Japan Academy (2015), Japan Prize (2016), Foreign Member of the Royal Society (2017), MRS Von Hippel Award (2018) and Eduard Rhein Technology Award (2022).

## Research footprint

Hosono's career is built on designing electronic function in materials that others thought unpromising (inference based on the Wikipedia summaries):

- **Transparent oxide semiconductors:** p-type CuAlO₂ films (Nature 1997), a single-crystal transparent-oxide TFT (Science 2003), and "Room-temperature fabrication of transparent flexible thin-film transistors using amorphous oxide semiconductors" (Nomura et al., Nature 2004). The amorphous In-Ga-Zn-O (IGZO) TFT became an industrial display technology.
- **Electrides:** converting the cement constituent 12CaO·7Al₂O₃ into a conductor, a stable electride and eventually a superconductor (Nature 2002; Science 2003), and electride-supported ammonia-synthesis catalysis (Kitano et al., Nature Chemistry 2012).
- **Iron-based superconductors:** "Iron-Based Layered Superconductor La[O1−xFx]FeAs (x = 0.05–0.12) with Tc = 26 K" (Kamihara, Watanabe, Hirano, Hosono; JACS 2008), which opened a new family of high-Tc superconductors. Science named iron-based superconductivity among its Breakthroughs of the Year (ja-Wikipedia).

He has around 450 papers and about 70 patents (de-Wikipedia, dated).

## Public statements and values

No direct quotes were verified. Wikipedia summaries emphasise that his discoveries often went against conventional wisdom, for example that iron, being magnetic, should suppress superconductivity. His "element strategy" work aims to replace rare elements with abundant ones.
