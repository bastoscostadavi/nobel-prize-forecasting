## Biography

Soljačić was born in Zagreb on 7 February 1974 and attended the XV Gymnasium (MIOC) there. He received BSc degrees in physics and electrical engineering from MIT (1996), then an MA (1998) and PhD in physics (2000) from Princeton under Mordechai Segev (Wikipedia). He joined MIT's physics faculty in 2005 and holds the Cecil and Ida Green Professorship. His honours include the Optica Adolph Lomb Medal (2005), MIT Technology Review TR35 (2006), a MacArthur Fellowship (2008), a Blavatnik National Award (2014) and Optica's Max Born Award (2023). He co-founded WiTricity (2007), Lightelligence (2017) and Axiomatic (2024), and he holds more than 100 US patents (Wikipedia).

## Research footprint

His work centres on electromagnetic phenomena in structured materials (Wikipedia). His PhD work was on nonlinear optics and solitons. In 2007 his group showed efficient non-radiative wireless power transfer over about 2 m by strongly coupled magnetic resonances: a 60 W bulb was lit at roughly 40% efficiency. That technology went to WiTricity. With John Joannopoulos and co-workers he helped build topological photonics. Their work includes observing backscattering-immune one-way edge states in photonic crystals (2009), a *Nature Photonics* review on topological photonics (2014), and experimental Weyl points in photonic crystals (2015). Other lines of work cover bound states in the continuum, nanophotonic light emission and thermal control, and optical neural networks for deep learning (2017). Since about 2017 he has focused more and more on AI. He co-authored the Kolmogorov–Arnold Network (KAN) architecture (2024) and works on AI for scientific discovery (ICFO colloquium title).
