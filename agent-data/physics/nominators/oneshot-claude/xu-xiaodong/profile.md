## Biography

Xu received his Ph.D. in physics from the University of Michigan in 2008 and was a postdoctoral researcher in physics at Cornell University from 2009 to 2010. From 2010 to 2026 he was on the University of Washington faculty, with appointments in Physics and in Materials Science and Engineering. In 2026 he moved to Stanford as Professor of Applied Physics. His honours include a U.S. Department of Energy Early Career award (2012, according to UW news), the 2025 National Academy of Sciences Award for Scientific Discovery and the 2025 Dan Maydan Prize in Nanoscience Research. No academy memberships were verified.

## Research footprint

Xu's Ph.D. work, co-authored with Duncan Steel, Dan Gammon and Allan Bracker, used coherent optical spectroscopy of single charged InAs quantum dots. It includes optical spin cooling (PRL 2007), coherent population trapping of a single electron spin, and optical locking of the nuclear field (Nature 2009). At UW he moved to atomically thin semiconductors and helped build valleytronics. Key papers include "Magnetic control of valley pseudospin in monolayer WSe2" (Nature Physics 2014) and the Nature Physics review "Spin and pseudospins in layered transition metal dichalcogenides" (2014, with Wang Yao, Di Xiao and Tony Heinz). His group co-discovered layer-dependent ferromagnetism in CrI3 down to the monolayer (Nature 2017) and went on to study van der Waals magnets, moiré excitons and moiré magnetism. In 2023 his group reported signatures of fractional quantum anomalous Hall states in twisted MoTe2 (Nature) and the observation of the fractionally quantized anomalous Hall effect (Nature), along with a fractional Chern insulator paper in PRL. His Stanford profile lists his current interests as anyon quantum matter, interacting topological phases, quantum magnetism, and optical control and readout of correlated systems. His methods combine device fabrication, magneto-optics and quantum transport.
