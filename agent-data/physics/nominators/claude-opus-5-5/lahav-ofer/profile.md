## Biography

Lahav was born in Tiberias, Israel, in 1959 and holds Israeli and British citizenship. He studied physics at Tel Aviv University (BSc 1980) and Ben-Gurion University (MSc 1985). He earned a Ph.D. in astronomy at Cambridge in 1988; INSPIRE lists George Efstathiou and Donald Lynden-Bell as advisors. He was on the staff of Cambridge's Institute of Astronomy from 1990 to 2003 and was a Fellow of St Catharine's College. At UCL he has been Head of Astrophysics (2004–2011), Vice-Dean for Research (2011–2015), Vice-Dean (International) and Co-Director of the STFC CDT in Data Intensive Science. He was Vice-President of the Royal Astronomical Society (2010–2012) and served on the STFC Science Board (2016–2019). His honours include a Royal Society Wolfson Research Merit Award, an ERC Advanced Grant (2012–2018), the RAS Gerald Whitrow Lecture (2014) and the George Darwin Lecture (2020).

## Research footprint

Lahav's early work covered large-scale structure. It includes "Dynamical effects of the cosmological constant" (1991, with Lilje, Primack and Rees), Wiener reconstruction of large-scale structure (1994), redshift-distortion measurements from IRAS surveys, and stochastic galaxy biasing (with Dekel, 1999). He took part in the 2dF Galaxy Redshift Survey era. He pioneered machine-learning photometric redshifts with ANNz (2004, with Collister). He helped found the Dark Energy Survey, co-chaired its Science Committee until 2016 and chairs DES:UK and DESI:UK. Other notable results include an upper bound of 0.28 eV on neutrino masses from photometric surveys (2010) and "A buyer's guide to the Hubble constant" (2021). He co-authors the Particle Data Group review "The Cosmological Parameters" (2025 edition, with Cortês and Liddle). His recent work focuses on testing ΛCDM against dynamical dark energy in light of DESI results, and on machine learning for cosmology.
