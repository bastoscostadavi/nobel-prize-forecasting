## Biography

Born 20 November 1976. He took an MSc in applied physics at the University of Groningen (1999) and a PhD at TU Delft (2005) under Leo Kouwenhoven. He was then a postdoc with David Awschalom at UC Santa Barbara (2005–2007). He returned to Delft as assistant professor in 2007 and became full professor in 2012. He was one of the four founding professors of QuTech (2014) and served as its Scientific Director (2016–2020). He also chaired the executive board of Quantum Delta NL (QDNL, 2021–2023), a national quantum programme worth €615 million, and co-founded Delft Networks (2024). His honours include the Nicholas Kurti European Science Prize (2012), the KNAW Ammodo Science Award (2015), the John Stewart Bell Prize (2017), the Spinoza Prize (2019) and the Physica Prize (2022). He is a member of the KNAW and KHMW and an APS Fellow.

## Research footprint

His PhD work on single electron spins in semiconductor quantum dots led to the review "Spins in few-electron quantum dots" (Hanson, Kouwenhoven, Petta, Tarucha and Vandersypen, RMP 2007). From about 2006 he turned to NV centres in diamond, controlling electron spins, nearby nuclear spins and spin–photon interfaces. His Delft team achieved:

- unconditional quantum teleportation between distant solid-state qubits (Pfaff et al., Science 2014)
- the first loophole-free Bell inequality violation, using electron spins 1.3 km apart (Hensen et al., Nature 2015)
- a multi-node quantum network (2021)
- qubit teleportation between non-neighbouring network nodes (Hermans et al., Nature 2022)

The focus has shifted from single-spin physics toward quantum-network architecture, entanglement distribution over metropolitan distances and the building of a quantum internet.
