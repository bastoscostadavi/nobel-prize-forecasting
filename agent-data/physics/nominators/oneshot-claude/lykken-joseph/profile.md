## Biography

Joseph David Lykken was born in 1957 in Minneapolis, the son of psychologist David T. Lykken. He received his PhD from MIT in 1982 with a thesis on gauge-invariant formulations of large-N QCD, supervised by Roman Jackiw. After postdoctoral work he joined the Fermilab Theory Division in 1989. He served as Fermilab's Deputy Director from July 2014 to September 2022, also acting as Chief Research Officer, and has since led Fermilab's quantum science effort, first as head of the Fermilab Quantum Institute and then of the Quantum Division. He is a Fellow of the APS and the AAAS, a former chair of the APS Division of Particles and Fields and a former trustee of the Aspen Center for Physics (Wikipedia; Fermilab News).

## Research footprint

Lykken's early career was in beyond-Standard-Model theory. In 1983, with Lawrence Hall and Steven Weinberg, he wrote an influential paper on supergravity as the messenger of supersymmetry breaking, a foundation of the minimal supersymmetric standard model phenomenology. In 1996 he proposed "weak-scale superstrings", in which extra dimensions could be accessible at collider energies. He joined the CMS experiment at the LHC and is an author of the 2012 Higgs-discovery paper; with Maurizio Pierini, Chris Rogan and Maria Spiropulu he developed the "razor" kinematic variables for new-physics searches. Since about 2018 his focus has shifted to quantum information science applied to high-energy physics: quantum sensing for dark matter, the MAGIS-100 atom-interferometer project, quantum machine learning for collider analysis, a pedagogical review "Quantum Information for Particle Theorists" (2020), and, with Spiropulu, Daniel Jafferis and Google Quantum AI, the 2022 "traversable wormhole dynamics" experiment on a Sycamore processor and its 2024 long-range follow-up (Wikipedia; arXiv).
