My best guess is **Charles L. Kane, Eugene J. Mele, and Laurens W. Molenkamp**, for their work on **topological insulators and the quantum spin Hall effect**.

Their case combines influential theoretical predictions with experimental confirmation: materials whose interiors insulate while their boundaries carry unusually robust electrical currents. The discoveries have had nearly two decades to demonstrate their importance—a strong fit for how the Nobel often recognizes physics.

My runner-up would be **Pablo Jarillo-Herrero, Allan MacDonald, and Rafi Bistritzer**, for twisted bilayer graphene, where rotating two atomically thin sheets produces remarkable electronic behavior, including superconductivity.

Confidence is low: Nobel deliberations are secret, and even strong candidates can wait decades. But if I had to choose one slate for 2026, I’d choose **Kane–Mele–Molenkamp**.
