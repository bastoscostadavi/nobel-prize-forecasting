## Biography

He is German. He studied at Imperial College London and RWTH Aachen, earning a master's degree in 1997 for work at Forschungszentrum Jülich on solitons in stacked Josephson junctions. He then did a PhD at the University of Erlangen-Nuremberg (2000) under Alexey Ustinov, on soliton and vortex dynamics in superconductors. From 2002 to 2005 he was a postdoc and then associate research scientist in Robert Schoelkopf's group in Yale's Department of Applied Physics. He joined ETH Zurich as a professor in January 2006. His honours include the Nicholas Kurti Science Prize (2006), ETH's Max Rössler Prize (2011) and a Helmholtz International Fellow Award (2020). He has held ERC grants (2009, 2013) and chaired the strategy commission of ETH's physics department.

## Research footprint

As first author with Schoelkopf, Girvin, Blais and others, he demonstrated strong coupling of a single microwave photon to a superconducting qubit (Nature 2004). This was the founding experiment of circuit QED. At ETH his group developed microwave quantum optics with circuits, including photon-correlation measurements, Hong-Ou-Mandel interference and deterministic quantum teleportation. It also developed hybrid systems coupling circuits to Rydberg atoms and semiconductor quantum dots. Later work targets scalable quantum computing, most notably repeated quantum error correction in a distance-three surface code (Krinner et al., Nature 2022). The group also works on cryogenic microwave links between separate dilution refrigerators. These links enabled a loophole-free Bell test with superconducting circuits across a 30-metre cryogenic channel (Storz et al., Nature 2023) and, in 2026, certified randomness amplification with Renato Renner's group. With Blais, Grimsmo and Girvin he co-wrote the Reviews of Modern Physics article on circuit QED (2021).
