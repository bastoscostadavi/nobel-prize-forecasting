## Biography

He was born in Nishiwaki, Hyōgo, in 1954. All his degrees are from the University of Tokyo: B.S. in applied physics (1976), M.S. (1978) and PhD (1981). He rose through Tokyo's physics and applied-physics departments and was Professor of Applied Physics from 1995. He led a series of national projects:
- JRCAT group leader, 1993–2002;
- director of AIST's Correlated Electron Research Center, 2001–2008;
- two JST-ERATO projects: Spin Superstructure (2001–2007) and Multiferroics (2006–2012);
- RIKEN groups from 2007;
- founding director of the RIKEN Center for Emergent Matter Science from 2013 (Wikipedia).

His honours include the Nishina Memorial Prize (1990), Bernd T. Matthias Prize (1991), Asahi Prize (2002), APS McGroddy Prize (2005) and IUPAP Magnetism Award/Néel Medal (2012). He has also received the Imperial Prize of the Japan Academy (2013), Person of Cultural Merit (2020) and the Leo Esaki Award (2023). He is a member of the Japan Academy (2022) and a Foreign Member of the Royal Society (2023).

## Research footprint

He is one of the most prolific experimentalists in correlated-electron physics. His early work covered Mott transitions and high-Tc cuprates, including electron-doped cuprates. In the 1990s he pioneered colossal magnetoresistance in manganites. In the 2000s he helped found the modern field of spin-driven multiferroics (Tokura, Seki and Nagaosa, *Rep. Prog. Phys.* 77, 2014). In 2010 his team made the real-space observation of a two-dimensional skyrmion crystal by Lorentz TEM (Yu et al., *Nature* 465, 901), with about 3,300 Crossref citations. Skyrmions in a multiferroic insulator followed (Seki et al., *Science*, 2012). With Naoto Nagaosa he reviewed skyrmion topology and dynamics (*Nat. Nanotechnol.* 8, 2013; about 3,900 citations).

Recent work covers:
- emergent electromagnetic induction (*Nature*, 2020);
- giant topological Hall effects;
- current-driven skyrmion dynamics (*Nature*, 2024);
- magnetic Weyl semimetals;
- topological-insulator heterostructures.

RIKEN reported a "p-wave magnet" result in 2026. Throughout, the theme is "emergent" phenomena and functions arising from strong electron correlation and spin textures.
