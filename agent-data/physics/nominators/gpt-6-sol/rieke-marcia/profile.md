## Biography

Marcia Jean Rieke (née Keyes) was born on 13 June 1951 in Hillsdale, Michigan, and grew up in Midland. She earned her B.S. (1972) and Ph.D. (1976) in physics at MIT, with the thesis "The Distribution of Celestial Infrared Sources." She went to the University of Arizona as a postdoc in 1976 and has stayed there, becoming Regents' Professor of Astronomy. She was vice-chair for program prioritisation of the Astro2010 decadal survey. Her memberships and awards include:

- American Academy of Arts and Sciences (2007);
- National Academy of Sciences (2012);
- American Philosophical Society (2025);
- NASA Distinguished Public Service Medal and the Bruce Medal (2023);
- Gruber Cosmology Prize (2024);
- AAS Henry Norris Russell Lectureship (2025).

(Wikipedia)

## Research footprint

Rieke helped found modern infrared astronomy. Wikipedia reports that the AAS Committee on the Status of Women called her, together with Judith Pipher, one of its "founding mothers." Her early career centred on building infrared cameras at Steward Observatory. She then:

- served as a co-investigator on Spitzer's MIPS instrument;
- was deputy PI of Hubble's NICMOS;
- was Principal Investigator of JWST's Near Infrared Camera (NIRCam) from 2002 to 2022.

Her science covers infrared observations of galactic nuclei and of galaxies in the early universe. JWST's deep extragalactic surveys with NIRCam are the most recent stage of this work. Her Gruber citation recognises her space-based infrared instrumentation and its role in studying early galaxies. Over time her work has moved from ground-based detector development to leading major space instruments and the high-redshift science they enable.
