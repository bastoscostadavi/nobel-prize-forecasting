## Biography

Philippe Grangier was born on 12 March 1957 in Tarare. He studied at the École Normale Supérieure de Cachan and did his doctorate under Alain Aspect at the Institut d'Optique in Orsay. He took part in the early-1980s Bell-inequality experiments and co-authored the 1982 PRL "Experimental Realization of Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen-Bohm Gedankenexperiment" (Aspect, Grangier, Roger). He has led the quantum optics group at the Institut d'Optique since 1988 (now in Palaiseau). He is a CNRS research director and professor at École Polytechnique. Honours include the Carl Zeiss Research Award (1990), CNRS Silver Medal (2002), Prix Lazare Carnot (2005), the International Quantum Communication Award (2006, with Zoller, Cirac and Wootters), Prix Jean Ricard (2008), the OSA Charles Hard Townes Award (2012), the Grand Prix SFO Léon Brillouin (2013) and the Prix Ampère (2023).

## Research footprint

- **Foundations and single photons:** the Aspect–Grangier–Roger Bell tests (1982) and subsequent single-photon interference experiments.
- **Continuous-variable QKD:** "Quantum key distribution using gaussian-modulated coherent states" (Grosshans, Van Assche, Wenger, Brouri, Cerf, Grangier; Nature 2003) founded CV-QKD. The technology was commercialised through SeQureNet.
- **Non-classical states:** "Generating Optical Schrödinger Kittens for Quantum Information Processing" (Ourjoumtsev, Tualle-Brouri, Laurat, Grangier; Science 2006).
- **Single atoms:** "Sub-poissonian loading of single atoms in a microscopic dipole trap" (Schlosser et al., Nature 2001), and "Observation of collective excitation of two individual atoms in the Rydberg blockade regime" (Gaëtan et al., Nature Physics 2009, with Browaeys). This line helped seed today's optical-tweezer Rydberg platforms.
- **Foundations (recent):** "Contexts, Systems and Modalities: A New Ontology for Quantum Mechanics" (Auffèves & Grangier, Foundations of Physics 2015) and follow-up work with philosopher Nayla Farouki.

## Public statements and values

The LCF foundations page says the work aims to "expliciter les principes physiques qui sous-tendent la mécanique quantique" (make explicit the physical principles underlying quantum mechanics) and to demystify it without losing rigour. It links to a CNRS Le Journal interview titled "Donner du sens à la mécanique quantique" (making sense of quantum mechanics). He advocates a realist, contextual reading of quantum theory (the CSM approach).
