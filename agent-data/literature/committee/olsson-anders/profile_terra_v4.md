## Education and appointments

Born 1949 (Huddinge per the Academy; Stockholm per Wikipedia). Studied Nordic languages, literary history and philosophy; co-founded the cultural journal *Kris* in the late 1970s and has worked as a critic since then. Debuted in 1981 with the essay collection *Mälden mellan stenarna*; PhD 1983 with *Ekelöfs nej*. Professor of literature at Stockholm University from 2004. Elected to the Swedish Academy in 2008 (seat 4, succeeding Lars Forssell). Served as permanent secretary 1 June 2018 – 31 May 2019, during the Academy's crisis after Sara Danius's departure, and has chaired the Nobel Committee since 1 June 2019. Awarded H.M. the King's Medal (12th size, Seraphim ribbon) in 2022 for services to Swedish academies.

## Affiliations and relationships

Horace Engdahl (post-structuralist criticism); Daniel Birnbaum (co-author, *Den andra födan*, 1992); the *Kris* circle. Chaired the committee through the post-2018 reforms: external members 2019–20, then Academy members only, with anonymous regional experts, from 2021. Co-opted permanent secretaries Mats Malm (to 2025) and Ingrid Carlberg (2026).

## Attributed statements

- As chair he has given the ceremony presentation speech in 2019, 2020, 2022, 2023 and 2025, and presented the laureate after the 2021, 2023 and 2024 announcements.
- On criteria (TNR, 2021): "Literary merit: That is the absolute and the only criterion for us." On Handke: the debate "did not concern his literary merit." Announced that from 2022 the committee would add international expert reports on Asian and African literature, where "we do not have deep competence within the academy."
- On breadth (Nobel video, Oct 2019): "Previously we had more, let's say, Eurocentric perspective of literature, and now we are looking all over the world"; "Previously, it was much more male-oriented."
- 2021 announcement: "Gurnah's dedication to truth and his aversion to simplification are striking."
- 2023 speech (Fosse): "Jon Fosse is not a difficult writer. He uses the simplest of words"; praised simplicity that "gains depth and intensity through repetition and variation" and the connection between "strong local ties" and belief in contemporary literature.
- 2025 speech: praised "a flowing syntax" and "an artistic gaze, entirely free of illusion ... with an unwavering faith in the power of literature."

## Subjects and forms in public work

- **Translation and poetry:** translated Celan's late work (*Det sena verket*, 2020); seven poetry collections from *Dagar, aska* (1984) to *men så oändligt lätt att svara dig* (2010), low-voiced, intense and existential in the Academy's description.

- **Hermeneutics and reading:** *Den okända texten* (1987, interpretation theory from the Church Fathers to Derrida), *Tankar om läsning* (2015, drawing on Proust); with Horace Engdahl, helped bring post-structuralism into Swedish criticism (Wikipedia).

- **Exile literature:** *Ordens asyl* (2011; English *Languages of Exile*, 2013 per Wikipedia), covering Ovid, Joyce, Beckett, Sebald, Nelly Sachs and Paul Celan.

- **The fragment:** *Skillnadens konst* (2006), from German Romanticism to contemporary Swedish poetry (Frostenson, Jäderlund).

- **Nihilism and mysticism:** *Läsningar av Intet* (2000), on the death of God and poetic nihilism from Romanticism on, with Meister Eckhart, Schopenhauer, Nietzsche, and readings from Baudelaire to Birgitta Trotzig.

- **Finland-Swedish and Swedish modernism:** edited Gunnar Björling's writings (five vols, 1995) and wrote *Att skriva dagen* (1995); *Ekelunds hunger* (1996) on Vilhelm Ekelund; work on Stagnelius (*Vad är en suck?*, 2013).

- **Gunnar Ekelöf:** dissertation *Ekelöfs nej* (1983), monograph *Gunnar Ekelöf* (1997), editor of the Academy's edition of Ekelöf's collected poems (2015).
