## Biography

Cundiff graduated in physics from Rutgers in 1985. He then worked as an associate scientist at SciTec, Inc. in Princeton from 1985 to 1987. At the University of Michigan he earned an MS (1991) and a PhD in applied physics (1992) under Duncan Steel, with a thesis on picosecond excitonic nonlinearities in disordered quantum wells. He was a postdoc at the University of Marburg (1993–94) and a member of technical staff at Bell Laboratories (1995–97). In 1997 he joined NIST's Quantum Physics Division at JILA in Boulder, with an adjoint appointment at the University of Colorado, and he was chief of the division from 2004 to 2009. He returned to Michigan in the mid-2010s as Harrison M. Randall Collegiate Professor. His honours include:
- DOC Gold (2001), Bronze (2009) and Silver (2013) medals;
- a Humboldt Research Award (2010);
- the OSA William F. Meggers Award (2011);
- the APS Arthur L. Schawlow Prize (2019).

He is a fellow of AAAS, APS, Optica (OSA) and IEEE (Wikipedia).

## Research footprint

Cundiff works on ultrafast optical technology and nonlinear light–matter interaction. At NIST/JILA in the late 1990s and 2000s he helped develop femtosecond optical frequency combs. With Jun Ye he co-authored the standard reference *Femtosecond Optical Frequency Comb: Principle, Operation and Applications*. His other main line is optical multidimensional coherent spectroscopy (MDCS), the optical analogue of multidimensional NMR. His group extended it from molecules to semiconductor nanostructures and atomic vapours. Results include:
- probing many-body exciton interactions in quantum wells (PRL 2006);
- detecting collective double-quantum resonances in atomic vapours (PRL 2012);
- 3D Fourier-transform spectra (Nat. Commun. 2013);
- discovering the "dropleton", a quantum droplet of electrons and holes (*Nature* 2014, with Kira and Koch);
- frequency-comb-based MDCS and tri-comb spectroscopy (*Science* 2017; *Nature Photonics* 2018).

He co-authored the monograph *Optical Multidimensional Coherent Spectroscopy*. Recent preprints apply these methods to excitons in monolayer transition-metal dichalcogenides and to quantum-interference photocurrents.
