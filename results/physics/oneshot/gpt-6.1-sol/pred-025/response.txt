My pick is **Charles L. Kane, Eugene J. Mele, and Laurens W. Molenkamp**, for the prediction and experimental discovery of **topological insulators**.

These materials have insulating interiors but conducting surfaces or edges, with properties protected by their quantum structure. Kane and Mele provided foundational theory; Molenkamp’s group demonstrated the predicted behavior experimentally.

They’re strong candidates because the work combines a major conceptual advance with convincing experiments and has had nearly two decades to establish its influence. The main complication is that the 2016 Physics Nobel already recognized related work on topological phases, although these contributions are distinct.

**Confidence: low.** Nobel selections are exceptionally hard to predict. My alternative would be **Peter Shor**, for quantum algorithms—potentially as part of a broader quantum-information award.
