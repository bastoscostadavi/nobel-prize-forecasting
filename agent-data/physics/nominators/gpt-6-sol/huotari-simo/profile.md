## Biography

Huotari received his PhD in physics from the University of Helsinki in 2003 (ORCID). ORCID records a University Lecturer post in Helsinki's Department of Physics from 2011. He is now Professor of Physics there, holds the title of Docent, and serves as Head of the Department of Physics (research portal). He supervises in the Doctoral Programme in Science. His portal fields are materials science, solid-state physics, synchrotron radiation, X-ray tomography, X-ray scattering and spectroscopy, and X-ray free-electron lasers. ## Research footprint

Huotari works on inelastic X-ray scattering as a probe of electronic structure. His most-cited papers, according to Semantic Scholar, include:

- *Direct tomography with chemical-bond contrast* (Nature Materials, 2011), which used X-ray Raman scattering for imaging;
- work on the momentum distribution and renormalization factor in sodium and the electron gas (PRL, 2010);
- studies of the structure of water under high pressure and temperature (PNAS, 2013);
- the X-ray emission spectroscopy of a pressure-induced magnetic transition in siderite.

A major strand is instrumentation. He led or co-authored papers on multi-element and large-solid-angle X-ray Raman spectrometers, including the one at beamline ID20 of the European Synchrotron Radiation Facility (J. Synchrotron Rad. 2017). He also worked on diced analyser crystals with position-sensitive detectors for resonant inelastic scattering. More recently his group has developed laboratory-based X-ray absorption spectroscopy, applied *operando* to catalysts and battery cathode materials (2022 papers in Angewandte Chemie and Materials Today Energy). The trajectory runs from fundamental electron-gas and condensed-matter spectroscopy at large facilities toward accessible lab-scale methods for energy materials.
