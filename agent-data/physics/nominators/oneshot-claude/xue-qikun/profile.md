## Biography

Born December 1963 in Mengyin, Shandong. He earned a B.Sc. in laser physics at Shandong University (1984) and taught at Qufu Normal University (1984–1987). He then received his M.Sc. and Ph.D. (1994) at the Institute of Physics, Chinese Academy of Sciences (IOP-CAS). From 1992 to 1999 he worked at the Institute for Materials Research, Tohoku University, and at North Carolina State University. He returned to IOP-CAS as a professor in 1999 and directed its State Key Laboratory for Surface Physics. He moved to Tsinghua in 2005. There he was physics department chair, dean of sciences, and Vice President for Research (2013–2020). He became President of SUSTech in November 2020. He was elected a CAS member in 2005 and a foreign member of the US National Academy of Sciences in 2024. His honours include the TWAS Prize (2010), the Future Science Prize (2016), the Fritz London Memorial Prize (2020), the Oliver E. Buckley Prize (2024, the first to a Chinese national) and China's State Preeminent Science and Technology Award (for 2023).

## Research footprint

Xue combines molecular beam epitaxy (MBE) with in-situ STM/STS and ARPES to grow atomically precise films and probe their quantum states. In early work he studied quantum size effects in ultrathin metal films, including superconductivity modulated by film thickness (Science 2004, with Enge Wang, Qian Niu and others). His best-known result is the first experimental observation of the quantum anomalous Hall effect in magnetically doped (Bi,Sb)2Te3 films (Chang et al., Science 2013). The Tsinghua–IOP team on that paper included Ke He, Yayu Wang, Li Lu, Xu-Cun Ma, and theorists Shou-Cheng Zhang, Xi Dai and Zhong Fang. He also discovered interface-enhanced high-temperature superconductivity in single-unit-cell FeSe films on SrTiO3 (Chinese Physics Letters 2012). His work links surface science, topology, magnetism and superconductivity, with a strong emphasis on building new instruments.
