## Biography

Sachdev was born on 2 December 1961 in New Delhi and went to school in Bangalore. He spent a year at IIT Delhi and then transferred to MIT (BS 1982). He earned his PhD at Harvard in 1985 under David R. Nelson (Wikipedia). He worked at Bell Labs (1985–1987) and at Yale (1987–2005), then returned to Harvard. He chaired Harvard's physics department around 2018 (INDIA New England News) and has held visiting chairs at Perimeter Institute. His honours include NAS membership (2014), the UNSW Dirac Medal (2015), the APS Lars Onsager Prize and the ICTP Dirac Medal (both 2018), the American Academy of Arts and Sciences (2019), and Foreign Membership of the Royal Society (2023). His book *Quantum Phases of Matter* won a 2024 PROSE Award. He has been Editor-in-Chief of *Reports on Progress in Physics* since 2022.

## Research footprint

Sachdev's research studies how quantum entanglement shapes the macroscopic properties of matter, especially near quantum phase transitions (Wikipedia). His monograph *Quantum Phase Transitions* (Cambridge, 1999) became a standard reference. With Jinwu Ye he introduced the random-exchange model now known as the Sachdev–Ye–Kitaev (SYK) model (1993). Its later links to black-hole horizons and holography opened a bridge between condensed matter and quantum gravity. He co-authored the "deconfined quantum criticality" proposal (Senthil, Vishwanath, Balents, Sachdev, Fisher, *Science* 2004). He has worked extensively on the cuprate phase diagram, the pseudogap, fractionalized Fermi liquids and strange-metal transport, and more recently on universal theories of strange metals with spatially random interactions. His emphasis has moved from spin-model and critical-point theory toward holographic and SYK approaches to non-Fermi liquids, while keeping close contact with experiments on correlated materials.

## Public statements and values

- In 2018 he urged Indian scientists to speak up against pseudoscientific claims: "It is very harmful to make false claims about India's contribution. In fact, great harm is done to the eager young minds in India" (INDIA New England News, 24 Nov 2018).
- The same interview reports him saying there are "enough great achievements to make all Indians proud" without "unnecessary unscientific claims."
- Harvard recognized his mentoring with the Star Family Prize for Excellence in Advising (2016) (same source).
