## Biography

Viefers was born on 28 March 1970 in Orsoy, Germany, and moved to Norway as a teenager (Wikipedia). She took all her degrees at the University of Oslo: bachelor's (1992), master's (1993) and doctorate (1997), with a thesis titled "Field theory of anyons and the fractional quantum Hall effect". She was a postdoc at NORDITA in Copenhagen (1998–2000) and a visiting scientist at Jyväskylä (1999). She was then a postdoc and assistant professor at Chalmers (2000–2002). She returned to Oslo as associate professor (2003–2007) and became full professor in 2007. She was a guest professor at Stockholm University in 2008 (UiO page). She was elected to the Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters in 2017 (Wikipedia). She now heads the Department of Physics, and her page says that for now she is not teaching or supervising students. She has served on the NORDITA board (NORDITA board listing).

## Research footprint

Her UiO page lists her interests as quantum effects in low-dimensional systems (quantum Hall effect, topological phases, anyons, cold atoms), quantum technology, and physics education research. Her main theoretical work is on anyon field theory and the fractional quantum Hall effect. She also worked on the analogy between rotating Bose–Einstein condensates and quantum Hall physics, including a 2008 review in *J. Phys.: Condens. Matter*, on composite-fermion constructions, and on quantum rings and few-body systems with Matti Manninen and Stephanie Reimann. With T. H. Hansson, M. Hermanns and S. H. Simon she co-wrote a *Reviews of Modern Physics* article (2017) on quantum Hall hierarchies and conformal-field-theory techniques. Since roughly 2015 she has also published physics education research on how secondary and university students understand quantum concepts (e.g. Bøe & Viefers, *Science & Education* 2021). Her work has shifted from technical many-body theory toward education and departmental leadership.
