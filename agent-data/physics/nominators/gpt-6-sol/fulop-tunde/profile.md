## Biography

English Wikipedia says she was born in 1970 in Transylvania, Romania, of Hungarian ethnicity. Her Chalmers engineering-physics studies ran 1991–1994 (ORCID), and she took her PhD at Chalmers in 1999. She was assistant professor in the Department of Electromagnetics (2000–2003) and associate professor in Radio and Space Science (2004–2009). She has been full professor in the Department of Physics since 2009, and she has been a visiting research fellow at Merton College, Oxford. Other roles and honours:
- member of the Young Academy of Sweden, 2011–2016;
- elected to the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences, 2019;
- member of the Royal Society of Arts and Sciences in Gothenburg, 2021;
- EPS Hannes Alfvén Prize, 2024, shared with Per Helander.

## Research footprint

Her central topic is runaway electrons in tokamaks. These are relativistic electron beams that can form during disruptions and damage reactor walls. With co-workers she has studied:
- kinetic instabilities driven by runaway beams (Fülöp, Pokol, Helander and Lisak, *Phys. Plasmas* 13, 2006);
- the effective critical electric field for runaway generation (Stahl et al., *PRL* 114, 115002, 2015);
- the effect of partially ionised impurities and radiation on the critical field (Hesslow et al., *PPCF*, 2018).

Her group developed kinetic and fluid runaway-electron modelling tools. It now applies them to disruption mitigation with pellet and gas injection in next-generation devices such as SPARC and reactor-scale spherical tokamaks (arXiv 2022–2025). A parallel line covers laser–plasma interaction and wakefield excitation, for example work on autoresonant beat-wave wakefields (2024). The Alfvén Prize citation credits contributions "yielding groundbreaking results that significantly impact the understanding and optimization of magnetically confined fusion plasmas."
