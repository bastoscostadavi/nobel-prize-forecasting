## Biography

McDermott's early publications place him at UC Berkeley in John Clarke's group. He was lead author of the SQUID-detected microtesla NMR and MRI papers with Clarke and Alexander Pines (*Science* 2002; *PNAS* 2004), which suggests Berkeley PhD training with Clarke (inference). He then worked in John Martinis's superconducting-qubit group, as lead author of "Simultaneous State Measurement of Coupled Josephson Phase Qubits" (*Science* 2005). He is now Roeske Professor of Physics at UW–Madison and is affiliated with the Wisconsin Quantum Institute (UW directory). He co-founded the quantum-computing company Qolab with John Martinis and others, and serves as its Head of Hardware (Qolab team page). Honours and dates of appointments were not retrieved; no CV was accessible.

## Research footprint

McDermott's lab works on the technologies needed to scale superconducting processors to thousands or millions of qubits for error correction (lab page). Its main areas are:

- **Quantum coherence.** This includes dielectric defects (two-level systems) and quasiparticle poisoning from pair-breaking photons (Liu et al., *PRL* 2024).
- **Correlated errors from ionising radiation.** The lab showed correlated charge noise and relaxation errors in qubit arrays (Wilen et al., *Nature* 2021). It later measured such noise at an underground facility.
- **Scalable control.** It uses single-flux-quantum digital logic, including multi-chip SFQ control of qubits.
- **Quantum measurement and hybrid systems.** It works with Mark Saffman on Rydberg atoms and with Mark Eriksson on Si/SiGe spin qubits. It also contributes to dark-matter detection with qubit-based sensors.

His early work spanned SQUID-based ultralow-field NMR and MRI and phase-qubit measurement.
