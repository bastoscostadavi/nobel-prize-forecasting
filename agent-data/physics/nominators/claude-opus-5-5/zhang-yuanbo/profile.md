## Biography

Born July 1978 in Xinyang, Henan. He studied physics (with a second degree in economics) at Peking University (B.S. 2000). He earned his Ph.D. at Columbia University (2006) under Philip Kim. He was a Miller Research Fellow at UC Berkeley (2006–2009) and a postdoc at IBM Almaden (2010). He joined Fudan's physics department in 2011 (Wikipedia gives 2009). He became a Changjiang Scholar (2016) and Xie Xide Chair Professor (2020). He received a New Cornerstone Investigator award in 2023. His honours include the IUPAP Young Scientist Prize in semiconductor physics (2010), the Qiushi Outstanding Young Scholar Award (2013), the Nishina Asia Award (for graphene electronic properties), the Shanghai Natural Science Award First Prize (2020, black phosphorus) and the Xplorer Prize (2020).

## Research footprint

As a graduate student with Philip Kim, Zhang co-authored the 2005 Nature paper that observed the anomalous quantum Hall effect and Berry's phase in graphene, published alongside Geim and Novoselov's paper. At Berkeley he studied graphene by scanning tunnelling spectroscopy. At Fudan, with Xianhui Chen (USTC) and collaborators, he introduced few-layer black phosphorus as a high-mobility 2D semiconductor (Nature Nanotechnology 2014). He followed this with quantum oscillations and the quantum Hall effect in black phosphorus. He also showed gate-tunable phase transitions in 1T-TaS2 using ionic gating (2015). His group reported the quantum anomalous Hall effect in the intrinsic magnetic topological insulator MnBi2Te4 (Deng et al., Science 2020) and high-temperature superconductivity in monolayer cuprates. His methods are exfoliation, device fabrication, ionic-liquid/solid gating and low-temperature, high-field transport.
