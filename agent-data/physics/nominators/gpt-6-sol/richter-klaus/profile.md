## Biography

Klaus Richter was born in Kiel on 26 December 1962. He studied physics at Kiel and Freiburg (diploma 1988) and received his doctorate at Freiburg in 1991 under John S. Briggs, on Rydberg states of helium. After a visit to MPI for Solid State Research in Stuttgart, he was a Humboldt postdoctoral fellow at Université Paris-Sud, Orsay (1992–1994). He then worked at Augsburg, where he habilitated in 1998 on "Semiclassical Theory of Mesoscopic Quantum Systems". He led a junior research group at MPI for the Physics of Complex Systems in Dresden (1996–2001) and has held a chair at Regensburg since 2001. He has been spokesperson of the DFG SFB "Emergent Relativistic Effects in Condensed Matter" since 2017. His honours include the Physik-Preis Dresden (2019), a DFG Reinhart Koselleck grant (2021) and UNICUM "Professor of the Year" (2022). In the DPG he chaired the Dynamics and Statistical Physics division (2006–2009) and the Condensed Matter section (2012–2015), served on the Executive Board (2017–2021) and was President (2024–2026).

## Research footprint

Richter works on semiclassical approaches linking classical dynamics to quantum behaviour. Early work covered two-electron atoms (with Tanner and Rost, RMP 2000) and orbital magnetism in ballistic systems (with Ullmo and Jalabert, Physics Reports 1996). With Martin Sieber he found the "Sieber–Richter" pairs of correlated periodic orbits ("Correlations between periodic orbits and their rôle in spectral statistics", Physica Scripta 2001). This result helped explain, semiclassically, why chaotic systems follow random-matrix statistics, and it was extended to transport ("Semiclassical Theory of Chaotic Quantum Transport", PRL 2002). His recent work spans:

- many-body quantum chaos (Bose–Hubbard systems, quantum scars, Fock-space random waves);
- links between random-matrix universality and low-dimensional quantum gravity (JT and topological gravity, 2022–2025);
- transport in graphene cavities and strained graphene;
- topological-insulator nanowires, HgTe Josephson junctions and constrictions;
- "controlling quantum chaos".

## Public statements and values

As incoming DPG President (2024) he said that, after a century of quantum mechanics, "wir heute … möglicherweise an der Schwelle zu einer zweiten Quantenrevolution" stehen (we may stand at the threshold of a second quantum revolution). He named quantum technology, AI and climate change as priorities. He warned about climate change: "es ist Eile geboten!" He also described physics as a "Kompass" for policy and media. During his presidency the DPG issued a joint climate appeal with the German Meteorological Society (2025).
