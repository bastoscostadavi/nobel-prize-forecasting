## Career and public service

Born 9 December 1949 on a farm in Trøgstad, Østfold. She was known as Anne Enger Lahnstein from 1975 to 2006. She trained as a nurse at Diakonhjemmet (1972), qualified as a social worker and deacon (1975), and taught nursing from 1975 to 1978. She was secretary of the Centre Party's parliamentary group (1979–85) and Storting member for Akershus (1985–2001). She led the Centre Party from 1991 to 1999. In the first Bondevik government (1997–99) she was Minister of Culture and standing deputy to the Prime Minister, and she was acting Prime Minister from August to September 1998.

After leaving politics she was Secretary General of the Norwegian Sea Rescue Society (2001–03), County Governor of Østfold (2004–15), chair of the Equal Pay Commission (2006–08), and chair of the National Theatre (2009–17) and Sykehuset Innlandet (2016–20). She received the Trollkjerringprisen in 1999.

## Published work and public activity

- **Sovereignty and democracy:** She led the "No" side in the 1994 EU referendum and was nicknamed "Nei-dronningen". SNL says she "emphasised constitutional and democratic arguments". She wrote *EF og Grunnloven* (1993).
- **Centrist coalition-building:** she worked to bring the centre parties (Sp, KrF, Venstre) together, which led to the 1997 Bondevik government (SNL).
- **Foreign affairs experience:** member of the Storting foreign and constitutional committee (1989–93), secretary of the foreign affairs committee (1993–97), and deputy chair of the defence committee (1999–2000).
- **Social and ethical questions:** she led the secretariat of Folkeaksjonen mot fri abort (1978–79) and wanted abortion decisions made by a board rather than by the woman alone. Later she worked on equal pay (*Den lille forskjellen*, 2009). Memoir: *Grønn dame, rød klut* (2001).
- Christian-social background (Diakonhjemmet). She volunteers in her local parish (2026 register).
- No published writing on disarmament or international peace research was found.
