My pick is **Charles L. Kane, Eugene J. Mele, and Laurens W. Molenkamp**, for predicting and experimentally demonstrating the **quantum spin Hall effect**, a foundational discovery in topological insulators.

Kane and Mele’s 2005 theory described materials whose interiors insulate while their edges conduct through states protected by time-reversal symmetry. Molenkamp’s team demonstrated the effect in 2007. It’s a strong Nobel combination: a striking theoretical prediction, experimental confirmation, and lasting influence.

The main caveat is that the 2016 Physics Nobel already recognized theoretical discoveries involving topology, which might make the committee favor another area.

My runner-up would be **Pablo Jarillo-Herrero, Allan MacDonald, and Rafi Bistritzer** for magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene.
