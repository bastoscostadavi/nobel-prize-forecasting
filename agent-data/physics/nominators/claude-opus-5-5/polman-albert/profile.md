## Biography

Albert Polman was born on 21 April 1961 in Groningen. He obtained his physics master's (1985) and PhD in materials science (1989) at Utrecht University, then was a postdoc at AT&T Bell Laboratories (1989–1991). He has been a group leader at AMOLF since 1991, a department head from 1999, and (per Wikipedia) director of AMOLF from 2006; he founded AMOLF's Center for Nanophotonics (2005) and co-initiated the Amsterdam nanoCenter (2003), and spent a sabbatical in Harry Atwater's group at Caltech (2003–2004). He was elected to the Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences (KNAW) in 2009, received the MRS Innovation in Materials Characterization Award (2014) and the EPS-QEOD Prize for Research into the Science of Light (2017), is an OSA Fellow, holds ERC Advanced Grants, became a Fellow of the Netherlands Academy of Engineering (2023) and joins the KNAW Board in September 2026 (AMOLF; Wikipedia).

## Research footprint

Polman's early work, at the boundary of optical physics and materials science, invented "optical doping": incorporating and optically activating erbium and other ions in thin films by ion implantation, which contributed to planar optical amplifiers. He then became a leading figure in plasmonics and nanophotonics, including influential work with Harry Atwater on plasmonics and photonic design for high-efficiency photovoltaics. His group developed angle-resolved cathodoluminescence spectroscopy, reaching about 10 nm optical resolution; it has been commercially available since 2011. Current work (AMOLF) covers nanopatterned light-trapping layers for solar cells, including the back-reflector in a triple-junction III-V/Si cell with record efficiency (36.1%, cell made by Fraunhofer ISE); perovskite characterisation; metasurfaces for analog optical computing; and time-resolved and quantum cathodoluminescence, electron-beam coherence and free-electron–light coupling (arXiv 2024–2026).

On joining the KNAW Board (2026) he said he looks forward "to help strengthen the ties between the KNAW and politics" (AMOLF news). EPS-QEOD prize citation: "for mastering light at the nanoscale and for demonstrating novel applications in nanoscale optical circuits, photovoltaics, and super-resolution imaging."
