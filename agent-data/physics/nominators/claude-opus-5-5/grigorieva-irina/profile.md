## Biography

Grigorieva was born in Russia. She completed her Ph.D. in condensed matter physics in 1989 at the Institute of Solid State Physics of the Russian Academy of Sciences. She then held postdoctoral positions in the UK (including Bristol), Belgium and the Netherlands, before joining the University of Manchester in 2001. She is the founding director of the EPSRC Centre for Doctoral Training in Science and Applications of Graphene and Related Nanomaterials (Graphene NOWNANO) and of the Centre for Doctoral Training in 2D Materials of Tomorrow (2DMoT). She received the IOP David Tabor Medal and Prize in 2019 and is a Fellow of the Institute of Physics. Manchester says she has been a "highly cited researcher" in physics every year since 2017. She is married to Andre Geim.

## Research footprint

Her early work was on vortex physics in superconductors. At Manchester she co-authored the gecko-foot-mimicking adhesive paper (Nature Materials 2003) and the founding graphene papers: "Electric field effect in atomically thin carbon films" (Science 2004) and "Two-dimensional gas of massless Dirac fermions in graphene" (Nature 2005). She led work on magnetism in graphene, including "Spin-half paramagnetism in graphene induced by point defects" (Nature Physics 2012) and switchable magnetism. She has also worked on graphene-based membranes and atomic-scale capillaries. Papers in that line include water permeation through graphene oxide (Science 2012), precise molecular sieving (Science 2014), tunable ion sieving (Nature Nanotechnology 2017), proton transport through one-atom-thick crystals (Nature 2014), square ice in nanocapillaries (Nature 2015) and molecular transport through capillaries made with atomic precision (Nature 2016). Her profile describes her current focus as "unconventional superconductivity, including materials with nontrivial topology and atomically thin superconductors" and tuning 2D materials "by ion intercalation". Examples include multiphase superconductivity in PdBi2 (2024–2025), lithium intercalation of bilayer graphene (2024) and 1D proximity superconductivity in the quantum Hall regime (2024).
