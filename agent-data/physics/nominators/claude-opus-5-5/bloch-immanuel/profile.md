## Biography

Bloch was born in Fulda in 1972. He studied physics at the University of Bonn, spent a research year at Stanford and received his Ph.D. from LMU Munich in 2000 under Theodor W. Hänsch, with a thesis on atom lasers and the phase coherence of BECs. After a period as a junior group leader in Munich, he was full professor at the University of Mainz from 2003 to 2009. He then returned to Munich as an MPQ director (appointed 2008) and LMU professor. His honours include the Otto Hahn Medal (2002), the DFG Leibniz Prize (2004), the German National Merit Medal (2005), the ICO Prize, the EPS Quantum Electronics Prize (2011), the Körber European Science Prize (2013), the Senior BEC Award, the Harvey Prize (2015), the Bavarian Maximilian Order (2021) and the DPG Stern-Gerlach Medal (2024). He is a member of the Leopoldina and an external member of CIFAR.

## Research footprint

Bloch is best known for the superfluid-to-Mott-insulator quantum phase transition in an optical lattice (Nature 2002, with Markus Greiner and others), which realized a proposal by Jaksch, Zoller and Cirac. Other early results include collapse and revival of the matter-wave field (Nature 2002), a Tonks-Girardeau gas (Nature 2004) and noise-correlation measurements of Hanbury Brown–Twiss bunching and antibunching. He wrote the reviews "Many-body physics with ultracold gases" (RMP 2008, with Dalibard and Zwerger) and "Quantum simulations with ultracold quantum gases" (Nature Physics 2012). His group developed single-atom-resolved imaging of a Mott insulator (Nature 2010). Topological work followed: the Zak phase (2013), the Hofstadter Hamiltonian (PRL 2013) and Chern-number measurement (2015). He also studied many-body localization (Science 2015, 2016) and its RMP Colloquium (2019), Hilbert-space fragmentation, fluctuating hydrodynamics (2023) and ultracold polar molecules, including field-linked resonances and tetratomic molecules. His broader work spans quantum simulation, quantum information and quantum technology infrastructure.
