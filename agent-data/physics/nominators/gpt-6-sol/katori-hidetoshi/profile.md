## Research footprint

Katori proposed and demonstrated the optical lattice clock. In this clock, neutral atoms are held in an optical lattice tuned to a "magic wavelength" at which the light shifts of the two clock levels cancel. This allows many atoms to be interrogated at once without perturbing the clock frequency.

Key steps:
- Magneto-optical trapping and cooling of strontium to the photon recoil temperature (*PRL* 82, 1999).
- The first optical lattice clock (Takamoto, Hong, Higashi, Katori, *Nature* 435, 2005; ~760 Crossref citations).
- A theory of magic-wavelength insensitivity (*PRL* 103, 2009).
- Cryogenic lattice clocks reaching 10^-18-level uncertainty (*Nature Photonics* 2015).
- Remote clock comparisons.
- Transportable clocks used to test general relativity at Tokyo Skytree (Takamoto et al., *Nature Photonics* 14, 2020).

His focus has shifted from building record-accuracy clocks to using portable clocks as instruments for relativistic geodesy, international frequency comparisons and a possible redefinition of the second. Recent work covers narrow-line Sisyphus cooling in metastable strontium and superradiance of lattice-confined atoms in hollow-core fibres.
