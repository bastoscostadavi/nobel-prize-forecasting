## Biography

Harshawardhan Wanare received an M.Sc. in physics from Sri Sathya Sai Institute of Higher Learning (1992) and a Ph.D. in physics from the University of Hyderabad (1998); as a research scholar (1993–98) he also worked at the Physical Research Laboratory, Ahmedabad. He was a project scientist at the Indian Institute of Science (1992–93), then a postdoctoral fellow at Washington State University (1998–99) and Illinois State University (1999–2000). He joined IIT Kanpur as a visiting assistant professor in 2000, became assistant professor (2001), associate professor (2009) and professor (2014), in the Department of Physics and the Centre for Lasers and Photonics (IITK homepage; ORCID). No academy memberships or major honours were found in the sources checked.

## Research footprint

His doctoral and early work, much of it published as "W. Harshawardhan" with G. S. Agarwal, dealt with quantum interference in driven atoms: electromagnetically induced transparency used to control optical bistability (PRA 1996), inhibition and enhancement of two-photon absorption (PRL 1996), and coherent population trapping with phase- or frequency-modulated fields. As a postdoc he worked on excitonic-state trapping in semiconductors (with D. S. Citrin) and on numerical solution of time-dependent Maxwell equations in random media (with R. Grobe and Q. Su). At IIT Kanpur he published a single-author PRL on multicoloured coherent population trapping (2006), then moved toward metamaterials: coherent control of metamaterial resonances with embedded EIT atoms (Optics Express 2008, PRA 2010, with S. Anantha Ramakrishna), and superluminal pulse propagation (PRA 2009). Later work covers double-cavity optical bistability, diffuse photon density waves and imaging through scattering media, photonic crystals (including Jackiw–Rebbi-type domain-wall modes, APL 2024), orbital-angular-momentum modes in fibres and mode sorting (PRA 2023; J. Opt. 2026), and applied optics such as UV-C disinfection chamber design and optical sensing (ORCID works list). His research moved from atomic coherence toward structured light and photonic-materials design.
