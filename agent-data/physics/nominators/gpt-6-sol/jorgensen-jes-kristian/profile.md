## Biography

Jørgensen earned an MSc in astronomy at the University of Copenhagen (2000), with a thesis on the molecular cloud LDN 1622. He earned his PhD at Leiden University (2004) with a thesis on the physical and chemical evolution of low-mass protostars. He was a postdoc at the Harvard-Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics (2004–2007) and a staff scientist at the Argelander Institute in Bonn (2007–2009). He returned to Copenhagen as associate professor in 2010 and became full professor of astrophysics at the NBI in 2021 (KU portal, ORCID). His grants include a Lundbeck Foundation grant, "The Earliest Phases of Circumstellar Disks" (2011, ORCID).

## Research footprint

His research covers the physics and chemistry of gas and dust around young stars. Central questions are when protoplanetary disks form planets and how complex organic molecules are inherited (KU portal). He uses interferometers and space telescopes: the Submillimeter Array, Spitzer, Herschel, ALMA and JWST.

Landmarks:
- **Spitzer c2d.** He contributed to the c2d Legacy survey on star-formation rates and lifetimes (Evans et al. 2009).
- **PROSAC.** He led the PROSAC survey of low-mass protostars with the Submillimeter Array.
- **Glycolaldehyde (2012).** He led the ALMA detection of glycolaldehyde, the simplest sugar-related molecule, in a solar-type protostar.
- **PILS (2016).** He led the ALMA Protostellar Interferometric Line Survey of IRAS 16293-2422 (*A&A* 2016), a chemical inventory of a young solar analogue.

He also joined the JWST "Ice Age" inventory of interstellar ices (2023) and interdisciplinary work linking protostellar timescales to chondrule ages (2017). Recent papers study binarity, infalling streamers and accretion shocks in Class I protostars. His work has moved from envelope structure toward chemistry and the earliest disk and planet formation.
