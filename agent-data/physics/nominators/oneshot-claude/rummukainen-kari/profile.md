## Biography

Rummukainen was born on 14 January 1962 (Wikidata). He studied at the University of Helsinki, with Wikidata dates of 1987 and 1990, the latter probably his doctorate. His PhD advisor was Keijo Kajantie (INSPIRE). His career (INSPIRE and Wikidata):
- staff at Nordita, 1996–2002;
- CERN staff, 2003–2006;
- senior position at the University of Oulu, 2006–2009;
- University of Helsinki, from 2009, now full professor of theoretical particle physics.

He was an Academy Professor of the Research Council of Finland from 2019 to 2023. He received the Väisälä Prize in 2006 and has been a member of the Finnish Academy of Science and Letters since 2009 (Wikidata). He currently leads an ERC Advanced Grant project, "CoCoS" (Helsinki portal).

## Research footprint

Rummukainen is a lattice-simulation specialist working on phase transitions in the early universe. His 1990s lattice studies of the electroweak transition with Kajantie, Mikko Laine and Mikhail Shaposhnikov, using dimensionally reduced effective theories, showed that in the Standard Model the transition is a smooth crossover for Higgs masses above roughly the W mass. That result shaped the study of electroweak baryogenesis.

Later, with Mark Hindmarsh, Stephan Huber and David Weir, he pioneered large-scale simulations of gravitational waves produced by sound waves in first-order phase transitions. This work feeds into LISA science planning.

Other work includes:
- lattice studies of strongly coupled gauge theories beyond the Standard Model;
- the sphaleron rate;
- SU(N) deconfinement interfaces;
- bubble nucleation methods.

He has recently extended his simulation methods to superfluid helium-3 quench dynamics (Helsinki portal).
