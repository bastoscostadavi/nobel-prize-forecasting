## Biography

Born 1969. He received his PhD from Zhejiang University in 1997. He then worked in ZJU's physics department, at Harvard's School of Engineering and Applied Sciences (with Eric Mazur's group, per co-authorship), and in ZJU's Department of Optical Engineering. He is a recipient of the National Science Fund for Distinguished Young Scholars and a Changjiang Scholar Distinguished Professor (2012 cohort). His other honours include a National Defense Science and Technology Award, a Ministry of Education Science and Technology Progress Award, the China Youth Science and Technology Award, a Fok Ying Tung Education Foundation young-teacher award, and the Wang Daheng Optics Award. (Chinese Wikipedia.)

## Research footprint

Tong is best known for showing that silica wires with sub-wavelength diameters can guide light with low loss. The paper, "Subwavelength-diameter silica wires for low-loss optical wave guiding" (Tong, Gattass, Ashcom, He, Lou, Shen, Maxwell, Mazur; *Nature* 426, 2003; ~1,470 Crossref citations), launched the field of optical nanofibers. He followed it with:
- the single-mode guiding theory of such wires (*Opt. Express* 2004; ~650 citations)
- photonic nanowires drawn directly from bulk glasses (2006)
- microfiber interferometers and resonators
- low-threshold single-mode semiconductor nanowire lasers

His group also wrote a widely used review of optical micro/nanofibers (*Nanophotonics* 2013). Recent work covers:
- deep-sub-cycle and sub-half-cycle ultrafast pulses generated in confined optical fibers (*Phys. Rev.* journals 2025–2026)
- polarization-entangled photon pairs from lithium niobate waveguides
- plasmonic nanocavities and atomically thin single-crystal gold
- SPAD LiDAR precision bounds

The trajectory runs from fiber-optic waveguiding toward ultrafast, quantum and plasmonic nanophotonics.
