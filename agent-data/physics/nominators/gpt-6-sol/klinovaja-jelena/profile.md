## Biography

She earned her bachelor's (2007) and master's (2009) degrees in applied mathematics and physics at the Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology. She completed her PhD in theoretical physics at the University of Basel in 2012 under Daniel Loss, summa cum laude. In 2013 she became a Harvard Fellow, a three-year independent fellowship. She returned to Basel in 2014 as tenure-track assistant professor, was promoted to associate professor in 2019, and is now Professor in the Department of Physics. Her honours include the 2013 Swiss Physical Society Prize in Condensed Matter Physics (sponsored by IBM) and an ERC Starting Grant (2017).

## Research footprint

Her group studies "quantum theory of condensed matter systems with a special focus on topological effects and spin phenomena" (Basel profile). Her main themes are:

- **Majorana bound states:** proposals and theory for Rashba nanowires, carbon nanotubes and graphene nanoribbons. This includes the transition from fractional to Majorana fermions in Rashba nanowires (Klinovaja, Stano and Loss, PRL 2012) and topological superconductivity with Majorana fermions in RKKY-coupled magnetic chains (Klinovaja, Stano, Yazdani and Loss, PRL 2013). The latter is relevant to atomic-chain experiments on superconductors.
- **Parafermions and fractional states:** parafermions in interacting nanowire bundles (Klinovaja and Loss, PRL 2014), and coupled-wire constructions of fractional topological phases.
- **Spin physics in nanostructures:** spin–orbit effects, nuclear-spin ordering in nanowires, and more recently higher-order topological insulators and superconductors, and hinge and corner states.

Her work combines analytical model-building with attention to realistic material platforms relevant to topological quantum computing.
