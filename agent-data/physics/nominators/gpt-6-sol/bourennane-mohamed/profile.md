## Biography

Wikipedia gives this account. He was born in Laghouat, Algeria, in 1962 and took a DES (1985) and a Magister (1988) in theoretical physics at USTHB in Algiers. His PhD came from KTH Royal Institute of Technology, Stockholm, in 2001, under Anders Karlsson, with a thesis on long-wavelength quantum cryptography, single-photon detection and entanglement applications. He was a postdoc at LMU Munich and the Max Planck Institute of Quantum Optics in Garching. In February 2005 he founded the Quantum Information and Quantum Optics group at Stockholm University, where he is professor. He was elected to the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences in 2014 and received the Algerian Scholar Award in 2024.

## Research footprint

His early work was on quantum key distribution with multilevel (qudit) encoding: Bourennane, Karlsson and Björk, *Phys. Rev. A* 64, 012306 (2001). He also worked on teleportation with three-particle entanglement (Karlsson and Bourennane, *PRA* 1998). In Munich he was lead author of the first experimental detection of genuine multipartite entanglement using witness operators (*PRL* 92, 087902, 2004, with Weinfurter, Gühne, Lewenstein and others). This is a widely cited paper, with about 430 Crossref citations.

In Stockholm his photonic experiments have addressed:
- quantum contextuality;
- device-independent tests of classical and quantum dimensions;
- measurement-device-independent entanglement witnesses;
- quantum telecloning;
- tests of nonclassicality with low detection efficiency;
- communication-complexity games ("quantum bidding in Bridge");
- network nonlocality in the bilocal scenario (2022).

More recently he has worked on photonic-waveguide realisations of non-Hermitian topological models (2023, with Emil Bergholtz). He also takes part in the Swedish National Quantum Communication Infrastructure consortium (Wikipedia). Across these strands, he consistently uses experiments to test foundational questions that bear on quantum technologies.
