## Biography

Palmroth was born on 10 May 1975 in Sahalahti, near Tampere, Finland (Wikipedia). She earned her MSc (1999) and PhD (2003) in physics at the University of Helsinki. Her thesis combined observations with a global MHD simulation to study solar wind–magnetosphere interaction. From 1996 to 2016 she worked at the Finnish Meteorological Institute (FMI), first as a trainee, then as a doctoral student, postdoc and Academy of Finland Research Fellow (2008–2013). She headed FMI's Earth Observation unit from 2011 to 2016 and was a research professor there from 2013 (ORCID). She became Professor of Space Physics at Helsinki in 2017. She directed the Finnish Centre of Excellence in Sustainable Space Science and Technology (2018–2025). Her honours include the Väisälä Prize (2016), the J. V. Snellman Award (2022) and the Copernicus Medal (2023, for the Vlasiator simulator). She is a member of the Finnish Academy of Science and Letters (2018), the Finnish Academy of Technical Sciences, the Finnish Society of Sciences and Letters (2021) and Academia Europaea (2022).

## Research footprint

Her early work coupled spacecraft and ground observations with global magnetohydrodynamic simulations of energy transfer from the solar wind into Earth's magnetosphere. Her best-known contribution is Vlasiator, a global hybrid-Vlasov code that treats ions kinetically across the whole near-Earth plasma environment, supported by European Research Council funding (Finnish Wikipedia). With this tool her group studies foreshock waves, magnetosheath jets, reconnection and other kinetic phenomena at global scale. The focus has moved from MHD-based coupling studies toward first-principles kinetic modelling for space weather. It has also moved toward applied questions of space sustainability, such as the growing amount of orbital debris (Wikipedia). She continues to publish in 2025–2026, including community papers on open-science heliophysics modelling (research portal).
