## Biography

Publication affiliations trace Heejun Yang's career. In 2010 he published on scanning-tunnelling studies of graphene edges from the Laboratoire de Photophysique Moléculaire, CNRS / Université Paris-Sud, Orsay (Nano Lett. 2010). From 2011 to 2012 he was at the Graphene Research Center of the Samsung Advanced Institute of Technology (SAIT), where he led the graphene "barristor" work (Science 2012). By about 2015 he was in the Department of Energy Science at Sungkyunkwan University (SKKU), where papers up to 2019 list him. From 2025 at the latest he is in the Department of Physics at KAIST. ## Research footprint

Yang is a device and materials physicist working on 2D crystals. His early work used STM to study quantum interference and standing waves at graphene edges (Nano Lett. 2010; PNAS 2011). At SAIT he was first author of the graphene barristor, a triode whose gate tunes a graphene–silicon Schottky barrier (Science 2012). At SKKU he became known for phase engineering of transition-metal dichalcogenides. He showed laser-induced phase patterning of MoTe2 to make ohmic homojunction contacts (Science 2015) and a band gap opening in few-layer monoclinic MoTe2 (Nature Physics 2015). He also wrote a Nature Physics review on structural and quantum-state phase transitions in van der Waals materials (2017, with S. W. Kim, M. Chhowalla and Y. H. Lee). Since about 2018 he has also worked on 2D memristors and neuromorphic devices, such as synaptic devices, in-sensor reservoir computing (Sci. Adv. 2021) and 2D-memristor Ising machines (ACS Nano 2024). Other recent topics are 2D ferroelectricity and polymorphism, spin-lattice effects in VTe2 and CrPS4, charge-density-wave order in NbSe2 (PRL 2026), and AI-driven scanning-probe characterisation (2025).
