## Intellectual work

- The 2021 Scientific Background (Card/Angrist/Imbens) cites Fredriksson, Öckert and Oosterbeek (2013) on class size.
- **Methods:** administrative register data, natural and quasi-experiments, and structural search models where needed. His footprint is very much in the "credibility revolution" tradition applied to Swedish policy.
- **Econometric methods for evaluation:** "Dynamic Treatment Assignment" (with Per Johansson, *JBES* 2008).
- **Immigration and integration:** "Settlement Policies and the Economic Success of Immigrants" (with Åslund and Edin) and work on peer and neighbourhood effects for immigrant students. These exploit Sweden's refugee-placement policy as a quasi-experiment.
- **Unemployment insurance design:** theory-plus-evidence papers with Bertil Holmlund, including "Optimal Unemployment Insurance in Search Equilibrium" (*JOLE* 2001), a *Journal of Economic Surveys* review (2006), and work on monitoring and sanctions (with Boone, Holmlund and van Ours, *EJ* 2007).
- **Education economics:** his most-cited paper is "Long-Term Effects of Class Size" (with Öckert and Oosterbeek, *QJE* 2013; about 350 citations in OpenAlex), which uses Swedish register data. Other work covers school starting age ("Life-cycle Effects of Age at School Start", *EJ* 2013) and "The Rising Return to Non-Cognitive Skill" (with Edin, Nybom and Öckert, *AEJ: Applied Economics*).

- As chair he gave the 2021 presentation speech. He opened with a Swedish natural experiment on COVID-19 school closures and said natural experiments "allow researchers to compare affected and unaffected groups in a way that mimics the comparison of treatment and control groups in clinical trials". ## Career and appointments

Born in 1967 (Swedish Wikipedia). Fredriksson took his PhD in economics at Uppsala University in 1997 and worked in the department until 2006. He became docent in 2001. From 2006 to 2010 he was Director-General of IFAU, the Swedish Institute for Evaluation of Labour Market and Education Policy. He was professor at Uppsala from 2007 to 2010 and at Stockholm University from 2010 to 2017. He then returned to Uppsala as professor (his site says 2017; Wikipedia says 2016). He has edited the *Scandinavian Journal of Economics*, where he is now an associate editor, and has been an associate editor of the *Economic Journal*. He is a member of the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences (since 2015 per Wikipedia), a research fellow of IZA, CESifo and RFBerlin, and a member of the Rockwool Foundation's programme committees.
