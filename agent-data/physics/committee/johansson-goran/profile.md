## Biography

Göran Johansson took his PhD in theoretical physics at Chalmers in 1998. His thesis was "Multiple Andreev Reflection – a Microscopic Theory of ac Josephson Effect in Mesoscopic Junctions", and he had completed a licentiate on the same subject in 1997. He then spent two years as a postdoc in Karlsruhe, Germany (APS Physics author bio). He returned to Chalmers as associate professor in the Applied Physics Laboratory at MC2 and is now full professor of Applied and Theoretical Quantum Physics. On 13 January 2021 he was elected to the Class for Physics of the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences. In 2025 he took over leadership of WACQT, the Knut and Alice Wallenberg-funded national programme that aims to build a 100-qubit superconducting quantum computer by 2030, from Per Delsing. He presented the 2025 physics prize at the announcement and gave the presentation speech to the laureates at the award ceremony (Chalmers news).

## Research / intellectual footprint

- **Early work (1990s–2000s):** Theory of mesoscopic transport through Josephson junctions (multiple Andreev reflection), and theory of readout for superconducting charge qubits. One example is the radio-frequency single-electron transistor as a qubit readout device (PRL 2001).
- **Microwave quantum optics (late 2000s–2010s):** He proposed a dynamical Casimir effect in a superconducting coplanar waveguide (PRL 2009). He was co-author of its experimental observation (Wilson et al., *Nature* 2011), which is his most-cited paper at about 950 citations in OpenAlex. Other work covers scattering of 1D microwave fields on artificial atoms: a single-photon router (PRL 2011), giant cross-Kerr effect (PRL 2013), propagating phonons coupled to an artificial atom (*Science* 2014), and "giant atoms" in waveguide QED (PRL 2018).
- **Quantum computing (2020s):** Gate fidelity and decoherence (PRL 2022, *Quantum* 2025), three-qubit gates, flip-chip processor building blocks, generation of microwave photons (PRL 2025), and quantum algorithms for optimisation and protein folding.
- **Style:** A theorist who works closely with experimental groups. Many of his papers are co-authored with Chalmers experimentalists. OpenAlex lists about 270 works, about 9,500 citations and h-index 45.

## Public statements and values

- At the 2025 announcement he explained that the laureates had taken quantum tunnelling from the microscopic world onto superconducting chips, which allows physicists to study quantum physics and ultimately build quantum computers (Physics World). News reports quote him holding a quantum-computer chip: "This really brings quantum physics from the subatomic world onto this chip."
- On a laureate's influence on him (Chalmers news, Dec 2025): Devoret is "one of the reasons I began working on superconducting quantum computers back in 2000… inspiring to see his deep commitment to every physical detail."
- On quantum technology (KAW): "We won't know how to utilize quantum processors until we achieve full-scale error correction." He stresses the value of "an open academic environment", the importance of qubit coherence, and applications in health and life sciences.

## Connections

- Long-standing Chalmers MC2 colleagues in superconducting quantum circuits include Per Delsing, his predecessor as WACQT director, and C. M. Wilson (co-authors on the dynamical Casimir work). Anton Frisk Kockum is a frequent co-author.
- Through WACQT he is linked to Lund University (quantum sensing) and KTH (quantum communication). The WACQT site notes that Anne L'Huillier's 2023 prize was among the programme's highlights.
- Chalmers hosted 2025 laureates Martinis and Clarke as WACQT guest researchers, and Clarke was a Chalmers Jubilee Professor in 2008 (Chalmers news).
