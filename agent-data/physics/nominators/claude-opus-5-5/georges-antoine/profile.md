## Biography

Antoine Georges was born on 14 April 1961 in Paris. He studied at École Polytechnique (X1980) and then at École Normale Supérieure, completing his PhD in 1988 (Pierre-Gilles de Gennes chaired his committee). He was a postdoc with Philip W. Anderson at Princeton (1989–1991), where he worked on high-Tc superconductivity, before returning to ENS as a CNRS researcher. He became full professor at École Polytechnique in 2003 and chaired its physics department (2006–2009). He was elected to the Collège de France chair in 2009 and has been a part-time professor at Geneva since 2011. Honours: Abragam Prize (1990/91), Dargelos Prize (2004), EPS Europhysics Prize (2006, with Kotliar, Metzner and Vollhardt), CNRS Silver Medal (2007), Aneesur Rahman Prize (2020) and Feenberg Medal (2022). He is a member of the Académie des sciences (2014), Academia Europaea and the NAS (2023).

## Research footprint

Georges is best known for dynamical mean-field theory (DMFT). Beginning in 1990–1991 with Gabriel Kotliar at Rutgers, and building on Metzner and Vollhardt's infinite-dimension limit, he mapped lattice models onto a self-consistent quantum impurity problem. The 1996 Reviews of Modern Physics article "Dynamical mean-field theory of strongly correlated fermion systems and the limit of infinite dimensions" (Georges, Kotliar, Krauth, Rozenberg) became the standard reference. He applied DMFT to the Mott transition (including V₂O₃), transition-metal oxides and cuprates, and with de' Medici and Mravlje developed the concept of "Hund's metals" (Annual Review 2013). Other work covers anomalous diffusion in disordered media (his thesis topic), the Kondo effect, spin glasses and quantum spin liquids (with Parcollet and Sachdev), and cold-atom analogues of solids. At Flatiron he leads computational many-body method development (TRIQS-type ecosystems, embedding and tensor methods; details inferred).
