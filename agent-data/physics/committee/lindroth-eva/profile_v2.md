## Biography

Lindroth trained in the Gothenburg atomic-theory group associated with Ingvar Lindgren. In the 1980s she worked on relativistic atomic-structure calculations using four-component Dirac functions and developed an early relativistic pair-equation program. A 1986 paper on diagonalising the Dirac Hamiltonian as a basis for a relativistic many-body procedure dates from this period; the available group history places her doctorate at about 1987. She later joined Stockholm University, where she is professor of theoretical atomic physics in the Department of Physics. She was elected to the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences in 2015. She has participated publicly in explanations of recent physics prizes, including the 2025 announcement, and has given public seminars about the Nobel Prize and attosecond science.

## Research / intellectual footprint

- Her early work includes relativistic many-body methods, atomic structure, doubly excited helium states, negative ions and photodetachment.
- She co-authored a broad evaluation of X-ray transition energies and worked on calculations for electron–ion storage-ring experiments, including dielectronic recombination and quantum-electrodynamic effects in highly charged ions.
- From roughly 2009 onward, her publications increasingly address atoms in intense ultrashort pulses, photoionisation time delays, resonances used as attosecond timing references, angular dependence and Fano-resonance dynamics.
- Her publication list includes theory papers as well as theory–experiment collaborations with groups at Lund University, ETH Zürich and other institutions.
- Additional listed topics include inner-shell vacancies in heavy elements and quantum dots.
