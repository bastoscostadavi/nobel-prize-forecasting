## Biography

Born in 1978, he attended preparatory classes at Lycée Louis-le-Grand and graduated from the Institut d'Optique Graduate School. He did his PhD (2001–2004) at the Laboratoire Kastler Brossel under Claude Fabre and Thomas Coudreau, working on non-classical light and continuous-variable entanglement. He was then a postdoc in Philippe Grangier's group at the Institut d'Optique and a Marie Curie fellow at Caltech (2005–2007) in H. Jeff Kimble's group. In 2007 he joined Université Pierre-et-Marie-Curie (now Sorbonne Université) as associate professor and became full professor in 2013. His honours include IUF junior (2011) and senior (2022) membership, an ERC Starting Grant (2012) and Advanced Grant (2023), the Jean-Jerphagnon Prize (2024), Optica Fellowship (2025) and the CNRS Silver Medal (2026). In 2022 he co-founded the start-up Welinq.

## Research footprint

His PhD work covered continuous-variable quantum optics, including the conditional preparation of non-classical states (PRL 2003) and optical "Schrödinger kittens" (Science 2006, with the Grangier group). At Caltech he contributed to early quantum-repeater elements: functional quantum nodes for entanglement distribution (Science 2007), heralded entanglement between atomic ensembles (PRL 2007), and mapping photonic entanglement into and out of a quantum memory (Nature 2008). At LKB his team builds cold-atom quantum memories with high efficiency. They reported about 70% efficiency with fidelity above 99% (Nature Communications 2018) and 85% reversible entanglement transfer between light and memories (Optica 2020). The team has also worked on hybrid discrete/continuous-variable entanglement and entanglement swapping across heterogeneous networks, and on waveguide QED with cold atoms trapped near nanofibres. Welinq commercialises quantum-memory and interconnect technology based on this work.
