## Biography

Jochim studied physics at Heidelberg from 1994 to 2000, with a year at UC Berkeley and San Francisco State University, and held a Studienstiftung scholarship. He received his Diplom in 2000. His doctoral work, "Bose-Einstein Condensation of Molecules", was done from 2000 to 2004 at the MPI for Nuclear Physics in Heidelberg and at the University of Innsbruck. He was a postdoc at the IBM Zurich Research Laboratory from 2004 to 2006 and a visiting scientist at the James Franck Institute, University of Chicago, in 2006. From 2006 to 2009 he was a Juniorprofessor in a joint Heidelberg University–MPIK appointment, and he has been Professor of Experimental Physics at Heidelberg since 2009. No prizes or academy memberships were verified in this session.

## Research footprint

As a doctoral researcher in Rudolf Grimm's Innsbruck group, Jochim was lead author of "Bose-Einstein condensation of molecules" (Science 2003). He co-authored work on the BEC-BCS crossover (PRL 2004), the pairing gap in a strongly interacting Fermi gas (Science 2004) and collective excitations in the crossover. At Heidelberg he studied three-component Fermi gases (PRL 2008) and radio-frequency association of Efimov trimers (Science 2010). He then built a few-fermion programme. Key results include deterministic preparation of a tunable few-fermion system (Science 2011), fermionization of two distinguishable fermions (PRL 2012), "From few to many: observing the formation of a Fermi sea one atom at a time" (Science 2013), two fermions in a double well as a Hubbard building block (PRL 2015) and an antiferromagnetic Heisenberg chain of a few atoms (PRL 2015). More recently his group has used 2D mesoscopic systems: emergent elliptic flow of a few fermions (2023, linking to heavy-ion physics), magnified wave-function imaging, a Laughlin state of two rapidly rotating fermions (2024) and single-shot atom- and spin-resolved imaging (2025). He co-authored a 2025 review titled "Few is different: deciphering many-body dynamics in mesoscopic quantum gases".
