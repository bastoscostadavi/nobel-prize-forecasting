## Biography

Michael Köhl was born in 1975. He studied physics at Heidelberg from 1995 as a scholar of the Studienstiftung. He did his diploma thesis in 1998–99 at MIT with Wolfgang Ketterle (degree from Frankfurt, 1999) and received his doctorate in 2001 with Theodor Hänsch at LMU Munich and MPQ Garching. He was a postdoc with Tilman Esslinger at ETH Zurich. In 2007 he became a Reader at the University of Cambridge, where he was promoted to Professor and made a Fellow of Trinity College in 2011. In 2013 he took an Alexander von Humboldt Professorship at the University of Bonn. His honours include the Röntgen Prize of the University of Giessen (2007), an ERC Starting Grant (2009), the IOP Thomson Medal (2012), a Royal Society Wolfson Research Merit Award (2012) and an ERC Consolidator Grant (2014).

## Research footprint

At ETH he co-authored some of the earliest optical-lattice experiments with fermions and in one dimension. These include the 1D superfluid–Mott insulator transition (PRL 2004), Fermi surfaces in a 3D optical lattice (PRL 2005), confinement-induced molecules and "Cavity QED with a Bose–Einstein condensate" (Nature 2007). In Cambridge he built a two-dimensional Fermi gas experiment, with results including "Observation of a pairing pseudogap in a two-dimensional Fermi gas" (Nature 2011) and "Attractive and repulsive Fermi polarons in two dimensions" (Nature 2012). He also demonstrated "A trapped single ion inside a Bose–Einstein condensate" (Nature 2010). His Bonn group now runs several platforms: fermions in optical lattices simulating the Hubbard model, a lithium BEC–BCS crossover experiment, a strontium tweezer experiment, fibre-based microresonators, 2D materials, and an ion–cavity quantum network node. The ion node uses Yb⁺ coupled to fibre cavities, and the group page mentions BBM92 quantum key distribution. Recent work includes the Higgs mode in strongly interacting Fermi gases, quenches across the BEC–BCS crossover and machine-learning analysis of phase diagrams.
