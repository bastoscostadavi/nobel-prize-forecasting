## Biography

Born 20 May 1945 in Ried im Innkreis, Austria. He studied physics and mathematics at the University of Vienna. He received a PhD in 1971 under Helmut Rauch with a neutron-physics thesis, and a habilitation at TU Wien in 1979. He worked at MIT (including as associate professor, 1981–83) and held professorships at TU Wien, TU Munich, Innsbruck and Vienna. He was scientific director of IQOQI Vienna (2004–2013) and President of the Austrian Academy of Sciences (2013–2022). His honours include the King Faisal Prize (2005), the inaugural Isaac Newton Medal of the IOP (2007), the Wolf Prize (2010), the John Stewart Bell Prize (2017), the Micius Prize (2019) and the 2022 Nobel Prize in Physics (with Aspect and Clauser) (Wikipedia).

## Research footprint

Zeilinger started in neutron interferometry with Rauch and Clifford Shull. With Greenberger and Horne he developed the GHZ theorem on multi-particle entanglement (around 1990), and his group gave experimental evidence for it in 1999. His Innsbruck group made one of the first demonstrations of quantum teleportation (1997) and entanglement swapping (1998). Later work included entanglement-based quantum cryptography, one-way quantum computing (2005), a 144 km free-space teleportation link between two Canary Islands, and Bell tests that closed loopholes, including a freedom-of-choice test and one without the fair-sampling assumption. He also showed matter-wave interference with C60 and C70 fullerenes (1999) and entanglement of orbital angular momentum. Over time his focus moved from foundational tests to quantum communication and satellite-scale experiments. He kept a strong interest in the informational interpretation of quantum mechanics.

## Public statements and values

- He called quantum mechanics "probably one of the most beautiful theories ever invented". He added that "with very few symbols" you can explain things "from the smallest quantum particles up to the origin of the universe" (NobelPrize.org telephone interview, 2022, as summarised on the page).
- He described the prize as a "huge appreciation for all the people who I worked with", starting with his teacher Helmut Rauch, whose foundations work "was really a curiosity", and his "150 or even more students" (same interview).
- He has spoken publicly with the Dalai Lama about physics (Nobel Week interview topics, 2022).

## Connections

- Doctoral advisor: Helmut Rauch. MIT collaboration with Clifford Shull.
- Long-standing collaborators: Daniel Greenberger and Michael Horne. Former postdoc Paul Kwiat.
- Doctoral students include Jian-Wei Pan, Thomas Jennewein, Gregor Weihs and Stefanie Barz (Wikipedia).
- Co-laureates: Alain Aspect and John Clauser.
- Institutional networks: University of Vienna, Innsbruck, IQOQI, Austrian Academy of Sciences, IST Austria (vice-chair of its board of trustees since 2006), International Academy Traunkirchen (which he founded).
