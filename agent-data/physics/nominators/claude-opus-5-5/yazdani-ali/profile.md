## Biography

Yazdani was born in 1967 and raised in Tehran, and he emigrated to California in the early 1980s. He earned a BA in physics from UC Berkeley (1989) and a PhD in applied physics from Stanford (1995) (Wikipedia). He was a postdoc at IBM with Don Eigler and then started his own group at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign. He joined Princeton in 2005. He became Class of 1909 Professor and director of the Princeton Center for Complex Materials (an NSF MRSEC) in 2015. In 2024 he stepped down from the center, became co-director of the Princeton Quantum Initiative, and was named James S. McDonnell Distinguished University Professor. His honours include Moore Foundation Investigator status (2008), APS Fellowship (2009), AAAS Fellowship (2012), a Humboldt Research Award (2014), the American Academy of Arts and Sciences (2015), NAS membership (2019), the APS Buckley Prize (2023, shared with Séamus Davis) and the Fritz London Memorial Prize (2025).

## Research footprint

Yazdani develops high-resolution "quantum microscopy" and uses it to image entangled quantum states of matter directly (Wikipedia). His work covers several areas:
- Early STM studies of local magnetic impurities in superconductors, which revealed bound states now called Yu–Shiba–Rusinov states (1997).
- Spectroscopic imaging of the cuprate pseudogap and its relation to superconductivity.
- Visualizing topological surface states and their protection from backscattering.
- Signatures of Majorana zero modes at the ends of ferromagnetic atomic chains on a superconductor (with B. A. Bernevig and others, *Science* 2014).
- Since 2019, STM of magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene and other moiré and flat-band systems, including direct imaging of correlated and Wigner-crystal states.

His focus has moved from single-atom superconductivity physics to topological matter and strongly correlated two-dimensional materials.
