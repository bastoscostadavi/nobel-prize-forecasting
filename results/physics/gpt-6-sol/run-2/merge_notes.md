# Physics nomination consolidation: gpt-6-sol, run 2

Consolidated the 100 nomination JSON files in this run into 61 candidate groups. Every source is assigned exactly once. Decisions below use only the source records. Nominee counts use the people explicitly designated in each source's `nominees` array; historical references in motivation prose are not additional nominations. Within each group, names are ordered by nomination frequency, then first occurrence in lexicographically sorted source files and source nominee-array order.

## Ambiguous merges and splits

- **Atomic quantum computing versus optical-lattice Hubbard simulation (c57, c06).** `bloch-immanuel.json` jointly describes the Cirac–Zoller trapped-ion controlled-NOT blueprint and optical-lattice theory. It is retained as a separate theoretical-foundations candidate because its trapped-ion computing contribution is distinct from the superfluid–Mott transition and Hubbard-model experiments central to `demarco-brian.json`, `kohl-michael.json`, and `lukin-mikhail.json`. The latter three are merged despite differing theoretical and experimental nominee subsets. Bloch's nomination is not duplicated into the lattice group. Rydberg-blockade arrays (c53) remain separate because the nominated blockade, entangling-gate, and tweezer-array achievement differs from lattice Hubbard simulation.
- **Exoplanet compound transit/first atmosphere nomination versus later atmospheric spectroscopy (c38, c36).** `dougherty-michele.json` bundles Charbonneau and Henry's first transit observations of HD 209458b with Seager's transmission prediction and the first sodium-atmosphere detection. Under the explicit compound-candidate policy it stands alone, retaining Charbonneau, Henry and Seager together. `van-dishoeck-ewine.json` separately centers atmospheric detection and characterization, including Snellen's high-resolution spectroscopy, orbital-motion and wind measurements, so its later spectroscopy candidate now has one source and the source's Charbonneau/Seager/Snellen nominee order. `queloz-didier.json` remains a separate transit and Kepler-census candidate (c37) because its population-census contribution is distinct. Each source is assigned once.
- **Black-hole microstate entropy versus AdS/CFT (c44, c24).** `kim-seok.json` combines the Strominger–Vafa microscopic entropy calculation with Maldacena's holographic duality; `van-raamsdonk-mark.json` centers the duality and bulk/boundary dictionary. Kept separate because merging would obscure the microstate-counting achievement and its credited scientists. The shared Maldacena nomination does not alone establish identical achievements.
- **Topological insulators/QSH versus compound QSH+QAH (c21, c51).** `xie-xincheng.json` explicitly bundles the Kane topological-insulator prediction, Molenkamp quantum spin Hall observation, and Xue integer quantum anomalous Hall observation. It stands alone under the explicit compound-candidate policy because the narrower TI/QSH group does not preserve its full bundle. `klinovaja-jelena.json` and `tokura-yoshinori.json` remain merged around topological-insulator establishment and QSH transport; Hasan's three-dimensional surface spectroscopy is included by the latter as establishment of the same topological-insulator phase. Kane and Molenkamp each occur twice, Mele and Hasan once; Mele appears first and occupies the third nominee position, with Hasan in `other_names`. The zero-field fractional Chern-insulator discovery (c23) remains separate because its fractional correlated state is distinct. Magic-angle graphene (c07) also remains separate despite motivation prose mentioning subsequent fractional states.
- **Photonic band gaps and implementations (c04).** `mork-jesper.json`, `segev-mordechai.json`, `tong-limin.json`, and `vuckovic-jelena.json` all center Yablonovitch/John's band-gap concept. Noda's cavities/lasers and Russell's structured fibres are additional implementations in particular nominations, so these sources are merged rather than split on the third nominee. Noda and Russell each occur once; Noda appears in the earlier source file and therefore occupies the third nominee slot.
- **Diamond defect spins, sensing, and networks (c08).** `awschalom-david.json`, `hanson-ronald.json`, and `roch-jean-francois.json` share optical readout/coherent control of NV spins and their sensing/network uses. Merged despite different third nominees and application emphasis. Semiconductor dot/donor spin qubits (c55) and atomic light–matter network interfaces (c29) remain distinct platform achievements.
- **Squeezed-light generation and interferometer deployment (c09).** `walmsley-ian.json` adds Kimble's laboratory generation of squeezing to the Schnabel/Mavalvala interferometer deployment also central to `adhikari-rana.json` and `nussenzveig-paulo.json`. These are merged around squeezed light for improved precision interferometry; Kimble is retained in `other_names`. Cavity optomechanical cooling/control (c27) is scientifically distinct.
- **ATLAS/CMS design and Higgs discovery (c01).** Sources emphasizing detector architecture and Della Negra are merged with those emphasizing Gianotti's discovery-era leadership because all nominate the same experimental Higgs discovery made possible by ATLAS and CMS. Silicon microstrip/hybrid pixel detector invention (c56) remains separate despite its later role in Higgs measurements.
- **Kilonova prediction and r-process identification (c18).** `frebel-anna.json` credits observational work by Berger, while `kankainen-anu.json` credits Piran's merger-site proposal; both center the quantitative kilonova framework and neutron-star-merger r-process identification. Merged with both additional names preserved; Berger precedes Piran on the one-occurrence tie.

## Name normalizations

The top-level `name_normalizations` object lists every differing exact source spelling for the canonical names below; canonical spellings themselves are omitted from alias lists.

- `Mikhail D. Lukin` → `Mikhail Lukin`: identical Harvard affiliation and matching defect-spin/network contributions in the source records. This combines three appearances in c08; the normalized spelling is also used in c29. Existing `Mikhail Lukin` is preserved in c53.
- `J. Ignacio Cirac` → `Juan Ignacio Cirac`: matching Max Planck Institute of Quantum Optics affiliation and Cirac–Zoller contributions in `lukin-mikhail.json` and `bloch-immanuel.json`. Used consistently in the separate c06 and c57 groups; their counts are not combined across groups.
- `Lyman A. Page Jr.` → `Lyman A. Page`: both WMAP records describe the same Princeton instrument lead. Combines the two occurrences in c22, using the spelling in the earlier source file.
- `Maura McLaughlin` (c20) and `Maura A. McLaughlin` (c16) retain their respective source spellings because no within-group variant occurs; the two distinct achievements are not merged.
- `Charles H. Bennett` (quantum cryptography) and `Charles L. Bennett` (WMAP) are kept separate, as the source initials, affiliations, and achievements differ.

## Source integrity

No malformed source records were found. Each source has the required nomination fields and one to three explicitly nominated people. All valid singleton nominations are retained. No scientific ranking or winner assessment was performed.
