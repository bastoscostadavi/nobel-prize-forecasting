My best guess is **John Pendry**, potentially sharing the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics with **David Smith and Ulf Leonhardt**, for work on **metamaterials and transformation optics**.

Their work showed how specially structured materials can manipulate electromagnetic waves in extraordinary ways—including negative refraction, unconventional lenses, and forms of electromagnetic cloaking. It combines fundamental theoretical ideas with experimental demonstrations, making it a plausible Nobel choice.

My strongest alternative would be **Immanuel Bloch, Ignacio Cirac, and Peter Zoller** for **quantum simulation**: using controllable quantum systems to study physics that ordinary computers struggle to calculate.

This is a low-confidence prediction. Nobel deliberations are secret, and several mature fields have credible candidates—but if I had to place one bet, I’d choose **Pendry and metamaterials**.
