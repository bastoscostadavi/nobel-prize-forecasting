## Biography

His LiU page says he did his candidate and doctoral studies in physics and mathematics at the Moscow Steel & Alloys Institute (now NUST MISIS) under Yuri Vekilov. He then worked with Hans Skriver in Denmark and with Börje Johansson at Uppsala University, whom he calls his mentor "in terms of science, as well as leadership". According to ORCID, he has been Professor at LiU's Department of Physics, Chemistry and Biology since 2003. There he heads the Theoretical Physics division and directs the Swedish Government Strategic Research Area in Materials Science on Advanced Functional Materials (AFM). He is a member of the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences and Vice Chair of its Class for physics (KVA directory). The directory does not give his election year.

## Research footprint

His work is ab initio (density-functional and beyond) theory of materials. His aim is to explain properties from quantum mechanics and to feed that understanding into applied materials science and industry. His LiU page lists these highlights:
- A theory of the Invar effect in Fe–Ni alloys (van Schilfgaarde, Abrikosov and Johansson, *Nature* 400, 46, 1999).
- Pressure-induced phase separation in Fe–Si (*Nature*, 2003).
- Stabilisation of bcc Fe–Ni at high pressure (*Science*, 2007).
- Theory of oxygen storage in ceria, with co-doping for fuel cells (*PRL* 2002, *PNAS* 2006).
- Multicomponent alloying concepts for hard coatings.
- An electronic topological transition in hcp Fe (*PRL* 2013).
- A novel electronic transition in osmium compressed to 770 GPa (*Nature*, 2015).

Recent work pairs theory with terapascal-range synthesis: rhenium nitrides made at about 900 GPa with Bayreuth, and the aromatic [N6]4- anion in K9N56. It also covers the mechanics of hard nitride ceramics and correlated MAX phases (arXiv listings). He increasingly frames the goal as "knowledge-based design" of materials, speeded up by supercomputing and machine learning.

## Public statements and values

- On materials and sustainability: "materials can be the solution to virtually any one of those goals" (LiU news, "Materials science at the service of humankind").
- On research policy: "It's very important to understand that we must preserve all layers of research, fundamental, strategic and applied." (same source)
- On design: he hopes to move from trial and error to knowledge-based design, cutting development time "From twenty years to maybe four or five." (same source)
- On group culture: "in a climate of collaboration we are able to achieve greater results than we would on our own". He also stresses fair co-authorship: "only those who actually contribute get their names on the paper" (LiU employee page).
