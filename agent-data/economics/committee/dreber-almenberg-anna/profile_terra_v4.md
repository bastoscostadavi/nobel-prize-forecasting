## Public statements and attribution

- **On JPE Microeconomics.** Her homepage calls it a journal "that cares more about credible results than clear results."
- **On the 2025 prize (SSE news, as a committee member).** She said "We can't take growth for granted." She stressed openness to new ideas, that "sustained growth and sustainable growth are not necessarily the same," that "AI has the potential to increase the rate at which useful knowledge is generated," and support for those who lose out from creative destruction.
- **On unreliable research (KAW).** She said "Researchers enjoy breaking new ground, and in attempting to do so, may fool themselves," and "it's easy to fall in love with your hypothesis." She endorses preregistration and registered reports, "evaluating the issues addressed by the research and its design instead of its findings," and larger team studies that "can say something much more reliable". She also said "our findings suggest there is nothing wrong with the scientific method" and that the high share of false positives "can be remedied."

## Research and methods

- **Prediction markets for science.** "Using prediction markets to estimate the reproducibility of scientific research" (PNAS 2015) is her signature method. It tests whether researchers can forecast which results replicate.
- **Earlier behavioral work.** She studied the evolution of cooperation with Rand, Fudenberg and Nowak ("Winners don't punish," Nature 2008; Science 2009). She has also worked on gender differences in competitiveness and deception, testosterone and financial risk-taking, and hormonal contraceptives and risk (Management Science).
- **Current work.** She is involved in Lab2 (a many-labs and multi-analyst hub for economics) and in the Institute for Replication, plus large hormone-administration trials (PNAS 2025).
- **Method:** lab and online experiments, large-team preregistered replications, prediction markets and surveys, and multi-analyst designs.
- **Replication and metascience.** She was on the large replication teams behind Camerer et al. (Science 2016, lab experiments in economics) and Camerer et al. (Nature Human Behaviour 2018, Nature/Science social-science experiments). She co-authored "Redefine statistical significance" (NHB 2017), her most-cited paper (OpenAlex). She also co-authored the "many analysts" neuroimaging study (Nature 2020) and an Annual Review of Psychology survey (2021).

## Biographical record

Dreber Almenberg did her PhD at the Stockholm School of Economics (PhD student 2004–09, degree 2009). From 2006 to 2009 she was a research scientist at Harvard's Program for Evolutionary Dynamics. She was then a fellow and research associate at the Harvard Kennedy School (2009–17) and a postdoc at SIFR (2009–11). At SSE she was assistant professor (2011–14), associate professor (2014–16), and from 2016 the Johan Björkman Professor. She was SSE's first female professor of economics. She was an affiliated professor at Innsbruck from 2018 to 2024. She became a Wallenberg Academy Fellow (2013) and later a Wallenberg Scholar. She joined the Young Academy of Sweden (2016), won the Assar Lindbeck Medal (2019), and was elected to KVA (2021) and IVA. Editorially, she was an Associate Editor at the JPE (2018–2026) and an Editor of JPE Microeconomics (2022–April 2026). She sits on the advisory board of the Journal of Comments and Replications in Economics.
