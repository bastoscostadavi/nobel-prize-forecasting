## Biography

Per INSPIRE, Forssén did his PhD at Chalmers (1998–2003), with Mikhail Zhukov listed as advisor. His early work was on few-body structure of halo and drip-line nuclei. He was a postdoc at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (2003–2006). He returned to Chalmers as a junior faculty member (2006–2008) and has held a senior position there since 2008. He became professor in 2018 (ORCID) and now heads the Division of Subatomic, High Energy and Plasma Physics. He was a visiting scientist at Oak Ridge National Laboratory and the University of Tennessee (2014–2015). No academy memberships or major named prizes were found in the sources consulted.

## Research footprint

- **Early work.** Three-body and halo-nucleus structure (Li-11, Be-14) in collaboration with R3B/GSI experiments. Large-basis ab initio no-core shell model studies of Be-9 and Be-11 with Petr Navrátil and collaborators (Phys. Rev. C 2005).
- **Nuclear forces from chiral EFT.** At Chalmers, with Andreas Ekström, he helped build optimised chiral interactions: NNLO_opt (PRL 2013) and NNLO_sat (PRC 2015), the latter giving accurate nuclear radii and binding energies.
- **Uncertainty quantification.** Order-by-order optimisation of chiral interactions with uncertainty analysis (PRX 2016).
- **Applications with ORNL/Tennessee coupled-cluster groups.** The neutron and weak-charge distribution of Ca-48 (Nature Physics 2016), the Pb-208 neutron skin (Nature Physics 2024), charge radii of potassium isotopes (Nature Physics 2021), the first observation of O-28 (Nature 2023) and "Multiscale Physics of Atomic Nuclei from First Principles" (PRX 2025).
- **Bayesian methods.** Recent work applies Bayesian inference and fast emulators to constrain low-energy constants and three-nucleon forces (e.g. with Furnstahl, Phillips and others, PRC 2021). It also covers renormalisation-group-invariant perturbative power counting (with Oliver Thim), spin-isospin symmetries in beta decay (PRL 2026) and entanglement in the nucleon-nucleon system.

The trajectory runs from few-body halo physics, to interaction design, to statistically rigorous ab initio predictions.
