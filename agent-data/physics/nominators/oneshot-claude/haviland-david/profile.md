## Biography

Born 22 July 1961 in Bar Harbor, Maine; grew up in Ames, Iowa. BSc in physics from Union College, New York (1979–83); Fulbright scholar at the University of Göttingen (1983–84); PhD from the University of Minnesota (1989) under Allen M. Goldman. Researcher at Chalmers University of Technology (1989–1997), then professor at KTH from 1997. Wallmark Prize of the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences (2008) "for his discoveries concerning the development of mesoscopic physics". Elected to the Academy in 2011. Nobel Committee for Physics member (listed 2018, 2020–2022, 2024) and chair in 2020.

## Research footprint

- **Early career:** the superconductor–insulator transition in ultrathin films. His PhD work produced "Onset of superconductivity in the two-dimensional limit" (Haviland, Liu, Goldman, PRL 1989).
- **Chalmers and early KTH:** single-charge and Coulomb-blockade physics in small Josephson junctions and SQUID arrays, including the observation of Bloch oscillations in an ultrasmall Josephson junction (Kuzmin & Haviland, PRL 1991).
- **From the late 2000s:** measuring and analysing nonlinear systems through intermodulation (frequency mixing). The patented intermodulation AFM uses multi-tone drives near cantilever resonance to reconstruct tip–surface forces (Platz, Tholén, Forchheimer, Haviland et al.). The same multifrequency lock-in approach led to measurement hardware now sold by Intermodulation Products.
- **Current work (2023–2025):** returns to superconducting microwave circuits, including multipartite entanglement and continuous-variable cluster states in microwave frequency combs (PRL 2023, 2025), nonreciprocal scattering, two-level-system losses in resonators, kinetic-inductance nanowire resonators, and microwave optomechanical AFM transducers.

Across these phases the constant is precision measurement physics built on superconducting and nanomechanical devices.
