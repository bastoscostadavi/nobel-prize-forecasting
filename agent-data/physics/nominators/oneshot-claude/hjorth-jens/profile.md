## Biography

Hjorth was born on 22 December 1964 (Wikidata). Wikidata records studies at Aarhus University ending in 1993. He is Professor of Astrophysics at the Niels Bohr Institute, affiliated with DARK (formerly the Dark Cosmology Centre), and holds a Villum Investigator grant on "Time in Astrophysics" (KU portal). Other roles and honours:
- co-lead of UCPH's Forward career-development programme;
- board member of the Carlsberg Foundation and the Tuborg Foundation;
- EliteForsk Prize (2008) and Villum Kann Rasmussen Annual Award (2017) (Wikidata);
- member of the Royal Danish Academy of Sciences and Letters since 2006 and of the IAU (Wikidata).

## Research footprint

His portal lists about 400 refereed papers and more than 35,000 citations, including about 33 in *Nature* or *Science*. His most-cited lead-author paper is Hjorth et al., *Nature* 423, 847 (2003), about 1,300 citations. It reported a very energetic supernova associated with GRB 030329 and helped establish the link between gamma-ray bursts and supernovae.

His current research themes (portal) are:
- time-domain astrophysical transients: supernovae and gravitational-wave counterparts;
- very high-redshift galaxies;
- cosmology;
- "the origin of universality in dark-matter halos", developed through statistical-mechanics approaches to collisionless systems.

He has also written on type Ia supernova cosmology and dust. Recent co-authored papers cover Type Ibn supernovae, reddening in type Ia sight lines, and quiescent galaxies at z > 5. His portal also lists "Art & Science" as a research interest.
