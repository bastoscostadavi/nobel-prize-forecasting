## Biography

Born February 1975. He received a B.S. in physics from USTC (1998). He earned a Ph.D. from Princeton University (2004) under N. Phuan Ong, with a thesis on "The Nernst Effect in High Temperature Superconductors". He was a Miller Research Fellow at UC Berkeley (2004–2007) and a visiting scholar at MIT (2007). He joined Tsinghua as a professor in December 2007. His honours include the William L. McMillan Award (2006), a Changjiang Professorship, the NSFC Outstanding Young Researcher award (2009), the Sir Martin Wood China Prize for Low Temperature Physics (2013), the Chinese Young Scientist Award (2013) and the Chinese Physical Society's Huang Kun Prize (CV; Chinese Wikipedia). He is a member of the China Democratic League.

## Research footprint

His doctoral work with Ong used the Nernst effect to reveal vortex-like excitations far above Tc in cuprates (Wang, Li & Ong, PRB 2006). This became an influential line of evidence on the pseudogap and phase fluctuations. At Tsinghua he built low-temperature, ultrahigh-vacuum STM/STS and high-field transport laboratories for strongly correlated materials. He studied the doping evolution of cuprate Mott insulators and in-gap states in the Kondo insulator SmB6 (PRL 2014). He was one of the project leaders, with Xue Qikun and Ke He, of the first observation of the quantum anomalous Hall effect (Chang et al., Science 2013), where his group did the transport. In later work on the intrinsic magnetic topological insulator MnBi2Te4 he reported axion-insulator and Chern-insulator phases (Liu et al., Nature Materials 2020) and studied edge states and quantisation in thin films.
