## Biography

Spiropulu, who is Greek, earned a BSc in physics at the Aristotle University of Thessaloniki (1993). She then took an MA (1995) and a PhD (2000) at Harvard on the CDF experiment at Fermilab. Her thesis introduced a blind-analysis search for supersymmetry at a hadron collider (Wikipedia). She was an Enrico Fermi Fellow at the University of Chicago (2001–03) and then moved to CERN, where she was a research scientist on CMS from 2004 and a senior research physicist until 2012. She joined Caltech as associate professor in 2008, became professor in 2011 and Ch'en Professor in 2017 (Caltech page). She is an APS Fellow (2014) and an AAAS Fellow (2009) and received the Weston Havens Foundation Award (2013) and the RSAC Frontier Award (2026). She co-chaired the National Academies' EPP-2024 elementary particle physics study, chaired the Fermilab Physics Advisory Committee and served on HEPAP (Wikipedia; Caltech page).

## Research footprint

Her early work was on searches for supersymmetry and extra dimensions using missing transverse energy at CDF. At CMS she co-convened the supersymmetry and beyond-Standard-Model search group (2005–08). With Chris Rogan, Maurizio Pierini and Joseph Lykken she invented the "razor" kinematic variables for new-physics searches. Her Caltech group played a role in the 2012 Higgs discovery and leads design and construction of the MIP Timing Detector (Barrel Timing Layer) for the High-Luminosity LHC. Since about 2013–14 she has driven programmes in AI/ML for particle physics and in quantum information science for high-energy physics. She founded AQT/INQNET with AT&T, Fermilab and JPL, which demonstrated time-bin qubit teleportation and entanglement-swapping testbeds. The QCCFP project produced the "traversable wormhole dynamics" experiment on Google's Sycamore processor (Jafferis et al., *Nature* 2022). She co-founded Bohr Quantum Technology.
