My prediction is **Michael Berry and Yakir Aharonov**, for their work on geometric phases in quantum physics.

The **Aharonov–Bohm effect** shows that magnetic flux can affect electrons’ interference even when they travel through regions with no magnetic field. The **Berry phase** describes a phase acquired as a quantum system’s parameters undergo a cycle. Both became foundational ideas, with experimentally verified consequences across many areas of physics.

They fit a familiar Nobel pattern: discoveries that are decades old, have clear attribution, and have proved broadly influential.

My strongest alternative would be **Ignacio Cirac and Peter Zoller**, for pioneering quantum computing and simulation with trapped ions.
