## Biography

Vučković was born in Niš, Serbia, and studied at the University of Niš (Wikipedia). She received an MS (1997) and a PhD (2002) in electrical engineering from Caltech; her thesis on photonic crystal structures was supervised by Axel Scherer (Wikipedia). After a short postdoc in Stanford Applied Physics, she joined the Stanford EE faculty in 2003. She chaired the EE department from 2021 to 2023 and was the inaugural director of Q-FARM, the Stanford–SLAC quantum initiative, from 2019 to 2021. She was elected to the National Academy of Sciences in 2023. She became an External Scientific Member of the Max Planck Institute of Quantum Optics in 2024. Her awards include PECASE (2007 per Stanford), a Humboldt Research Award (2010), a Vannevar Bush Faculty Fellowship (2022), the Zeiss Research Award (2025) and Optica's R. W. Wood Prize (2026). She is a Fellow of APS, Optica and IEEE and the lead editor of *Physical Review Applied* (Stanford Profiles).

## Research footprint

Her lab studies light-matter interaction at the nanoscale, especially solid-state quantum emitters such as quantum dots and colour centres in diamond and silicon carbide, coupled to photonic-crystal cavities. A well-known early result is the control of cavity reflectivity with a single quantum dot (Englund et al., *Nature* 2007), followed by controlled phase shifts with a single dot (Fushman et al., *Science* 2008). From around 2015 she pioneered photonic **inverse design**: computer-optimised nanophotonic devices such as a compact on-chip wavelength demultiplexer (Piggott et al., *Nature Photonics* 2015). This led to the Spins software and the Spins Photonics company. Current projects include tin-vacancy centres in thin-film diamond, heterogeneously integrated photonics, on-chip Ti:sapphire lasers, and cryogenic electro-optic nonlinearities in strontium titanate (lab page). The focus has moved from fundamental cavity QED toward scalable, manufacturable quantum and classical photonic systems.
