## Biography

Sudbø was born on 3 May 1961 (Wikipedia). He received his PhD from Brown University in 1990 (NTNU CV) and is Professor of Physics at NTNU. In 2005 the Research Council of Norway gave him the Møbius Prize for his research on superconductivity (Wikipedia). With Kristian Fossheim he co-wrote the graduate textbook *Superconductivity: Physics and Applications* (Wiley, 2004). He was one of the four Primary Investigators who founded QuSpin, the Norwegian Centre of Excellence in quantum spintronics (2017–2027), and he is now its Center Director (QuSpin staff page). In 2006 an official Norwegian inquiry examined his co-authorship of two retracted medical papers written by his twin brother. It found the allegations against him unfounded and held his brother solely responsible (Wikipedia).

## Research footprint

His NTNU page describes the group's focus as condensed matter "in the quantum regime". The topics are critical phenomena, spin and charge transport, heterostructures of unconventional materials, and systems with several coexisting broken symmetries. The group studies novel superconductors (graphene, pnictides, topological superconductors), high-Tc cuprates, multicomponent Bose–Einstein condensates and superfluids, topological excitations, topological magnons, and unconventional magnetism and superconductivity. It also runs large-scale Monte Carlo simulations. His best-known earlier work covers vortex-matter phase transitions and gauge-field criticality in superconductors, plus multicomponent condensates. One example is a *Nature* paper with Egor Babaev and N. W. Ashcroft on possible superconducting–superfluid transitions in metallic hydrogen (2004). His 2026 papers treat altermagnets, fluctuation conductivity in multicomponent superconductors, RKKY-type interactions in ferromagnet/topological-insulator heterostructures, and real-time critical slowing down in ultrafast quenches of superconductivity (NTNU publication list). Over time his research has moved from statistical mechanics of superconductors toward spintronics-related heterostructures.
