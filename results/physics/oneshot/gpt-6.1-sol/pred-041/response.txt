My pick is **Pablo Jarillo-Herrero**, potentially sharing the prize with **Allan MacDonald and Rafi Bistritzer**, for work on **magic-angle graphene**.

Their key insight: stack two atomically thin graphene sheets and rotate them by roughly 1.1 degrees, and electrons behave dramatically differently—including forming superconducting states. MacDonald and Bistritzer supplied the foundational theory; Jarillo-Herrero’s group demonstrated the effect experimentally in 2018.

It’s a plausible Nobel choice because it opened a major field: controlling materials’ electronic properties through the angle between their layers.

**This is a low-confidence prediction**, not inside information. Nobel deliberations are secret, and I can’t verify the latest 2026 developments here. But if I had to name one person, I’d choose Jarillo-Herrero.
