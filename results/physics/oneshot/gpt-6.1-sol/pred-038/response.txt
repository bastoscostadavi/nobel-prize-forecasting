My best guess is **Charles L. Kane, Eugene J. Mele, and Laurens W. Molenkamp**, for their work on **topological insulators**.

These materials insulate in their interior but conduct along their boundaries, with conducting states protected by their quantum properties. Kane and Mele developed foundational theory; Molenkamp led landmark experiments demonstrating the predicted behavior.

They’re strong candidates because the discovery established a major area of physics, its significance has held up over roughly two decades, and it offers a compelling combination of theoretical prediction and experimental confirmation.

My runner-up would be **Michael Berry and Yakir Aharonov**, for discoveries concerning quantum phases that profoundly changed how physicists understand quantum mechanics.

That’s a speculative forecast with low confidence. Nobel nominations are confidential, and I can’t verify the latest 2026 reporting here. But if you want one concrete prediction: **Kane–Mele–Molenkamp**.
