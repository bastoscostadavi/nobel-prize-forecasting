## Biography

Naoto Nagaosa was born on 21 February 1958. He graduated in applied physics from the University of Tokyo (1980), was a research associate at the Institute for Solid State Physics from 1983, received his D.Sci. in 1986, and was a visiting scientist at MIT (1988–1990). He rose through the University of Tokyo's Department of Applied Physics to full professor (1998), retiring as professor emeritus in 2023. He led the correlated-electron theory team at AIST (2001–2008) and RIKEN teams from 2007, and since 2013 has been deputy director of RIKEN CEMS and director of its Strong Correlation Theory Research Group. Honours include the Nishina Memorial Prize (2005), Medal with Purple Ribbon (2018), Toray Science and Technology Prize (2024), Asahi Prize (2025) and Japan Academy Prize (2026). He is listed in the US National Academy of Sciences directory (ja-Wikipedia; RIKEN; NAS).

## Research footprint

Nagaosa is a field-theoretic condensed matter theorist. Early work concerned gauge theories of strongly correlated electrons and high-temperature superconductivity, summarised in the review "Doping a Mott insulator" with Patrick Lee and Xiao-Gang Wen (2006). He then became central to the Berry-phase picture of transport: a 2003 study with Zhong Fang, Yoshinori Tokura and others linked the anomalous Hall effect to momentum-space monopoles, and he co-authored the 2010 Reviews of Modern Physics article on the anomalous Hall effect. With Tokura he developed the theory of magnetic skyrmions and emergent electromagnetism, and more recently he has worked on shift currents, nonreciprocal and nonlinear responses, and quantum geometry. His 2026 arXiv output covers skyrmion and antiskyrmion dynamics, the spin Hall effect, polarons, Weyl-semimetal nonreciprocity and quantum-geometric superconducting transport. The RIKEN group describes its method as combining first-principles calculation, quantum field theory and numerics (RIKEN).
